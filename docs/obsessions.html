合成器與古琴之間：袁中平的南風旅程

來源：FOND/SOUND

一九八三年，袁中平離開台北，到紐約學音樂。那時他還是個彈吉他的年輕人，晚上在餐廳裡表演。

一九八九年，他做出專輯《南風旅程》。裡面有電子合成器（一種可以做出各種聲音的機器）的聲響，也有吉他、中國笛子和手鼓。這張專輯拿下那一年的金鼎獎。

多年以後，他成為台灣有名的古琴大師。這張唱片留住了他還在找路的樣子。

In 1983, Yuan Zhongping left Taipei for New York to study music. He was a young guitarist at the time, performing evenings in restaurants. In 1989, he released the album "Southern Wind Journey." It featured the sounds of electronic synthesizers—machines capable of producing a wide range of sounds—alongside guitar, Chinese bamboo flute, and hand drums. The album won the Golden Tripod Award that year. Years later, he would become a renowned guqin master in Taiwan. This record captures him still finding his way.

樹皮做的麵包：芬蘭大飢荒留下的三塊黑麵包

來源：Helsinki University Museum

赫爾辛基大學博物館裡，放著三塊又小又黑的麵包。它們來自一八六八年的大飢荒（很多人沒有東西吃的年代）。

那一年，芬蘭三分之一的收成壞掉。人們把松樹裡面的樹皮磨成粉，混進少少的麥子，做成「樹皮麵包」。它帶一點松香的甜味，在當時算是最好的代替品。可是大雪封住山林的時候，連樹皮也拿不到。

這三塊麵包由一位醫生留下，他的女兒在一九二七年把它們送給博物館。

In the Helsinki University Museum sit three small, dark loaves of bread. They come from the Great Famine of 1868—a time when many people had nothing to eat. That year, Finland lost one-third of its harvest. People ground the inner bark of pine trees into flour, mixed it with small amounts of wheat, and baked "bark bread." It carried a subtle, pine-scented sweetness, which made it the best available substitute at the time. When heavy snows sealed off the forests, however, even bark became impossible to obtain. These three loaves were preserved by a doctor; his daughter donated them to the museum in 1927.

開鎖之夜：芝加哥人把鎖當成謎題

來源：TOOOL Chicago / Pumping Station: One

每個月的第一個星期三晚上八點，一群人在芝加哥的 Pumping Station: One 聚會。那是一個動手做東西的工作室，就在 Elston 大道上。

他們是國際開鎖組織的芝加哥分會，一起研究怎麼打開一把鎖。聚會免費，任何人都可以走進去。老手會把練習用的鎖和工具交給新手，從頭教起。

下一場就在十月初。你不用會修鎖，只要好奇：一把鎖，是怎麼決定誰可以打開它的？

Every first Wednesday of the month at 8 p.m., a group gathers at Pumping Station: One, a makerspace on Elston Avenue in Chicago. They are members of the Chicago chapter of the International Locksport Organization, meeting to study and practice the craft of opening locks. Entry is free and anyone is welcome. Experienced members hand beginners practice locks and tools, teaching them from scratch. The next meetup is in early October. You don't need to be a locksmith—just curious about one essential question: How does a lock decide who can open it?

亂中有序：一個畫出奇異吸子的小網站

來源：strange-attractors.org

天氣為什麼很難預測？數學家羅倫茲發現，有些系統只要一點點不同，後來的結果就差很多。可是把它們的變化畫成圖，卻美得像一條絲帶。這種圖形叫「奇異吸子」。

strange-attractors.org 是一個人做的小網站。你可以在上面轉動幾個數字，看著一個圖形慢慢變成另一個。喜歡的就存在自己的電腦裡，不用註冊帳號。

沒有廣告，也沒有會員，只有一個人想把美的東西分享出來。

Why is weather so hard to predict? Mathematician Lorenz discovered that some systems, even with the smallest difference, can lead to vastly different outcomes later. Yet when you plot these changes as a graph, they're stunningly beautiful—like ribbons flowing across the page. This type of pattern is called a "strange attractor." strange-attractors.org is a small website built by one person. You can adjust a few numbers and watch one shape gradually transform into another. Save your favorites to your computer—no account registration needed. No ads, no memberships, just one person sharing something beautiful with the world.
