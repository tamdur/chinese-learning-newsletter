從教堂鋼琴到好萊塢：小蟲的《活在愛裡》

來源：FOND/SOUND

小蟲小時候住在高雄，常常偷偷溜進教堂，借神父的鋼琴練習。一九九五年，他飛到美國好萊塢，在那裡錄下了這張專輯（音樂唱片）。

《活在愛裡》有十二首歌，混合了美國的靈魂樂。它的節奏溫暖，情感卻很台灣，是那個年代少見又動人的作品。

Johnny Bug Chen (小蟲) spent his childhood in Kaohsiung, often sneaking into a church to practice on the priest's piano. In 1995, he flew to Hollywood, where he recorded this album. "Because Of Love" features twelve songs that blend in American soul music. Its rhythm is warm, yet its emotion distinctly Taiwanese — a rare and moving work for that era.

在結冰的海上溜冰：芬蘭的島嶼冬天

來源：Visit Turku

芬蘭的圖爾庫群島（很多島聚在一起）大約有五萬個小島，是世界上島最多的地方。冬天海水結冰後，人們就能穿上冰鞋，從一座島滑到另一座島。

這種玩法不在溜冰場，一天可能滑二三十公里。滑冰季節大約從一月到三月，出發前嚮導（帶路的人）會先檢查冰夠不夠厚。

Finland's Turku Archipelago, with roughly fifty thousand small islands clustered together, is the place with the most islands in the world. When the seawater freezes in winter, people can put on ice skates and glide from one island to another. This isn't skating in an indoor rink — in a single day you might cover twenty or thirty kilometers. The skating season runs from around January to March, and before setting out, a guide checks that the ice is thick enough.

把畫編起來：芝加哥的紙編織工作坊

來源：The Weaving Workshop

七月二十八日，芝加哥的編織藝術家迪伊．克萊門茲會在她的學校開一堂三小時的課。大家先用顏料在紙上畫出顏色，再把紙剪成一條條。

接著把紙條編在一起，就變成一幅編出來的畫。課堂上會教兩種基本的編法。就算你從沒編過，也能輕鬆參加。

On July 28th, Chicago weaving artist Dee Clements will hold a three-hour class at her school. Participants first paint colors onto paper, then cut the paper into strips. Next, they weave the strips together to create a woven picture. The class teaches two basic weaving techniques. Even if you've never woven before, you can join with ease.

一張會閃的燈塔地圖：五萬座海上的光

來源：lighthouses.app

有一個人生病在家休息時，上網查起美國的燈塔，發現海岸巡防隊每年都會公布所有的航海燈，卻沒人把它們畫成地圖。

於是他做了這個網站，在地圖上標出五萬多座燈。每座燈都照著真正的節奏在閃。船隻就是靠這些不同的閃法，認出自己在哪裡。

While recovering from an illness at home, one person started looking up American lighthouses online. He discovered that the Coast Guard publishes all of its navigational lights every year, yet no one had ever mapped them. So he built this website, plotting over fifty thousand lights on a map. Each light flashes according to its real rhythm. Ships rely on these distinctive flash patterns to recognize where they are.
