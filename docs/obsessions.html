不准跳舞的年代，青蛙王子唱迪斯可

來源：South China Morning Post (Post Magazine)

從一九五七年起，台灣不准大家在外面隨便跳舞，只有美軍基地和國際大飯店可以。

一九八〇年，迪斯可（一種跳舞音樂）紅遍全世界。歌手高凌風，外號「青蛙王子」，出了一張國語迪斯可唱片。他把美國流行歌改成乾淨的中文歌詞，通過政府的檢查，可是跳舞的節奏一點也沒少。

二〇二〇年，一張新合輯找回當年的女歌手。她們的唱片幾乎沒有留下介紹，今天成了挖唱片的人最想找的寶物。

From 1957 on, Taiwan did not allow people to dance freely in public. Only US military bases and international hotels were permitted to host dancing. In 1980, disco swept the world. The singer Kao Ling-feng, nicknamed the "Frog Prince," released a Mandarin disco record. He reworked American pop songs with clean Chinese lyrics that passed government censorship, but the dance beat was left untouched. In 2020, a new compilation brought back the female singers of that era. Their records left almost no liner notes or background information behind, and today they are the treasures that record diggers most want to find.

走五公里，才看得到的森林教堂

來源：Metsähallitus / luontoon.fi

在芬蘭北方的拉普蘭，伊納里有一座小木頭教堂，叫作皮爾帕湖荒野教堂。它建於一七六〇年，沒有路可以開車到，只能走五公里的小路，經過兩個湖和白樺樹林。

一八八八年以後，這裡就很少使用。今天，它每年夏至（一年白天最長的那天）還會辦一場禮拜，也是很多新人最想結婚的地方。

教堂沒有上鎖，也不收門票。管理單位只請遊客安靜走進去，尊重旁邊的老墓地。比起玻璃屋旅館，這裡更像真正的拉普蘭。

In Lapland, in northern Finland, near the village of Inari, stands a small wooden church called Pielpajärvi Wilderness Church. Built in 1760, it cannot be reached by car. Visitors must walk five kilometers along a forest path that passes two lakes and a stand of silver birch trees. After 1888, the church was used only rarely. Today it still holds a service every year on midsummer, the day with the most daylight of the year, and it is a favorite wedding spot for many couples. The church is never locked, and there is no entry fee. The managing authority asks only that visitors step inside quietly and respect the old graveyard beside it. Compared with the glass igloo hotels, this place feels much more like the real Lapland.

親手做一本集印章的小書

來源：Japanese Culture Center (Chicago) / Eventbrite

在日本，很多人去寺廟和神社時，會帶一本「御朱印帳」（收集印章的摺頁小書），請廟方蓋上紅色的印章和毛筆字。

十一月十九日星期四晚上五點半，芝加哥林肯大道上的日本文化中心有一堂兩小時的課。藝術家 Mami Takahashi 會教大家兩種日本傳統縫書法：四個洞的「四目綴」，和像烏龜殼的「龜甲綴」。

不需要經驗，材料都準備好了。每個人可以帶回三本自己做的小書。

In Japan, many people visiting temples and shrines bring a goshuin-cho (a folding booklet for collecting seals) and ask the staff to stamp it with red seals and brush-written calligraphy.

On Thursday, November 19, at 5:30 p.m., the Japanese Cultural Center on Lincoln Avenue in Chicago is hosting a two-hour class. Artist Mami Takahashi will teach two traditional Japanese bookbinding stitches: yotsume-toji, a four-hole stitch, and kikkou-toji, a stitch whose pattern looks like a turtle's shell. No experience is needed, and all materials are provided. Each participant can take home three handmade booklets.

只會切換開關的程式語言

來源：esolangs.org

網站 esolangs.org 是一群志工一起寫的百科，專門收集奇怪又好玩的程式語言。

其中一個叫 Boolfuck。它的記憶只有一條很長很長的帶子，每一格只能是 0 或 1。它不會加減，「+」只是把這一格換成相反的數字，「;」一次印出一格。

可是，別的程式語言能算的東西，它也全都算得出來。原來最簡單的開關，也藏著電腦的全部本領。

The website esolangs.org is an encyclopedia written collaboratively by volunteers, devoted to collecting strange and playful programming languages. One of them is called Boolfuck. Its memory is a single, very long tape, and each cell can hold only a 0 or a 1. It cannot add or subtract. The "+" command simply flips the current cell to the opposite digit, and the ";" command prints one cell at a time. Yet anything that other programming languages can compute, Boolfuck can compute too. It turns out that the simplest of switches hides the full power of a computer.
