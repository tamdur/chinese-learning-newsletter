黑名單上的抓狂歌

來源：Wikipedia／中央社

1989年，台灣剛解除戒嚴（軍隊管理社會的命令）兩年。一群音樂人用「黑名單工作室」當名字，出了專輯《抓狂歌》。黑名單，就是政府不讓某些人回家的名單。

他們把台語民謠、饒舌（用說的方式來唱）和搖滾放在一起，用台語唱出街頭小人物的故事。有一首歌還放進了國歌的聲音。

很多人說，這張唱片打開了九十年代「新台語歌」的大門。

In 1989, Taiwan had lifted martial law (the military's control over society) only two years earlier. A group of musicians chose the name "Blacklist Studio" and released an album called Crazy Song. A "blacklist" was a list of people the government would not allow to come home. The band blended Taiwanese folk music, rap (singing in a spoken, rhythmic style), and rock, and used Taiwanese to tell stories of ordinary people on the street. One song even worked in the sound of the national anthem. Many people say this record opened the door to the "new Taiwanese-language songs" of the 1990s.

只在芬蘭出生的彩虹石

來源：GoSaimaa

1940年，人們在芬蘭東南方的小村 Ylämaa 挖土蓋防守工事，挖出一種會發光的石頭。從不同角度看，它會閃出藍、綠、金色，被取名叫「光譜石」。

到今天，全世界只有這裡能大量開採這種石頭。村子後來叫自己「芬蘭寶石村」，有寶石博物館，也能看工人切開石頭，露出裡面的彩光。

遊客還可以自己在碎石堆裡找石頭，再做成一枚戒指，當作蜜月的紀念。

In 1940, people digging earthworks for defensive fortifications in Ylämaa, a small village in southeastern Finland, unearthed a stone that glows. Viewed from different angles, it flashes blue, green, and gold, and it was named "spectrolite." To this day, this is the only place in the world where the stone is mined in large quantities. The village later took the name "Finland's Gemstone Village." It has a gemstone museum, and visitors can watch workers cut the stones open to reveal the colorful light inside. Tourists can also dig through piles of broken rock to find their own stones, then have one made into a ring as a souvenir of their honeymoon.

西區的尋寶週末

來源：Chicago Gallery News

11月14日和15日，芝加哥的 Randolph Street Market 會在 West Loop 的 Plumbers Hall 舉行室內冬季市集。這個市集已經辦了二十多年。

大約125個攤位分在好幾層樓，賣老衣服、五六十年代的家用品、黑膠唱片和古董首飾，也有新的藝術作品。現場還有音樂和酒吧。

網上買票大約15美元，現場20美元，12歲以下小孩免費。來找找別人留下的寶貝吧！

On November 14 and 15, Chicago's Randolph Street Market will hold an indoor winter market at Plumbers Hall in the West Loop. The market has been running for more than twenty years. Some 125 vendors are spread across several floors, selling vintage clothing, household goods from the 1950s and 1960s, vinyl records, and antique jewelry, as well as new works of art. There is live music on site, and a bar. Advance tickets online cost about $15, or $20 at the door. Children under 12 get in free. Come and hunt for treasures that other people have left behind!

一個人守了三十年的沉船網站

來源：Great Lakes Shipwreck Research

1994年，美國威斯康辛州的歷史學者 Brendon Baillod 做了一個小網頁，收集五大湖沉船的資料。這是網路上最早談這個題目的網頁之一。

三十多年來，網站慢慢長大，記下一艘艘沉船的故事、船的歷史和老照片。潛水員和歷史學者到今天還在用它。

他後來也成立了研究基金會，但這個手工做的網站，一直是他一個人的心血。

In 1994, Brendon Baillod, a historian in Wisconsin, built a small web page to collect data on shipwrecks in the Great Lakes. It was one of the earliest pages on the internet to cover the subject. Over more than thirty years, the site has slowly grown, recording the story of one wreck after another, the histories of the ships, and old photographs. Divers and historians still use it today. He later set up a research foundation, but this hand-built website has remained his alone, a labor of love he has carried by himself.
