從噪音裡聽見自由

來源：Sound Traces

1992年，輔仁大學三個學生組成「零與聲音解放組織」。他們用撿來的東西錄音，自己做、自己賣，完成台灣第一捲噪音（很吵、沒有曲調的聲音）卡帶。

帶頭的林其蔚讀法文系。後來幾年，他們在台北辦「噪音行動」表演，讓觀眾傳著一捲寫了注音符號的紙帶，一起出聲。對他們來說，吵雜也是一種音樂。

In 1992, three students at Fu Jen Catholic University formed the "Zero and Sound Liberation Organization." They recorded with objects they had picked up, made the tapes themselves, and sold them themselves, producing Taiwan's first noise cassette (sound that is loud and has no melody). The leader, Lin Chi-wei, studied French literature. In the following years, they staged "Noise Action" performances in Taipei, where audience members passed around a paper tape printed with Zhuyin symbols and made sounds together. For them, racket was also a kind of music.

森林與湖邊的芬蘭探戈

來源：Music Finland

探戈在1913年左右來到芬蘭。後來，芬蘭人把它變成自己的樣子：用手風琴演奏，曲子大多聽起來很憂傷，歌詞唱的是森林、湖水和鄉下，也唱出漫長黑暗的冬天。

最有名的歌是1955年的〈Satumaa〉，意思是「童話之地」。每年七月，小鎮塞伊奈約基會舉辦探戈節，選出探戈國王和皇后，吸引超過十萬人。

Tango arrived in Finland around 1913. Over time, Finns made it their own: they played it on the accordion, and most of the tunes sound melancholy. The lyrics sing of forests, lakes, and the countryside, and of long, dark winters. The most famous song is "Satumaa" from 1955, which means "land of fairy tales." Every July, the small town of Seinäjoki holds a tango festival where a tango king and queen are chosen, drawing more than 100,000 visitors.

喝一杯，來場哲學辯論

來源：Choose Chicago

11月19日星期四，芝加哥的「超級辯論」要辦第二場哲學對決。芝加哥大學的哲學老師班傑明·卡拉德，會和一位還沒公開的對手當場辯論。

觀眾可以替雙方打分數。辯論結束後，大家還能一邊吃東西、喝酒，一邊繼續討論。一般門票30美元。

On Thursday, November 19, Chicago's "Super Debate" series holds its second philosophy showdown. Benjamin Callard, a philosophy professor at the University of Chicago, will go head-to-head with an opponent who has not yet been revealed. The audience gets to score both sides. Once the debate ends, everyone can keep the discussion going over food and drinks. General admission tickets cost $30.

一個人寫的山頂小屋百科

來源：TrailChick

美國華盛頓州的山上，還留著大約90座老的防火瞭望塔（站在高處看森林有沒有起火的小屋）。

一位愛爬山的女生在部落格 TrailChick 上，把它們一座一座記下來：哪些還有人值班，哪些可以租來過夜，還教大家拜訪時要有什麼禮貌。這是一份用熱情畫出來的山頂地圖。

In the mountains of Washington State, about 90 old fire lookouts (small cabins perched on high ground, where people watch for forest fires) are still standing. One female hiker, through her blog TrailChick, has recorded them one by one: which ones still have someone on duty, which can be rented for an overnight stay, and what good manners to observe when you visit. It is a mountaintop map drawn with real enthusiasm.
