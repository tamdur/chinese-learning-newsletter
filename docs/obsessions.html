月琴變成鼓：排灣歌手的跳舞專輯

來源：Wind Music Label

排灣族歌手 Sauljaljui 花了四年，做出第一張由她自己製作的專輯《Vaivaik》，在排灣語裡是「出發」的意思。

月琴（一種圓圓的、用手彈的樂器）平常用來彈旋律，她卻只拿它來打節奏，讓整張唱片聽起來像可以跳舞的音樂。主打歌還請來馬來西亞歌手 Alena Murang 和台灣歌手 Putad Pihay 一起唱。

這張專輯拿下金曲獎最佳編曲和最佳原住民語專輯，後來還到歐洲巡迴演出。

Paiwan singer Sauljaljui spent four years creating her first self-produced album, "Vaivaik," a title meaning "set out" in the Paiwan language.

The yueqin—a round, hand-plucked instrument—is typically used for melody, but Sauljaljui uses it purely for percussion, giving the entire album a danceable quality. The lead single features Malaysian singer Alena Murang and Taiwanese singer Putad Pihay.

The album won Golden Melody Awards for Best Arrangement and Best Indigenous Language Album, and subsequently toured throughout Europe.

寫進工作規定的咖啡時間

來源：Visit Finland

芬蘭人是全世界最愛喝咖啡的人，每人一天大約喝四杯。工作規定還保障上班族每天有兩次十分鐘的咖啡休息，叫做 kahvitauko，不算在午餐時間裡。

芬蘭的冬天又長又暗，有時一天只有三、四個小時有太陽，一杯熱咖啡能讓人打起精神。他們喜歡配一個灑了糖的甜麵包，或是有肉桂香味的捲麵包 korvapuusti。

三月去度蜜月時，不妨在赫爾辛基找一家老咖啡館，上午像當地人一樣，點一杯咖啡配一個麵包。

Finns are the world's biggest coffee drinkers, consuming about four cups per person each day. Their labor regulations guarantee office workers two ten-minute coffee breaks daily—called kahvitauko—which don't count toward their lunch hour.

Finland's winters are long and dark, with some days offering only three or four hours of daylight. A hot cup of coffee can lift the spirits during these gloomy stretches. Finns typically enjoy theirs with a sugar-dusted pastry or a cinnamon roll known as korvapuusti.

If you're heading to Helsinki for a honeymoon in March, find yourself an old-fashioned café and spend the morning like the locals do—order a coffee and a pastry.

用文字打拳：Write Club 第十七季開打

來源：TheaterMania

十月二十日星期二晚上七點半，Write Club Chicago 在 Lakeview 的 GMan Tavern（3740 N. Clark St.）開始第十七季。這是一場「文字拳擊賽」。

兩位作家一組，各拿到相反的題目，像是「秩序」（整齊有規則）對上「混亂」。每人只有七分鐘，上台讀出自己寫的文章。

台下觀眾投票選出贏家，贏家可以拿獎金和一個大獎盃。七點開門，門口建議捐二十五美元。

On Tuesday, October 20th at 7:30 p.m., Write Club Chicago launches its seventeenth season at GMan Tavern (3740 N. Clark St.) in Lakeview. It's a "text boxing match."

Two writers per round receive opposing prompts—for instance, "order" (neat and organized) versus "chaos." Each has just seven minutes to take the stage and read what they've written.

The audience votes for the winner, who takes home prize money and a large trophy. Doors open at 7 p.m.; a twenty-five dollar donation is suggested at the entrance.

一本筆記本，五十萬支公用電話

來源：The Payphone Project

一九九五年，紐約鋼琴家 Mark A. Thomas 帶著一本藍色筆記本走在曼哈頓街上，把路過的公用電話號碼一個一個寫下來。

他的想法很簡單：請陌生人隨便打一支街上的電話，看看誰會接。後來世界各地的人都寄來自己家附近的號碼，The Payphone Project 就這樣變成一個巨大的網站。

現在大部分的電話早就被拆掉了，但這些號碼還有用：曾經有一位媽媽靠它找到離家出走的女兒。

In 1995, New York pianist Mark A. Thomas took to the streets of Manhattan with a blue notebook, jotting down payphone numbers one by one as he passed them.

His idea was simple: ask strangers to call random payphones on the street and see who would answer. Eventually, people around the world began sending in payphone numbers from their own neighborhoods, and The Payphone Project grew into a massive website.

Most of those payphones have been gone for years now, but the numbers still prove useful: one mother was able to find her runaway daughter with help from it.
