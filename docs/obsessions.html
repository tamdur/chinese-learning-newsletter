女聲扮男聲的老唱片

來源：聯合報

1960到70年代，惠美唱片出了一套廣播歌仔戲唱片。主角是年輕的楊麗花，她唱「小生」，就是由女生來演戲裡的男主角。她把聲音練得像男人一樣。

這些唱片後來幾乎不見了。雲林科技大學的柯榮三老師花了很多年去找，原本19部戲，他找回了17部。

其中一部是《薛平貴與王寶釧》，可能是這個老故事最早留下來的錄音。

In the 1960s and 70s, Huimei Records released a series of radio Taiwanese opera (gezai opera) recordings. The lead performer was a young Yang Lihua, who sang the xiaosheng role, the male lead that was traditionally played by a woman on stage. She trained her voice until it sounded like a man's. These records later nearly disappeared. Professor Ke Rong-san of National Yunlin University of Science and Technology spent many years searching for them. Of the original 19 operas, he recovered 17. One of them is Xue Pinggui and Wang Baochuan, which may be the earliest surviving recording of this old story.

一個叫「皮褲」的花瓶

來源：Wikipedia / Cooper Hewitt

芬蘭最有名的設計，是阿爾托的波浪花瓶。1936年，他拿它去參加玻璃設計比賽，取的名字很奇怪，叫「愛斯基摩女人的皮褲」。

1937年，赫爾辛基的薩伏伊餐廳開幕，桌上就放著這個花瓶，後來它就改叫「薩伏伊花瓶」。很多人說，它彎彎的邊，像芬蘭湖岸的樣子。

最早的花瓶不是用尺畫出來的。人們把木棍插在地上圍成一圈，再把熱熱的玻璃吹進去，讓它在木棍中間鼓起來，邊就變成波浪了。

The most famous piece of Finnish design is Alvar Aalto's wave vase. In 1936, he entered it in a glass design competition under an odd name: "Eskimo Woman's Leather Trousers." In 1937, the Savoy restaurant opened in Helsinki with this vase on its tables, and it was later renamed the "Savoy Vase." Many people say its curvy rim looks like the shoreline of a Finnish lake. The earliest vases were not drawn with a ruler. People stuck wooden sticks into the ground in a circle, then blew hot glass inside so it swelled out between the sticks, and that is how the edge turned into waves.

在海德公園聽性別的歷史

來源：Chicago Humanities Festival

11月7日星期六下午4點到5點，芝加哥人文節秋季的最後一天，在芝加哥大學舉行。

主講人是歷史學家蘇珊．史崔克。她是跨性別研究的開路人，也拍過得到艾美獎的紀錄片《尖叫皇后》。這次她要談自己的新書《改變性別》。

一般人都可以買票。能在小小的大學講堂裡，聽一位開創整個學問領域的學者說話，這樣的機會很少。

On Saturday, November 7, from 4 to 5 p.m., the final day of Chicago Humanities Festival's autumn season, the talk takes place at the University of Chicago. The speaker is historian Susan Stryker, a pioneer of transgender studies who also made the Emmy-winning documentary Screaming Queens. This time she will discuss her new book, Changing Sex. Tickets are open to the public. The chance to hear a scholar who helped found an entire field speak in a small university lecture hall is a rare one.

永遠不停的念珠電梯

來源：Lift and Escalator Library

英國有一個小小的慈善團體，把幾十年來研究電梯和手扶梯的文章放上網路，做成一座免費的圖書館。

其中一篇講「念珠電梯」：一串沒有門的小車廂一直繞圈轉，像手上的念珠，所以用拉丁文的「我們的天父」來命名。1868年，英國利物浦第一次出現這種電梯。

1970年代起，因為不夠安全，就不准再蓋新的。可是今天德國還有大約230座，捷克還有68座，因為大家都捨不得讓它們停下來。

A small British charity has put decades of articles about elevators and escalators online, creating a free library for anyone to read. One piece covers the "paternoster," a chain of doorless little cars that circles endlessly like prayer beads on a rosary, which is why it is named after the Latin words for "Our Father." The first one opened in Liverpool, England, in 1868. Starting in the 1970s they were judged too unsafe, and no new ones were allowed to be built. Yet Germany still has about 230 today, and the Czech Republic has 68, because people just can't bear to let them stop.
