工地走出來的台語饒舌

來源：The News Lens

李英宏二十幾歲時在工地當工頭。三十歲那年，他在顏社推出第一張專輯《台北直直撞》，馬上拿到兩個金曲獎提名。

台語饒舌（一種快速唸唱的歌）在他之前很少見。他用爸爸那種工人的口氣唱台語，配上復古的放克、迪斯可節奏。後來台北北門一帶，還掀起一波改編這首歌的熱潮。

In his twenties, Li Yinghong worked as a foreman on construction sites. At thirty, he released his debut album "Taipei Head-On" with Yan She Records and immediately earned two Golden Melody Award nominations. Taiwanese rap—a rapid vocal style—was rarely heard before him. He sang in Taiwanese using his father's working-class tone, paired with retro funk and disco rhythms. Around Taipei's North Gate district, the song even sparked a wave of covers and remixes.

玻璃鳥的故鄉：芬蘭最老的玻璃村

來源：Nuutajärvi Glass Village

芬蘭的努塔耶爾維村，從一七九三年到今天，師傅一直守著同一批爐火，是全國最老的玻璃村。二○二三年，這門手藝被列入聯合國的無形文化遺產名單。

有名的設計師法蘭克曾在這裡當藝術總監，後來把棒子交給托伊卡。托伊卡設計的玻璃鳥就在這座老工廠誕生，現在成了很多人收藏的寶貝。遊客還能看師傅用嘴吹出滾燙的玻璃。

In Finland's Nuutajärvi village, craftsmen have tended the same furnaces since 1793, making it the country's oldest glass workshop. In 2023, the craft was inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list. Legendary designer Franck once served as its art director, later handing the torch to Toikka. Toikka's glass birds—born in this historic factory—have become treasured collector's items. Visitors can watch the craftsmen blow and shape molten glass right before their eyes.

樓梯底下的人生：格萊斯納屋僕人導覽

來源：Glessner House Museum

九月二十六日早上，芝加哥草原大道上的格萊斯納屋，要打開平常不開放的僕人房間，一次只收八個人。

這棟一八八七年蓋的大宅，有六成空間是為八位住在家裡的僕人準備的。導覽用女主人法蘭西絲留下的日記，說出馬車伕查爾斯、廚師瑪蒂等人的真實生活。要先報名，也要有爬樓梯的力氣。

On the morning of September 26th, the Glessner House on Chicago's Prairie Avenue will open its normally closed servant quarters to visitors—eight at a time. This grand mansion, built in 1887, devotes sixty percent of its space to the eight servants who lived in the household. The tour draws on the diary kept by Frances, the lady of the house, to tell the true stories of people like Charles the carriage driver and Mattie the cook. You'll need to register in advance and have the strength to climb stairs.

一副教你好好迷路的紙牌

來源：Near Future Laboratory

二○○八年，設計工作室「近未來實驗室」做了一副「漂流牌」。每張牌上有一樣東西和一個動作：看到它，就拍一張照，或是在下個路口右轉。

這個玩法來自法國的「漂流」想法：不看地圖亂走，把住慣的城市重新看一次。這副牌在紐約的藝術節登場，借走的人走完，就把牌傳給下一個人。

In 2008, the design studio Near Future Laboratory created a deck called the Drift Cards. Each card features an object and an action: when you spot it, take a photo, or turn right at the next corner. The game draws from the French concept of "drifting"—wandering a city without a map, seeing the familiar streets with fresh eyes. The deck debuted at an arts festival in New York, where each person who borrowed it would pass the cards along to the next person after completing their walk.
