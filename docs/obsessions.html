把水聲變成音樂：臺南「聽說」的水之歌

來源：Tingshuo (聽說) Bandcamp

臺南有一個聲音團體，叫做「聽說」。他們花了大約三年，到處錄下水的聲音：河流、魚市場、科學園區裡的水塔，還有處理髒水的工廠。

他們把這些聲音做成專輯 Listening to the Water。這不是輕柔的大自然音樂，而是一座城市「水管」的聲音。臺灣常常缺水，這些聲音也藏著大家對乾旱（很久不下雨）的擔心。

後來，團員也到韓國、日本和美國錄水聲，讓一個臺南的故事，變成跨國的聆聽（仔細地聽）。

In Tainan there is a sound collective called Tingshuo (聽說, a name that also means "I hear" or "they say"). Over roughly three years, the members went around recording the sounds of water: rivers, fish markets, the water towers inside a science park, and factories that treat wastewater. They turned these recordings into an album, Listening to the Water. It is not gentle nature music. It is the sound of a city's "water pipes." Taiwan often faces water shortages, and these sounds also carry people's worry about drought. Later, the members traveled to South Korea, Japan, and the United States to record water sounds too, turning a Tainan story into a transnational act of listening.

不會天黑的森林接力賽

來源：Jukola.com

每年六月，芬蘭會舉辦「尤科拉接力賽」。這是世界上最大的定向越野（拿著地圖和指南針找路）比賽，從1949年就開始了。大約一萬八千人參加，不能用手機。

男子組在晚上出發，但芬蘭夏天的夜晚不會全黑。跑者在淡淡的光裡穿過草叢和泥地，直到日出才跑回終點。

比賽的名字來自芬蘭小說《七兄弟》裡的一家人。大家在森林裡露營一整個週末，好像蓋起一座臨時的森林城市。

Every June, Finland hosts the Jukola Relay. It is the world's largest orienteering race (navigating with a map and compass), and it has been held since 1949. About 18,000 people take part, and phones are not allowed. The men's teams set off in the evening, but Finnish summer nights never get fully dark. Runners push through brush and mud in the faint light, and they do not reach the finish until sunrise. The race is named after a family in the Finnish novel Seven Brothers. Everyone camps in the forest for the whole weekend, like a temporary forest city going up overnight.

用報紙守護國家：夏威夷人的紙上抗爭

來源：Newberry Library

十一月二十日星期五，芝加哥的紐伯瑞圖書館有一場免費講座。芝加哥大學的博士生 Sam Maza 會告訴大家，一百多年前的夏威夷人怎麼用印刷品保護自己的國家。

1843年，英國曾經短暫占領夏威夷；1893年，夏威夷王國被推翻。當時的人寫報紙、印小冊子、編歌曲，還一起簽請願書（很多人簽名的請求信）。

連王國自己的郵局，也成了傳送反對聲音的管道。講座不用錢，但要先報名。

On Friday, November 20, the Newberry Library in Chicago is hosting a free lecture. Sam Maza, a PhD student at the University of Chicago, will explain how Hawaiians more than a century ago used printed materials to protect their nation. In 1843, Britain briefly occupied Hawaii, and in 1893, the Hawaiian Kingdom was overthrown. People at the time wrote newspapers, printed pamphlets, composed songs, and gathered signatures on petitions (letters requesting action, signed by many people). Even the kingdom's own post office became a channel for carrying dissenting voices. The lecture is free, but registration is required in advance.

一張裝滿元素的桌子

來源：theodoregray.com

2002年，美國人西奧多‧格雷需要一張新的會議桌。他讀一本談化學的回憶錄時，看錯了一段話，就決定做一張「元素週期表」形狀的木桌。

每個元素都有一塊小木蓋，打開是一個小盒子，裡面放著真的元素。世界各地的陌生人還寄東西給他。這張桌子後來得到「搞笑諾貝爾獎」化學獎。

格雷沒有停下來。他的網站記下了幾千個樣本：哪一天買的、從哪裡來、花了多少錢。這是一個人對化學幾十年的熱愛。

In 2002, Theodore Gray, an American, needed a new conference table. While reading a memoir about chemistry, he misread a passage and decided to build a wooden table in the shape of the periodic table. Each element got its own small wooden lid. Open it and you find a little box holding a real sample of that element. Strangers from around the world even sent him things. The table later won an Ig Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Gray did not stop there. His website logs several thousand samples: when each was bought, where it came from, and how much it cost. It is one person's decades-long love of chemistry.
