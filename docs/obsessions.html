油污手裡的吉他：台灣第一張工人抗議專輯

來源：Multitude.asia

一九九六年，台灣很多工廠突然關門。一群工人和年輕人組成了「黑手那卡西」樂團。「黑手」是台灣話，指手上都是油污（機器的髒油）的修理工人。

一九九七年，他們用很少的錢，錄下台灣第一張工人抗議專輯《福氣個屁》。那時候有一句很紅的廣告詞「福氣啦」，他們把它改成生氣的話，問大家：經濟奇蹟到底是誰的福氣？

後來他們還唱了《台灣牛大戰WTO》。他們真正的錄音室，其實是工會和罷工（停下工作來抗議）的現場。

In 1996, many factories across Taiwan suddenly closed. A group of workers and young people formed the band Black Hand Nakasi. "Black Hand" is Taiwanese slang for mechanics and repair workers whose hands are covered in machine grease. In 1997, with almost no money, they recorded Taiwan's first worker-protest album, "Blessed My Ass." There was a hugely popular advertising slogan at the time — "How blessed!" — and they flipped it into an angry retort, asking everyone: whose blessing was the economic miracle, really? Later they also recorded "Taiwan Ox Battles the WTO." Their real recording studio wasn't a studio at all — it was the union halls and the picket lines of strikes, where workers protest by stopping work.

站在羊毛上結婚：芬蘭的婚禮地毯

來源：thisisFINLAND

芬蘭有一種厚厚的羊毛地毯，叫做 ryijy。九世紀時，維京人把它帶到芬蘭西邊的海岸。

到了十七世紀，它成了婚禮的主角。新娘會自己織一張有花紋的地毯，當作嫁妝（結婚時帶過去的東西）。結婚那天，兩個人就站在上面；之後它會變成新床上的被子。

這門手藝差點消失。一九二九年，設計師 Eva Brummer 在赫爾辛基的工作室把它救了回來。現在，芬蘭的新人又開始訂做自己的婚禮地毯了。

Finland has a kind of thick woolen rug called a ryijy. In the 9th century, Vikings brought it to Finland's western coast. By the 17th century it had become the centerpiece of weddings. A bride would weave her own patterned rug as part of her dowry — the possessions she brought into the marriage. On the wedding day the couple stood on it; afterward it became the cover on their new bed. The craft nearly disappeared. In 1929, designer Eva Brummer revived it at her studio in Helsinki. Today, Finnish couples are once again commissioning their own wedding rugs.

把壞掉的東西帶來：圖書館裡的修理咖啡館

來源：Forward Chicago

八月八日星期六上午十點到中午，芝加哥林肯廣場的 Sulzer 圖書館裡，有一場免費的「修理咖啡館」。

壞掉的玩具、破了的布娃娃、斷掉的項鍊、裂開的衣服，都可以帶過去。志工（自願來幫忙的人）會坐下來，陪你一起把它修好。想付錢的人，隨意捐一點就好。

這個聚會已經辦了十幾年，幾乎每個月的第二個星期六都有。那天，圖書館的會議室會變成一間工作坊。

On Saturday, August 8th, from 10am to noon, there's a free "Repair Café" at the Sulzer Library in Chicago's Lincoln Square. Broken toys, torn stuffed animals, snapped necklaces, split clothing — bring any of it along. Volunteers, people who come to help of their own accord, will sit down and fix it with you. If you'd like to pay, donate whatever you please. The gathering has been running for more than a decade, on almost every second Saturday of the month. For that morning, the library's meeting room turns into a workshop.

藏在學名裡的玩笑：一個人的怪名字目錄

來源：curioustaxonomy.net

每一種生物都有一個拉丁文的學名。取名字的時候，科學家其實常常在偷偷開玩笑。

科普作家 Mark Isaak 做了一個網站 curioustaxonomy.net，花了幾十年收集這些名字。有一種翼龍（會飛的古代爬蟲）叫 Arthurdactylus conandoylensis，紀念寫福爾摩斯的作家；有一種三葉蟲叫 Godzillius，來自哥吉拉。

網站上每一個名字，都寫清楚是出自哪一篇論文。

Every living thing has a Latin scientific name. And when it comes to the naming, scientists are quite often slipping in a private joke. The popular-science writer Mark Isaak built a website, curioustaxonomy.net, and has spent decades collecting these names. There is a pterosaur — a flying ancient reptile — called Arthurdactylus conandoylensis, in tribute to the author who wrote Sherlock Holmes; there is a trilobite called Godzillius, after Godzilla. For every name on the site, it is spelled out exactly which paper it came from.
