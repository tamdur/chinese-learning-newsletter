三個音的祕密：客家山歌遇見藍調

來源：作家生活誌 showwe.tw

台灣音樂家林少英在美國柏克利音樂學院學爵士鋼琴。她研究客家山歌多年，發現老山歌的旋律核心只有三個音，竟然和美國藍調（黑人的憂傷歌謠）的骨架很像。

二○一五年，她推出專輯《山歌浪想》。她沒有幫老歌穿上華麗的新衣，而是照著這副骨架重新蓋房子。錄音時，爵士樂手、嗩吶（一種響亮的吹奏樂器）首席和交響樂團的弦樂、法國號一起上場。這張專輯入圍了金曲獎。

Taiwanese musician Lin Shao-Ying studied jazz piano at the Berklee College of Music in the United States. After years of studying Hakka mountain songs, she made a striking discovery: the melodic core of these old songs consists of just three notes — remarkably close to the skeleton of American blues, the sorrowful songs of Black America.

In 2015 she released the album Mountain Song Reverie. Rather than dressing the old songs in lavish new clothes, she rebuilt the house on that same skeleton. In the recording sessions, jazz players, the principal suona (a loud, piercing traditional wind instrument), and an orchestra's strings and French horns all took the stage together. The album was nominated for a Golden Melody Award.

一句求婚的話，造出一座刀城

來源：Iisakki Järvenpää Knife Factory / Visit Kauhava

一八七九年十月二十日，二十歲的伊薩基向未婚妻莉莎宣布：「我決定要當一個做刀的人。」這句話後來讓芬蘭西部的小鎮考哈瓦成為全國的刀具之都。

他創立的工廠今天還在，用的是一九五八年蓋的廠房。刀身、銀和銅的配件、白樺樹皮做的刀柄、皮革刀鞘（裝刀的套子），全部在廠裡手工打造。旅客可以預約參觀。隔壁的刀與紡織博物館，收藏著這一帶兩項老手藝。

On 20 October 1879, twenty-year-old Iisakki declared to his fiancée Liisa: "I have decided to become a knife maker." That one sentence would eventually turn Kauhava, a small town in western Finland, into the country's knife capital.

The workshop he founded is still going today, in a factory building put up in 1958. Blades, silver and brass fittings, handles made of birch bark, and leather sheaths are all forged and finished by hand on site. Visitors can book a tour. Next door, the Knife and Textile Museum holds the two old crafts of this region.

林肯公園圖書館的毛線圈

來源：Chicago Public Library

每個星期二下午四點到六點，芝加哥公共圖書館林肯公園分館都有一場免費的編織聚會，八月十一日也不例外。地點在富勒頓大道一一五○號，帶頭的是一位志工，名叫黛比。

老手會帶著做到一半的作品來，互相欣賞，也一起想辦法解決卡住的地方。沒學過的人也歡迎：不用帶毛線，不用帶棒針，走進去就有人從頭教你。不必報名，也不必買票。

Every Tuesday from 4 to 6 p.m., the Lincoln Park branch of the Chicago Public Library hosts a free knitting and crochet circle — and August 11th is no exception. It meets at 1150 W. Fullerton Ave., led by a volunteer named Deb.

Old hands bring their works-in-progress to show off and to puzzle out the tricky spots together. Complete beginners are welcome too: no need to bring yarn, no need to bring needles — walk in and someone will teach you from scratch. No registration, no ticket.

快要消失的聲音，都收在這裡

來源：Museum of Endangered Sounds

二○一二年，美國維吉尼亞州的三位廣告系學生做了一個網頁，專門收集快要消失的科技聲音。起頭是其中一人發現：舊手機的按鍵會喀喀響，新的觸控螢幕卻安安靜靜。

網站上有撥接上網的刺耳叫聲、軟碟機讀取的聲音、錄影帶倒帶、電子信箱的提示音、手機鈴聲，還有小精靈遊戲投幣的叮噹。他們還編了一位虛構（假想出來）的館長來主持。十四年過去，這座玩笑做成的博物館還開著。

In 2012, three advertising students in Virginia built a web page devoted to collecting the sounds of technology that is about to disappear. It began when one of them noticed that the keys on an old phone went click-clack, while the new touchscreen sat perfectly silent.

The site holds the piercing screech of a dial-up connection, the sound of a floppy drive reading, a VHS tape rewinding, the email notification chime, mobile ringtones, and the clink of a coin dropping into a Pac-Man machine. They even invented a fictional curator to preside over it. Fourteen years on, this museum built as a joke is still open.
