般若波羅蜜多心經

舍利子，是諸法空相，不生不滅，不垢不淨，不增不減。是故空中無色，無受想行識。

Shariputra, all dharmas are marked with emptiness — they are neither born nor destroyed, neither defiled nor pure, neither increasing nor decreasing. Therefore, in emptiness there is no form, no feelings, perceptions, mental formations, or consciousness.

孟子・公孫丑上

孟子曰：「伯夷，非其君不事，非其友不友。不立於惡人之朝，不與惡人言。立於惡人之朝，與惡人言，如以朝衣朝冠坐於塗炭。推惡惡之心，思與鄉人立，其冠不正，望望然去之，若將浼焉。是故諸侯雖有善其辭命而至者，不受也。不受也者，是亦不屑就已。柳下惠，不羞汙君，不卑小官。進不隱賢，必以其道。遺佚而不怨，阨窮而不憫。故曰：『爾為爾，我為我，雖袒裼裸裎於我側，爾焉能浼我哉？』故由由然與之偕而不自失焉，援而止之而止。援而止之而止者，是亦不屑去已。」

Mencius said: "Bo Yi would serve no lord who was not his ideal and befriend no one who was not his ideal. He would not stand in the court of a wicked man, nor would he speak with a wicked man. To stand in such a court or speak with such a man would be, to him, like sitting in court robes and court cap amidst mud and coals. Extending this hatred of wickedness, if he stood beside a villager whose cap was crooked, he would turn away in disgust as if he might be defiled. For this reason, even when feudal lords came with polite invitations, he would refuse them. He refused because he disdained to go to them. Liu Xia Hui, on the other hand, was not ashamed to serve a corrupt lord and did not consider a minor office beneath him. When advanced, he did not conceal his talents, but always acted according to his principles. When overlooked and left in obscurity, he did not complain; when reduced to poverty, he did not grieve. He said: 'You are you and I am I. Even if you were to stand naked beside me, how could you defile me?' So he was content to remain in their company without losing himself. When they pulled at him to stay, he stayed—he stayed because he did not deign to leave."

Since ancient times, Buddha has only transmitted the original essence, and master has only secretly entrusted the original mind. The robe has become a source of contention — let it stop with you; do not transmit it further. If you pass on this robe, your life will hang by a thread. You must leave quickly, for I fear someone may harm you.' Huineng asked, 'Where should I go?' The Patriarch said, 'Stop when you reach Huai; hide when you encounter Hui.' Huineng received the robe and bowl at the third watch and said, 'I am originally a southerner and do not know these mountain paths. How do I reach the river crossing?' The Fifth Patriarch said, 'Do not worry — I will escort you myself.' The Patriarch accompanied him all the way to the Jiujiang courier station and told him to board a boat. The Fifth Patriarch took the oar and began rowing. Huineng said, 'Please sit, Master. It is proper for the disciple to row.' The Patriarch said, 'It is fitting that I ferry you across.'
