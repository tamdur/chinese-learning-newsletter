般若波羅蜜多心經

觀自在菩薩，行深般若波羅蜜多時，照見五蘊皆空，度一切苦厄。

Avalokitesvara Bodhisattva, when practicing the profound Prajna Paramita, illuminated the five aggregates and saw that they are all empty, thus overcoming all suffering and distress.

孟子・滕文公上

「夏后氏五十而貢，殷人七十而助，周人百畝而徹，其實皆什一也。徹者，徹也；助者，藉也。龍子曰：『治地莫善於助，莫不善於貢。貢者校數歲之中以為常。樂歲，粒米狼戾，多取之而不為虐，則寡取之；凶年，糞其田而不足，則必取盈焉。為民父母，使民盻盻然，將終歲勤動，不得以養其父母，又稱貸而益之。使老稚轉乎溝壑，惡在其為民父母也？』夫世祿，滕固行之矣。《詩》云：『雨我公田，遂及我私。』惟助為有公田。由此觀之，雖周亦助也。

'Under the Xia dynasty, each family held fifty mu and paid tribute; under the Yin dynasty, each family held seventy mu under the aid system; under the Zhou dynasty, each family held a hundred mu under the tithe system. In reality, all amounted to a tax of one-tenth. The tithe means sharing; the aid system means lending labor. Long Zi said: "For land taxation, nothing is better than the aid system, and nothing worse than the tribute system. Under the tribute system, an average is calculated over several years to set a fixed amount. In good years, when grain is so abundant it goes to waste, one could take more without being oppressive, yet one takes only the fixed amount. In bad years, when the harvest does not even replenish the soil, one still demands the full amount. To act as parent of the people yet cause them to gaze about with resentful eyes — toiling all year long, unable to support their parents, forced to borrow at interest to make up the difference, so that the old and young end up dead in the ditches — where in this is being a parent to the people?" Now hereditary stipends are something Teng already practices. The Odes say: "May the rain fall on our public field and then extend to our private plots." Only the aid system has public fields. From this we can see that even under the Zhou, the aid system was used.'

六祖大師法寶壇經・疑問品第三

人有兩種，法無兩般。迷悟有殊，見有遲疾。迷人念佛求生於彼，悟人自淨其心。所以佛言：『隨其心淨即佛土淨。』使君東方人，但心淨即無罪。雖西方人，心不淨亦有愆。東方人造罪，念佛求生西方。西方人造罪，念佛求生何國？凡愚不了自性，不識身中淨土，願東願西。悟人在處一般，所以佛言：『隨所住處恒安樂。』使君心地但無不善，西方去此不遙。若懷不善之心，念佛往生難到。今勸善知識，先除十惡即行十萬，後除八邪乃過八千。

'People are of two kinds, but the Dharma is not of two kinds. Delusion and awakening differ, and understanding may come slowly or quickly. The deluded recite the Buddha's name seeking rebirth in that land; the awakened purify their own minds. Therefore the Buddha said: "As the mind is purified, so is the Buddha-land purified." Prefect, you are a person of the East — just purify your mind and you are without transgression. Even a person of the West, if their mind is impure, still has faults. An Easterner who commits transgressions recites the Buddha's name seeking rebirth in the West. But when a Westerner commits transgressions, which land do they recite the Buddha's name to be reborn in? Ordinary fools do not understand their own nature and do not recognize the Pure Land within their own being, so they wish for the East or wish for the West. But for the awakened, any place is the same. Therefore the Buddha said: "Wherever one dwells, there is constant peace and joy." Prefect, if your mind is simply free from unwholesomeness, the West is not far from here. But if you harbor unwholesome thoughts in your mind, reciting the Buddha's name for rebirth will be hard to reach. Now I urge all good friends: first remove the ten evils, and you have traveled the 100,000 li; then remove the eight wrong practices, and you have crossed the 8,000 li.'
