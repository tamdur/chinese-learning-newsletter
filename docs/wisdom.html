般若波羅蜜多心經

故知般若波羅蜜多，是大神咒，是大明咒，是無上咒，是無等等咒，能除一切苦，真實不虛。故說般若波羅蜜多咒，即說咒曰：揭諦揭諦，波羅揭諦，波羅僧揭諦，菩提薩婆訶。

Therefore, know that Prajna Paramita is the great transcendent mantra, the great bright mantra, the supreme mantra, the unequalled mantra, which can remove all suffering and is true, not false. Therefore he proclaimed the Prajna Paramita mantra, saying: Gate gate paragate parasamgate bodhi svaha.

孟子．離婁下

孟子曰：「君子所以異於人者，以其存心也。君子以仁存心，以禮存心。仁者愛人，有禮者敬人。愛人者人恆愛之，敬人者人恆敬之。有人於此，其待我以橫逆，則君子必自反也：我必不仁也，必無禮也，此物奚宜至哉？其自反而仁矣，自反而有禮矣，其橫逆由是也，君子必自反也：我必不忠。自反而忠矣，其橫逆由是也，君子曰：『此亦妄人也已矣。如此則與禽獸奚擇哉？於禽獸又何難焉？』是故君子有終身之憂，無一朝之患也。乃若所憂則有之：舜人也，我亦人也。舜為法於天下，可傳於後世，我由未免為鄉人也，是則可憂也。憂之如何？如舜而已矣。若夫君子所患則亡矣。非仁無為也，非禮無行也。如有一朝之患，則君子不患矣。」

Mencius said: "What distinguishes the exemplary person from others is what he holds in his heart. The exemplary person holds benevolence in his heart and holds propriety in his heart. The benevolent person loves others; the person of propriety respects others. One who loves others will always be loved by them; one who respects others will always be respected by them. Suppose someone here treats me with violence and insolence. The exemplary person will surely reflect: 'I must have been lacking in benevolence; I must have been lacking in propriety. How else could this have come to me?' If upon reflection he has been benevolent, and upon reflection he has been proper, and the violence and insolence continue, the exemplary person will reflect again: 'I must have been lacking in sincerity.' If upon reflection he has been sincere, and the violence and insolence still continue, the exemplary person says: 'This is simply a deranged person. Being like this, what is there to choose between him and an animal? And why would one take issue with an animal?' Thus the exemplary person has lifelong concern but no momentary anxiety. His concern is this: Shun was a man, and I too am a man. Shun became a model for the world that could be passed down to later generations, while I still amount to nothing more than an ordinary villager — this is worth worrying about. And what does he do about it? He simply strives to be like Shun. As for what an exemplary person might be anxious about — there is nothing. He does nothing that is not benevolent; he does nothing that is not proper. Should some momentary trouble arise, the exemplary person is not troubled by it."

'That is what he teaches. I wonder what Dharma the Master uses to instruct people?' The Master said: 'If I claimed to have a teaching to give people, I would be deceiving you. I simply untie bonds according to circumstances, provisionally calling it samadhi. As for the precepts, concentration, and wisdom your teacher speaks of — they are truly inconceivable. But my understanding of precepts, concentration, and wisdom is different.' Zhicheng said: 'There should be only one kind of precepts, concentration, and wisdom. How can there be a difference?' The Master said: 'Your teacher's precepts, concentration, and wisdom are suited to those of the Mahayana. My precepts, concentration, and wisdom are suited to those of the Supreme Vehicle. Awakening and understanding differ; perception may be slow or fast. Listen to what I say and see whether it is the same as his or not. The Dharma I teach never departs from self-nature. To teach Dharma apart from the essence is called teaching through characteristics, and one's self-nature remains forever confused. You must know that all the myriad dharmas arise from the functioning of self-nature — this is the true teaching of precepts, concentration, and wisdom.'
