般若波羅蜜多心經

無罣礙故，無有恐怖，遠離顛倒夢想，究竟涅槃。三世諸佛，依般若波羅蜜多故，得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。

Without hindrance, there is no fear. Far from all inverted views, one attains nirvana. All buddhas of past, present, and future rely on Prajna Paramita and attain supreme, perfect enlightenment.

孟子 — 萬章下

曰：「以君命將之，再拜稽首而受。其後廩人繼粟，庖人繼肉，不以君命將之。子思以為鼎肉，使己僕僕爾亟拜也，非養君子之道也。堯之於舜也，使其子九男事之，二女女焉，百官牛羊倉廩備，以養舜於畎畝之中，後舉而加諸上位。故曰：王公之尊賢者也。」萬章曰：「敢問不見諸侯，何義也？」孟子曰：「在國曰市井之臣，在野曰草莽之臣，皆謂庶人。庶人不傳質為臣，不敢見於諸侯，禮也。」

Mencius said: "The gift should be presented with a formal command from the ruler, and the recipient bows twice, kowtows, and accepts. After that, the granary keeper delivers grain and the cook delivers meat on a regular basis, without each delivery requiring a formal command from the ruler. Zisi felt that having cauldrons of meat delivered with ceremony each time, forcing him to bow and kowtow repeatedly, was not the proper way to support a gentleman. When Yao dealt with Shun, he had his nine sons serve Shun, gave two of his daughters in marriage to him, and provided officials, cattle, sheep, and storehouses to support Shun amid the fields — and only afterward raised him to the highest position. Therefore this is called 'the way a king or duke honors a worthy man.'" Wan Zhang said: "May I ask on what principle a man does not go to see the feudal lords?" Mencius said: "One who lives in the city is called a subject of the marketplace. One who lives in the countryside is called a subject of the wilds. Both are commoners. A commoner who has not presented the ceremonial gift to become a subject does not dare appear before the feudal lord — that is proper ritual."

金剛般若波羅蜜經 — 一相無相分第九

「須菩提！於意云何？須陀洹能作是念：『我得須陀洹果』不？」須菩提言：「不也，世尊！何以故？須陀洹名為入流，而無所入；不入色、聲、香、味、觸、法，是名須陀洹。」「須菩提！於意云何？斯陀含能作是念：『我得斯陀含果』不？」須菩提言：「不也，世尊！何以故？斯陀含名一往來，而實無往來，是名斯陀含。

"Subhuti, what do you think? Can a stream-enterer have the thought 'I have attained the fruit of stream-entry'?" Subhuti said: "No, World-Honored One. Why? A stream-enterer is called 'one who has entered the stream,' yet there is nothing entered. One does not enter into form, sound, smell, taste, touch, or mental objects — this is what is called a stream-enterer." "Subhuti, what do you think? Can a once-returner have the thought 'I have attained the fruit of once-returning'?" Subhuti said: "No, World-Honored One. Why? A once-returner is called 'one who comes and goes once more,' yet in truth there is no coming or going — this is what is called a once-returner."
