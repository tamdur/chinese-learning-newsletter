般若波羅蜜多心經

來源：https://pages.ucsd.edu/~dkjordan/chin/chtxts/ShinJing.html

無苦集滅道，無智亦無得。以無所得故，菩提薩埵，依般若波羅蜜多故，心無罣礙。

There is no suffering, no cause of suffering, no cessation, no path; no wisdom and no attainment. With nothing to attain, a bodhisattva relies on Prajna Paramita, and the mind is without hindrance.

孟子

來源：https://ctext.org/mengzi

孟子曰：「所就三，所去三。迎之致敬以有禮，言將行其言也，則就之；禮貌未衰，言弗行也，則去之。其次，雖未行其言也，迎之致敬以有禮，則就之；禮貌衰，則去之。其下，朝不食，夕不食，飢餓不能出門戶。君聞之曰：『吾大者不能行其道，又不能從其言也，使飢餓於我土地，吾恥之。』周之，亦可受也，免死而已矣。」

Mencius said: 'There were three conditions for accepting office and three for leaving. First: if the ruler welcomes you with respect and due ceremony, indicating he intends to follow your counsel—then accept. If courtesies remain but your counsel goes unfollowed—then leave. Second: even if your counsel is not yet followed, if the ruler still welcomes you with respect and due ceremony—then accept. If courtesies decline—then leave. Third: if you have had neither breakfast nor supper and are so hungry you cannot leave your door, and the ruler learns of it and says, "I am ashamed that I cannot follow his Way or even heed his words, yet I let him starve in my domain"—then you may accept his relief. But only to escape death, nothing more.'

六祖大師法寶壇經

來源：六祖大師法寶壇經

神秀作偈成已，數度欲呈，行至堂前，心中恍惚，遍身汗流，擬呈不得；前後經四日，一十三度呈偈不得。秀乃思惟：『不如向廊下書著，從他和尚看見，忽若道好，即出禮拜，云是秀作；若道不堪，枉向山中數年，受人禮拜，更修何道？』是夜三更，不使人知，自執燈，書偈於南廊壁間，呈心所見。偈曰：
身是菩提樹，心如明鏡臺，時時勤拂拭，勿使惹塵埃。
「秀書偈了，便却歸房，人總不知。

After Shenxiu finished composing his verse, he tried to present it several times. Each time he walked to the hall, his mind became dazed, his whole body broke out in sweat, and he could not bring himself to submit it. Over four days, he attempted thirteen times without success. Then Shenxiu thought, 'It would be better to write it on the corridor wall and let the Master see it for himself. If he says it is good, I will come forward, bow, and say that I, Shenxiu, wrote it. If he says it is no good, then I have wasted years in these mountains receiving people's reverence for nothing — and what further cultivation should I pursue?' That night, at the third watch, telling no one, he took a lamp and wrote his verse on the wall of the south corridor, expressing what his mind had seen. The verse said: The body is the bodhi tree, / The mind is like a bright mirror's stand. / At all times diligently polish it — / Do not let it gather dust. After writing the verse, Shenxiu returned to his room, and no one knew.
