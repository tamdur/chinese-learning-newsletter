般若波羅蜜多心經

觀自在菩薩，行深般若波羅蜜多時，照見五蘊皆空，度一切苦厄。

Avalokitesvara Bodhisattva, when practicing the profound Prajna Paramita, illuminated the five aggregates and saw that they are all empty, thus overcoming all suffering and distress.

孟子：萬章上

passage 165 of 269

曰：「是詩也，非是之謂也；勞於王事，而不得養父母也。曰：『此莫非王事，我獨賢勞也。』故說《詩》者，不以文害辭，不以辭害志。以意逆志，是為得之。如以辭而已矣，《雲漢》之詩曰：『周餘黎民，靡有孑遺。』信斯言也，是周無遺民也。孝子之至，莫大乎尊親；尊親之至，莫大乎以天下養。為天子父，尊之至也；以天下養，養之至也。《詩》曰：『永言孝思，孝思維則。』此之謂也。《書》曰：『祗載見瞽瞍，夔夔齊栗，瞽瞍亦允若。』是為父不得而子也。」

Mencius said: "That poem does not mean what you think. It is about someone laboring at the king's business and being unable to care for his parents. He says: 'All of these are the king's affairs, yet I alone bear this heavy burden.' Therefore, in interpreting the Odes, one must not let the literal words distort the phrases, nor let the phrases distort the intended meaning. One should use one's own understanding to trace back to the author's intent — that is the way to grasp it. If one went only by the literal words, then the poem 'Yun Han' in the Odes says: 'Of the remaining multitudes of Zhou, not a single one survived.' If we believed this literally, it would mean that not a single person was left in Zhou. The highest expression of a filial son is nothing greater than honoring his parents. The highest expression of honoring one's parents is nothing greater than supporting them with the whole realm. To be the father of the Son of Heaven — that is the ultimate in honor. To be supported with the whole realm — that is the ultimate in support. The Odes say: 'Forever cherishing filial thoughts — those filial thoughts are a model for all.' This is what it refers to. The Documents say: 'Respectfully and reverently, Shun appeared before the Blind Man, full of awe and trembling — and the Blind Man also was brought to trust and comply.' This is what it means for a father not to be able to treat his son as merely a son."

六祖大師法寶壇經：宣詔品第九

passage 165 of 178

薛簡傳師指授如來知見，朕積善餘慶，宿種善根，值師出世，頓悟上乘。感荷師恩，頂戴無已，并奉磨衲袈裟及水晶鉢，勅韶州剌史修飾寺宇，賜師舊居為國恩寺。」。

'Xue Jian has conveyed the Master's teaching of the Tathagata's knowledge and vision. We have accumulated good fortune and blessings over past lives, planted wholesome roots, and have been fortunate to encounter the Master's appearance in the world, suddenly awakening to the Supreme Vehicle. Feeling deep gratitude for the Master's kindness, we reverently present a mona robe and kasaya along with a crystal bowl, and we command the prefect of Shaozhou to repair and adorn the monastery, bestowing the Master's former residence with the name "Guoen" — "Imperial Grace" — Temple.'
