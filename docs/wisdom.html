般若波羅蜜多心經 — 星期三

舍利子，是諸法空相，不生不滅，不垢不淨，不增不減。是故空中無色，無受想行識。

Shariputra, all dharmas are marked with emptiness — they are neither born nor destroyed, neither defiled nor pure, neither increasing nor decreasing. Therefore, in emptiness there is no form, no feelings, perceptions, mental formations, or consciousness.

孟子 · 梁惠王上

曰：「然則小固不可以敵大，寡固不可以敵眾，弱固不可以敵彊。海內之地方千里者九，齊集有其一。以一服八，何以異於鄒敵楚哉？蓋亦反其本矣。今王發政施仁，使天下仕者皆欲立於王之朝，耕者皆欲耕於王之野，商賈皆欲藏於王之市，行旅皆欲出於王之塗，天下之欲疾其君者皆欲赴愬於王。其若是，孰能禦之？」王曰：「吾惛，不能進於是矣。願夫子輔吾志，明以教我。我雖不敏，請嘗試之。」

Mencius said, 'So then, the small certainly cannot match the large, the few cannot match the many, and the weak cannot match the strong. Within the seas, the territories of a thousand li square number nine, and Qi possesses but one of them. To use one to subdue eight — how is this different from Zou fighting Chu? You must go back to the root of the matter. If now Your Majesty were to issue benevolent government, causing all the officials in the world to wish to serve in your court, all the farmers to wish to plow in your fields, all the merchants to wish to store their goods in your markets, all the travelers to wish to journey on your roads, and all those who are aggrieved by their own rulers to wish to bring their complaints to you — if it were truly so, who could withstand you?' The king said, 'I am dull and cannot advance to this. I hope you, Master, will support my resolve and instruct me clearly. Though I am not clever, let me try.'

金剛般若波羅蜜經 · 尊重正教分第十二

「復次，須菩提！隨說是經，乃至四句偈等，當知此處，一切世間，天、人、阿修羅，皆應供養，如佛塔廟。何況有人，盡能受持讀誦。須菩提！當知是人成就最上第一希有之法，若是經典所在之處，即為有佛，若尊重弟子。」

"Furthermore, Subhuti, wherever this sutra is spoken, even as few as four lines of verse, know that that place should be revered by all the world — by gods, humans, and asuras — as if it were a shrine of the Buddha. How much more so for one who can fully receive, uphold, read, and recite it. Subhuti, know that such a person achieves the supreme, foremost, and rarest of dharmas. Wherever this sutra is found, there the Buddha is present, and it should be honored as one would honor his revered disciples."
