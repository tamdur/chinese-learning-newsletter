般若波羅蜜多心經 — Heart Sutra — Tuesday

舍利子，色不異空，空不異色，色即是空，空即是色，受想行識，亦復如是。

Shariputra, form is not different from emptiness, emptiness is not different from form. Form is emptiness, emptiness is form. The same is true of feelings, perceptions, mental formations, and consciousness.

孟子 — 萬章下 — 第179段

孟子曰：「伯夷，聖之清者也；伊尹，聖之任者也；柳下惠，聖之和者也；孔子，聖之時者也。孔子之謂集大成。集大成也者，金聲而玉振之也。金聲也者，始條理也；玉振之也者，終條理也。始條理者，智之事也；終條理者，聖之事也。智，譬則巧也；聖，譬則力也。由射於百步之外也，其至，爾力也；其中，非爾力也。」北宮錡問曰：「周室班爵祿也，如之何？」

Mencius said: "Bo Yi was the sage of purity; Yi Yin was the sage of responsibility; Liu Xia Hui was the sage of harmony; Confucius was the sage of timeliness. Confucius is what we call the complete concert. The complete concert is one in which the bells initiate and the jade chimes bring it to conclusion. The sound of the bells initiates orderly sequence; the resonance of the jade chimes concludes orderly sequence. Initiating orderly sequence is the work of wisdom; concluding orderly sequence is the work of sageness. Wisdom may be compared to skill; sageness may be compared to strength. It is like shooting from beyond a hundred paces: reaching the target depends on your strength, but hitting the mark does not depend on your strength alone." Beigong Qi asked: "How did the House of Zhou arrange its ranks and emoluments?"

信心銘 — Faith in Mind — 第1段

前空轉變皆由妄見不用求真唯須息見二見不住慎勿追尋纔有是非紛然失心二由一有一亦莫守一心不生萬法無咎無咎無法不生不心能隨境滅境逐能沉境由能境能由境能欲知兩段元是一空一空同兩齊含萬象不見精麤寧有偏黨大道體寬無易無難小見狐疑轉急轉遲執之失度必入邪路放之自然體無去住任性合道逍遙絕惱繫念乖真昏沉不好

That prior emptiness was transformed —
all from deluded views.
No need to seek the true;
simply cease to cherish opinions.

Do not dwell in dualistic views;
be careful not to pursue them.
The moment right and wrong arise,
the mind is lost in confusion.

Two comes from one,
yet do not cling even to the one.
When the one mind does not arise,
the myriad things are without fault.

No fault, no things;
no arising, no mind.
The subject dissolves with its object;
the object sinks with its subject.

Object exists because of subject;
subject exists because of object.
If you wish to know both sides,
know they are originally one emptiness.

In one emptiness both are the same,
each containing the myriad forms.
Do not distinguish coarse from fine,
and how can bias arise?

The Great Way is vast in substance,
neither easy nor difficult.
Small views breed doubt;
the more you hurry, the more you fall behind.

Cling to it and you lose measure,
surely entering a crooked path.
Let it go naturally,
and its essence neither departs nor abides.

Surrender to your nature and accord with the Way,
wandering free, beyond vexation.
Bound-up thinking strays from the real;
sinking into dullness is equally no good.
