般若波羅蜜多心經 — Friday

無苦集滅道，無智亦無得。以無所得故，菩提薩埵，依般若波羅蜜多故，心無罣礙。

There is no suffering, no cause of suffering, no cessation, no path; no wisdom and no attainment. With nothing to attain, a bodhisattva relies on Prajna Paramita, and the mind is without hindrance.

孟子 — 萬章上

孟子曰：「否，不然。好事者為之也。百里奚，虞人也。晉人以垂棘之璧與屈產之乘，假道於虞以伐虢。宮之奇諫，百里奚不諫。知虞公之不可諫而去，之秦，年已七十矣，曾不知以食牛干秦穆公之為汙也，可謂智乎？不可諫而不諫，可謂不智乎？知虞公之將亡而先去之，不可謂不智也。時舉於秦，知穆公之可與有行也而相之，可謂不智乎？相秦而顯其君於天下，可傳於後世，不賢而能之乎？自鬻以成其君，鄉黨自好者不為，而謂賢者為之乎？」

Mencius said: "No, that is not so. This is the fabrication of meddlesome people. Baili Xi was a man of the state of Yu. The state of Jin offered the Chuiji jade disc and horses from Qu to borrow passage through Yu in order to attack Guo. Gong Zhi Qi remonstrated against granting passage, but Baili Xi did not remonstrate. He knew the Duke of Yu could not be remonstrated with, so he left and went to Qin. He was already seventy years old. If he still did not know that feeding cattle to seek an audience with Duke Mu of Qin would be degrading — could he be called wise? Yet by not remonstrating when remonstrance was impossible, can he be called unwise? He knew that the Duke of Yu was going to be destroyed and left beforehand — he cannot be called unwise. When he was elevated in Qin, he knew that Duke Mu was worthy of working with and served as his minister — can that be called unwise? As minister of Qin, he made his lord illustrious throughout the realm, worthy of being remembered by later generations — could someone without talent have done this? To sell oneself in order to advance one's lord — even a self-respecting villager would not do such a thing. Would you say that a truly worthy man would?"

六祖大師法寶壇經 — 付囑品第十

能善分別相，第一義不動，但作如此見，即是真如用。報諸學道人，努力須用意，莫於大乘門，却執生死智。若言下相應，即共論佛義；若實不相應，合掌令歡喜。此宗本無諍，諍即失道意，執逆諍法門，自性入生死。

師復曰：「汝等好住。吾滅度後，莫作世情悲泣雨淚，受人弔問，身著孝服，非吾弟子，亦非正法。但識自本心，見自本性，無動無靜，無生無滅，無去無來，無是無非，無住無往。恐汝等心迷，不會吾意，今再囑汝，令汝見性。

"Be skilled in discerning characteristics, while the First Principle remains unmoving. If you can see things in this way, this is the function of true suchness. I say to all who study the Way: make diligent effort and pay attention. Do not, at the gate of the Great Vehicle, cling to the wisdom of birth-and-death. If there is accord at the very moment of speaking, then discuss together the meaning of the Buddha's teaching. If there is truly no accord, then join palms and let there be good will. This school is fundamentally without contention; contention means losing the meaning of the Way. Clinging to disputatious approaches to the Dharma, the self-nature falls into birth-and-death." The Master further said: 'Take good care of yourselves. After my passing, do not follow worldly customs by weeping and shedding tears, receiving condolences, or wearing mourning garments — that would be unworthy of my disciples and contrary to the true Dharma. Simply recognize your own original mind and see your own original nature — without movement or stillness, without arising or ceasing, without going or coming, without right or wrong, without abiding or departing. I fear your minds may be confused and you may not understand my meaning, so I admonish you once more to help you see your nature.'
