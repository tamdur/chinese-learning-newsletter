般若波羅蜜多心經 — 星期三

舍利子，是諸法空相，不生不滅，不垢不淨，不增不減。是故空中無色，無受想行識。

Shariputra, all dharmas are marked with emptiness — they are neither born nor destroyed, neither defiled nor pure, neither increasing nor decreasing. Therefore, in emptiness there is no form, no feelings, perceptions, mental formations, or consciousness.

孟子 — 萬章下

孟子曰：「其詳不可得聞也。諸侯惡其害己也，而皆去其籍。然而軻也，嘗聞其略也。天子一位，公一位，侯一位，伯一位，子、男同一位，凡五等也。君一位，卿一位，大夫一位，上士一位，中士一位，下士一位，凡六等。「天子之制，地方千里，公侯皆方百里，伯七十里，子、男五十里，凡四等。不能五十里，不達於天子，附於諸侯，曰附庸。天子之卿受地視侯，大夫受地視伯，元士受地視子、男。

Mencius said: "The full details cannot be learned, for the feudal lords, finding the system harmful to themselves, all destroyed the records. However, I, Ke, have heard its general outline. The Son of Heaven held one rank, dukes one rank, marquises one rank, earls one rank, and viscounts and barons shared one rank — five ranks in all. The ruler held one rank, ministers one rank, grand officers one rank, senior scholars one rank, middle scholars one rank, and junior scholars one rank — six ranks in all. The Son of Heaven's domain was a thousand li square. Dukes and marquises each had a hundred li square, earls seventy li, viscounts and barons fifty li — four grades in all. Those who could not reach fifty li were not granted direct access to the Son of Heaven and were attached to a feudal lord; they were called dependencies. The Son of Heaven's ministers received land equivalent to a marquis, grand officers equivalent to an earl, and chief scholars equivalent to a viscount or baron."

信心銘

不好勞神何用疏親欲取一乘勿惡六塵六塵不惡還同正覺智者無為愚人自縛法無異法妄自愛著將心用心豈非大錯迷生寂亂悟無好惡一切二邊良由斟酌夢幻空華何勞把捉得失是非一時放卻眼若不眠諸夢自除心若不異萬法一如一如體玄兀爾忘緣萬法齊觀歸復自然泯其所以不可方比止動無動動止無止兩既不成一何有爾究竟窮極不存軌則

It is no good to weary the spirit — what use is there in estrangement or intimacy? If you wish to travel the One Vehicle, do not despise the six sense-objects. The six sense-objects, not despised, become identical with true awakening. The wise act without contrivance; the foolish bind themselves. Dharma has no separate dharmas; delusion creates its own attachments. Using the mind to grasp the mind — is this not the great mistake? From confusion arise stillness and turmoil; in awakening there is no good or bad. All dualities come from deliberate thinking. Dreams, illusions, flowers in the sky — why trouble yourself to grasp them? Gain and loss, right and wrong — let them all go at once. If the eyes never sleep, all dreams naturally cease. If the mind makes no distinctions, the myriad things are one suchness. One suchness is profound in its essence, unmoving, free from conditions. Contemplate the myriad things as equal and you return to your natural state. When their reasons are dissolved, no comparisons can be made. Stop movement and there is no movement; set movement going and there is no stillness. When both cease to exist, how can the one stand alone? Pursue this to the ultimate end: no rules or measures remain.
