般若波羅蜜多心經

般若波羅蜜多心經（Monday）

觀自在菩薩，行深般若波羅蜜多時，照見五蘊皆空，度一切苦厄。

Avalokitesvara Bodhisattva, when practicing the profound Prajna Paramita, illuminated the five aggregates and saw that they are all empty, thus overcoming all suffering and distress.

孟子

孟子・告子上

孟子曰：「魚，我所欲也；熊掌，亦我所欲也，二者不可得兼，舍魚而取熊掌者也。生，亦我所欲也；義，亦我所欲也，二者不可得兼，舍生而取義者也。生亦我所欲，所欲有甚於生者，故不為苟得也；死亦我所惡，所惡有甚於死者，故患有所不辟也。如使人之所欲莫甚於生，則凡可以得生者，何不用也？使人之所惡莫甚於死者，則凡可以辟患者，何不為也？由是則生而有不用也，由是則可以辟患而有不為也。是故所欲有甚於生者，所惡有甚於死者，非獨賢者有是心也，人皆有之，賢者能勿喪耳。一簞食，一豆羹，得之則生，弗得則死。嘑爾而與之，行道之人弗受；蹴爾而與之，乞人不屑也。萬鍾則不辨禮義而受之。萬鍾於我何加焉？為宮室之美、妻妾之奉、所識窮乏者得我與？鄉為身死而不受，今為宮室之美為之；鄉為身死而不受，今為妻妾之奉為之；鄉為身死而不受，今為所識窮乏者得我而為之，是亦不可以已乎？此之謂失其本心。」

Mencius said: "Fish is something I desire; bear's paw is also something I desire. If I cannot have both, I will give up fish and take bear's paw. Life is something I desire; righteousness is also something I desire. If I cannot have both, I will give up life and take righteousness. Life is indeed something I desire, but there are things I desire more than life, so I will not do anything to get it by improper means. Death is indeed something I detest, but there are things I detest more than death, so there are dangers I will not avoid. If there were nothing people desired more than life, then why would they not use every possible means to survive? If there were nothing people detested more than death, then why would they not do anything to avoid danger? Yet there are means of survival that people will not use, and ways of avoiding danger that people will not take. This shows that there are things people desire more than life, and things they detest more than death. It is not only the worthy who have this heart—all people have it. The worthy are simply those who do not lose it. A single basket of rice, a single bowl of soup—get them and you live, go without and you die. But if they are offered with a contemptuous shout, even a passerby will refuse them. If they are offered with a kick, even a beggar will scorn them. Yet a salary of ten thousand bushels some people accept without regard for propriety and righteousness. What does ten thousand bushels add to me? Is it for the beauty of mansions, the service of wives and concubines, or the gratitude of acquaintances in need? Before, one would have died rather than accept, but now one accepts for the beauty of mansions. Before, one would have died rather than accept, but now one accepts for the service of wives and concubines. Before, one would have died rather than accept, but now one accepts for the gratitude of poor acquaintances. Can this not be stopped? This is called losing one's original heart."

Subhuti, what do you think? When the Tathagata was with Dipankara Buddha, was there any dharma through which he attained anuttara-samyak-sambodhi? No, World-Honored One! As I understand the meaning of what the Buddha has said, when the Buddha was with Dipankara Buddha, there was no dharma through which he attained anuttara-samyak-sambodhi. The Buddha said: So it is, so it is! Subhuti, there is in reality no dharma through which the Tathagata attained anuttara-samyak-sambodhi. Subhuti, if there were any dharma through which the Tathagata attained anuttara-samyak-sambodhi, then Dipankara Buddha would not have given me the prediction: 'In a future life, you shall become a buddha named Shakyamuni.' It is precisely because there is no dharma through which one attains anuttara-samyak-sambodhi that Dipankara Buddha gave me the prediction, saying: 'In a future life, you shall become a buddha named Shakyamuni.'
