般若波羅蜜多心經

舍利子，是諸法空相，不生不滅，不垢不淨，不增不減。是故空中無色，無受想行識。

Shariputra, all dharmas are marked with emptiness — they are neither born nor destroyed, neither defiled nor pure, neither increasing nor decreasing. Therefore, in emptiness there is no form, no feelings, perceptions, mental formations, or consciousness.

孟子・滕文公下

孟子曰：「予豈好辯哉？予不得已也。天下之生久矣，一治一亂。當堯之時，水逆行，氾濫於中國。蛇龍居之，民無所定。下者為巢，上者為營窟。《書》曰：『洚水警余。』洚水者，洪水也。使禹治之，禹掘地而注之海，驅蛇龍而放之菹。水由地中行，江、淮、河、漢是也。險阻既遠，鳥獸之害人者消，然後人得平土而居之。

Mencius said: "How could I be fond of disputation? I have no choice. The world has existed for a long time, alternating between order and chaos. In the time of Yao, the waters flowed backward and flooded the Central Kingdom. Snakes and dragons made their homes there, and the people had nowhere to settle. Those on low ground built nests in trees; those on high ground dug burrows in the hillsides. The Book of Documents says: 'The floodwaters warn me.' The floodwaters were the great flood. Yu was sent to control them. He dug channels to drain the water into the sea and drove the snakes and dragons into the marshes. The water flowed through the channels in the earth — the Yangtze, the Huai, the Yellow River, and the Han. Once the dangers were removed and the birds and beasts that had plagued the people were gone, people were at last able to live on level ground.

六祖大師法寶壇經・懺悔品第六

向者三身佛，在自性中，世人總有；為自心迷，不見內性。外覓三身如來，不見自身中有三身佛。汝等聽說，令汝等於自身中，見自性有三身佛。此三身佛，從自性生，不從外得。何名清淨法身佛？世人性本清淨，萬法從自性生。思量一切惡事，即生惡行；思量一切善事，即生善行。如是諸法在自性中，如天常清，日月常明，為浮雲蓋覆，上明下暗。忽遇風吹雲散，上下俱明，萬象皆現。世人性常浮游，如彼天雲。

The three-body Buddha mentioned above is within your own self-nature. Everyone in the world possesses it, but because their own minds are deluded, they do not see their inner nature. They seek the three-body Tathagata outside themselves and do not see that the three-body Buddha is within their own bodies. Listen to my explanation, which will enable you to see within your own bodies that your self-nature possesses the three-body Buddha. These three-body Buddhas arise from self-nature, not from external seeking. What is meant by the pure Dharma-body Buddha? The nature of all people in this world is originally pure, and the myriad dharmas are born from self-nature. Thinking about all manner of evil gives rise to evil conduct; thinking about all manner of good gives rise to good conduct. Thus all dharmas exist within self-nature, like the sky that is always clear and the sun and moon that always shine. When floating clouds cover them, it is bright above and dark below. But when a wind suddenly blows the clouds away, above and below are both bright and the myriad forms all appear. The nature of people in this world constantly drifts and floats, like those clouds in the sky.
