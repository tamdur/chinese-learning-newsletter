般若波羅蜜多心經

https://pages.ucsd.edu/~dkjordan/chin/chtxts/ShinJing.html

舍利子，色不異空，空不異色，色即是空，空即是色，受想行識，亦復如是。

Shariputra, form is not different from emptiness, emptiness is not different from form. Form is emptiness, emptiness is form. The same is true of feelings, perceptions, mental formations, and consciousness.

孟子 — 告子上

公都子不能答，以告孟子。孟子曰：「敬叔父乎？敬弟乎？彼將曰『敬叔父』。曰：『弟為尸，則誰敬？』彼將曰『敬弟。』子曰：『惡在其敬叔父也？』彼將曰『在位故也。』子亦曰：『在位故也。庸敬在兄，斯須之敬在鄉人。』」季子聞之曰：「敬叔父則敬，敬弟則敬，果在外，非由內也。」公都子曰：「冬日則飲湯，夏日則飲水，然則飲食亦在外也？」

Gongduzi could not answer, and reported this to Mencius. Mencius said: "Ask him: 'Do you respect your uncle, or your younger brother?' He will say, 'My uncle.' Then say: 'If your younger brother is serving as the ceremonial impersonator of the dead, then whom do you respect?' He will say, 'My younger brother.' Then you ask: 'What happened to your respect for your uncle?' He will say: 'It is because of the position my brother holds.' Then you can likewise say: 'It is because of the position the villager holds. Ordinary respect belongs to the elder brother; temporary, situational respect goes to the villager.'" When Jizi heard this, he said: "When I respect my uncle, it is respect; when I respect my younger brother, it is also respect. So it really is determined by external circumstances, not from within." Gongduzi said: "In winter we drink hot water; in summer we drink cold water. Then is eating and drinking also determined by external circumstances?"

金剛般若波羅蜜經 — 離相寂滅分第十四

何以故？我於往昔，節節支解時，若有我相、人相、眾生相、壽者相，應生瞋恨。須菩提！又念過去，於五百世，作忍辱仙人，於爾所世，無我相、無人相、無眾生相、無壽者相。是故須菩提！菩薩應離一切相，發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心。不應住色生心，不應住聲、香、味、觸、法生心，應生無所住心。若心有住，則為非住。」「是故佛說：菩薩心不應住色布施。須菩提！菩薩為利益一切眾生故，應如是布施。如來說：一切諸相，即是非相。

Why? In the past, when my body was being dismembered limb by limb, if I had held the marks of self, person, sentient being, or life span, I would have given rise to anger and hatred. Subhuti, I also recall that in the past, for five hundred lifetimes, I was the ascetic known as the Patient One. During all those lifetimes, I was free of the mark of self, the mark of person, the mark of sentient being, and the mark of life span. Therefore, Subhuti, a bodhisattva should leave behind all marks and give rise to the aspiration for anuttara-samyak-sambodhi. One should not give rise to a mind that dwells on form, nor should one give rise to a mind that dwells on sound, smell, taste, touch, or dharmas. One should give rise to a mind that dwells on nothing. If the mind dwells on anything, it does not truly dwell. Therefore the Buddha says: a bodhisattva's mind should not dwell on form when practicing generosity. Subhuti, a bodhisattva, for the benefit of all sentient beings, should practice generosity in this way. The Tathagata says: all marks are not marks.
