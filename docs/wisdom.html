般若波羅蜜多心經

故知般若波羅蜜多，是大神咒，是大明咒，是無上咒，是無等等咒，能除一切苦，真實不虛。故說般若波羅蜜多咒，即說咒曰：揭諦揭諦，波羅揭諦，波羅僧揭諦，菩提薩婆訶。

Therefore, know that Prajna Paramita is the great transcendent mantra, the great bright mantra, the supreme mantra, the unequalled mantra, which can remove all suffering and is true, not false. Therefore he proclaimed the Prajna Paramita mantra, saying: Gate gate paragate parasamgate bodhi svaha.

孟子 · 離婁上

孟子曰：「規矩，方員之至也；聖人，人倫之至也。欲為君盡君道，欲為臣盡臣道，二者皆法堯舜而已矣。不以舜之所以事堯事君，不敬其君者也；不以堯之所以治民治民，賊其民者也。孔子曰：『道二：仁與不仁而已矣。』暴其民甚，則身弒國亡；不甚，則身危國削。名之曰『幽厲』，雖孝子慈孫，百世不能改也。《詩》云『殷鑒不遠，在夏后之世』，此之謂也。」

Mencius said: "The compass and square are the ultimate standard for circles and squares; the sage is the ultimate standard for human relationships. To be a ruler, one must fulfill the Way of a ruler; to be a minister, one must fulfill the Way of a minister—in both cases, one need only take Yao and Shun as models. Not to serve one's ruler as Shun served Yao is to disrespect one's ruler; not to govern the people as Yao governed the people is to rob one's people. Confucius said: 'There are only two paths: benevolence and the lack of benevolence.' A ruler who is excessively cruel to his people will be assassinated and his state will perish; if he is not excessively cruel, he will still be endangered and his state will be diminished. He will be given a posthumous name like 'Dark' or 'Cruel,' and even his filial sons and loving grandsons will not be able to change it for a hundred generations. The Odes say: 'The mirror of Yin is not far off—it lies in the age of the Xia dynasty's heirs.' This is what it means."

六祖大師法寶壇經

分為四門，開覺知見、示覺知見、悟覺知見、入覺知見。若聞開示，便能悟入，即覺知見，本來真性而得出現。汝慎勿錯解經意，見他道：『開示悟入，自是佛之知見。我輩無分。』若作此解，乃是謗經毀佛也。彼既是佛，已具知見，何用更開？汝今當信，佛知見者，只汝自心，更無別佛。蓋為一切眾生，自蔽光明，貪愛塵境，外緣內擾，甘受驅馳。便勞他世尊，從三昧起，種種苦口，勸令寢息，莫向外求，與佛無二。故云：『開佛知見。

'It is divided into four aspects: opening awakened insight, revealing awakened insight, realizing awakened insight, and entering awakened insight. If upon hearing the opening and revealing, one can realize and enter, then awakened insight — one's original true nature — manifests. You must be careful not to misinterpret the sutra's meaning and think: "Opening, revealing, realizing, and entering belong only to the Buddha's knowledge and insight — we have no share in it." To hold such a view is to slander the sutra and defame the Buddha. Since the Buddha already possesses knowledge and insight, what need would there be to open it further? You should now have faith that the Buddha's knowledge and insight is nothing other than your own mind — there is no other Buddha. It is because all sentient beings veil their own luminosity, crave and cling to the sense-objects, and are disturbed from within by external conditions, that they willingly submit to being driven about. This is what compelled the World-Honored One to rise from samadhi and, with all manner of earnest exhortation, urge them to rest and be still, to stop seeking outside themselves, and to realize they are no different from the Buddha. Hence it is said: "Opening the Buddha's knowledge and insight."'
