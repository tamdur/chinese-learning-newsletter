般若波羅蜜多心經

無罣礙故，無有恐怖，遠離顛倒夢想，究竟涅槃。三世諸佛，依般若波羅蜜多故，得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。

Without hindrance, there is no fear. Far from all inverted views, one attains nirvana. All buddhas of past, present, and future rely on Prajna Paramita and attain supreme, perfect enlightenment.

孟子 · 離婁上

「故曰，城郭不完，兵甲不多，非國之災也；田野不辟，貨財不聚，非國之害也。上無禮，下無學，賊民興，喪無日矣。《詩》曰：『天之方蹶，無然泄泄。』泄泄，猶沓沓也。事君無義，進退無禮，言則非先王之道者，猶沓沓也。故曰：責難於君謂之恭，陳善閉邪謂之敬，吾君不能謂之賊。」

"Therefore it is said: incomplete city walls and a shortage of arms and armor are not the disaster of a state; uncultivated fields and ungathered wealth are not the harm of a state. But when those above lack ritual propriety and those below lack learning, rebellious people will arise, and the state's destruction will come any day. The Odes say: 'Heaven is about to shake things up—do not be so complacent!' Complacent means idle and careless. To serve one's ruler without righteousness, to advance and retire without propriety, and to speak while rejecting the Way of the former kings—that is idle and careless. Therefore it is said: to hold one's ruler to a high standard is called respect; to present what is good and block what is evil is called reverence; to say 'my ruler is incapable' is called being a thief to him."

'With what do you take as the essential theme when you recite this sutra?' Fada replied: 'This student's capacity is dull and dim. I have always just followed the text and recited aloud — how would I know its essential purport?' The Master said: 'I cannot read, but if you try reciting it once for me, I will explain it to you.' Fada then recited the sutra in a loud voice. When he reached the Parables chapter, the Master said: 'Stop! This sutra takes as its central theme the causes and conditions of the Buddha's appearance in the world. No matter how many parables are offered, none go beyond this. What are these causes and conditions? The sutra says: "All the Buddhas, the World-Honored Ones, appear in the world for the sake of one great matter of causes and conditions." This one great matter is the Buddha's knowledge and insight. Worldly people are outwardly deluded by attachment to forms, and inwardly deluded by attachment to emptiness. If one can be free from forms while among forms, and free from emptiness while in emptiness, then one is deluded neither inwardly nor outwardly. If you awaken to this Dharma, in a single thought the mind opens — this is called "opening the Buddha's knowledge and insight." Buddha simply means awakening.'
