般若波羅蜜多心經

心經

舍利子，是諸法空相，不生不滅，不垢不淨，不增不減。是故空中無色，無受想行識。

Shariputra, all dharmas are marked with emptiness — they are neither born nor destroyed, neither defiled nor pure, neither increasing nor decreasing. Therefore, in emptiness there is no form, no feelings, perceptions, mental formations, or consciousness.

孟子 · 告子上

孟子

公都子曰：「告子曰：『性無善無不善也。』或曰：『性可以為善，可以為不善；是故文武興，則民好善；幽厲興，則民好暴。』或曰：『有性善，有性不善；是故以堯為君而有象，以瞽瞍為父而有舜；以紂為兄之子且以為君，而有微子啟、王子比干。』今曰『性善』，然則彼皆非與？」

Gongduzi said: "Gaozi says: 'Human nature is neither good nor not good.' Others say: 'Human nature can be made good or not good. That is why when Kings Wen and Wu arose, the people were inclined toward goodness; when Kings You and Li arose, the people were inclined toward cruelty.' Still others say: 'Some natures are good and some are not good. That is why even with Yao as sovereign there was Xiang; even with the Blind Man as father there was Shun; and even with the tyrant Zhou as nephew and sovereign, there were Viscount Wei of Qi and Prince Bigan.' Now you say human nature is good—are all these others wrong?"

金剛般若波羅蜜經 · 離相寂滅分第十四

金剛般若波羅蜜經

又說：一切眾生，即非眾生。須菩提！如來是真語者、實語者、如語者、不誑語者、不異語者。」「須菩提！如來所得法，此法無實無虛。須菩提！若菩薩心住於法而行布施，如人入闇，則無所見；若菩薩心不住法而行布施，如人有目，日光明照，見種種色。」「須菩提！當來之世，若有善男子、善女人，能於此經，受持讀誦，則為如來，以佛智慧，悉知是人，悉見是人，皆得成就無量無邊功德。」。

And further says: all sentient beings are not sentient beings. Subhuti, the Tathagata is one who speaks truth, speaks reality, speaks things as they are, does not speak deceptively, and does not speak inconsistently. Subhuti, the dharma the Tathagata has attained is neither real nor unreal. Subhuti, if a bodhisattva practices generosity while the mind dwells on dharmas, it is like a person entering darkness — they cannot see anything. If a bodhisattva practices generosity while the mind does not dwell on dharmas, it is like a person with eyes in bright sunlight — they can see all manner of forms. Subhuti, in ages to come, if a good man or good woman is able to receive, uphold, read, and recite this sutra, the Tathagata, with his buddha-wisdom, will fully know and fully see that such a person will achieve immeasurable and boundless merit.
