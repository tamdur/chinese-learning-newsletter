般若波羅蜜多心經

舍利子，是諸法空相，不生不滅，不垢不淨，不增不減。是故空中無色，無受想行識。

Shariputra, all dharmas are marked with emptiness — they are neither born nor destroyed, neither defiled nor pure, neither increasing nor decreasing. Therefore, in emptiness there is no form, no feelings, perceptions, mental formations, or consciousness.

孟子·離婁上

孟子曰：「離婁之明，公輸子之巧，不以規矩，不能成方員：師曠之聰，不以六律，不能正五音；堯舜之道，不以仁政，不能平治天下。今有仁心仁聞而民不被其澤，不可法於後世者，不行先王之道也。

Mencius said: "Even with the sharp eyes of Li Lou and the skill of Gongshu Ban, without compass and square one cannot make perfect circles and squares. Even with the keen ears of Music Master Kuang, without the six pitch-pipes one cannot tune the five notes correctly. Even with the Way of Yao and Shun, without benevolent government one cannot bring order and peace to the realm. Now there are those who have benevolent hearts and benevolent reputations, yet the people do not receive their blessings, and they cannot serve as models for later generations—this is because they do not put into practice the Way of the former kings.

六祖大師法寶壇經·機緣品第七

俄成寶坊，師住九月餘日，又為惡黨尋逐，師乃遯于前山。被其縱火焚草木，師隱身挨入石中得免。石今有師趺坐膝痕，及衣布之紋，因名避難石。師憶五祖懷會止藏之囑，遂行隱于二邑焉。

僧法海，韶州曲江人也。初參祖師，問曰：「即心即佛，願垂指諭。」師曰：「前念不生即心，後念不滅即佛；成一切相即心，離一切相即佛。吾若具說，窮劫不盡。聽吾偈曰：即心名慧，即佛乃定，定慧等持，意中清淨。

Soon the place became a precious sanctuary. After the Master had resided there for just over nine months, he was again pursued by hostile parties. The Master fled into the mountains ahead of them. When they set fire to the grass and trees, the Master hid by pressing himself into a rock crevice and was spared. The rock still bears the imprint of the Master's crossed knees and the texture of his robe's cloth, and so it is called the Refuge Rock. Remembering the Fifth Patriarch's instruction to 'stop and hide at Huai,' the Master went into hiding between the two districts. The monk Fahai, a native of Qujiang in Shaozhou, first came to visit the Patriarch and asked: 'What is meant by "mind itself is Buddha"? I humbly request your instruction.' The Master said: 'When the preceding thought does not arise, that is mind. When the succeeding thought does not cease, that is Buddha. Giving rise to all forms is mind; being free from all forms is Buddha. If I were to explain this fully, I could not exhaust it even in an entire kalpa. Listen to my verse: Mind itself is called wisdom; Buddha itself is samadhi. When samadhi and wisdom are maintained equally, the mind becomes pure and clear.'
