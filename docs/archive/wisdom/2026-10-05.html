般若波羅蜜多心經 — 星期一

觀自在菩薩，行深般若波羅蜜多時，照見五蘊皆空，度一切苦厄。

Avalokitesvara Bodhisattva, when practicing the profound Prajna Paramita, illuminated the five aggregates and saw that they are all empty, thus overcoming all suffering and distress.

孟子 — 萬章下

「柳下惠，不羞汙君，不辭小官。進不隱賢，必以其道。遺佚而不怨，阨窮而不憫。與鄉人處，由由然不忍去也。『爾為爾，我為我，雖袒裼裸裎於我側，爾焉能浼我哉？』故聞柳下惠之風者，鄙夫寬，薄夫敦。「孔子之去齊，接淅而行；去魯，曰：『遲遲吾行也。』去父母國之道也。可以速而速，可以久而久，可以處而處，可以仕而仕，孔子也。」

"Liu Xia Hui was not ashamed to serve a corrupt ruler, nor did he consider any office too lowly. When he advanced in office, he did not hide his worthiness, and always acted according to his principles. When overlooked and passed over, he did not resent it; when impoverished and in dire straits, he did not feel sorry for himself. When among villagers, he was so easy-going and content that he could not bear to leave. He said: 'You are you and I am I. Even if you stood stripped naked at my side, how could you defile me?' Therefore, those who heard of Liu Xia Hui's example — the narrow-minded became tolerant, and the mean-spirited became generous. Confucius, when leaving Qi, grabbed the half-washed rice and departed at once. When leaving Lu, he said: 'Slowly, slowly shall I go' — this was the proper way to leave one's fatherland. When it was right to leave quickly, he left quickly; when it was right to stay long, he stayed long; when it was right to remain in retirement, he remained; when it was right to serve in office, he served. That was Confucius."

信心銘

至道無難唯嫌揀擇但莫憎愛洞然明白毫釐有差天地懸隔欲得現前莫存順逆違順相爭是為心病不識玄旨徒勞念靜圓同太虛無欠無餘良由取捨所以不如莫逐有緣勿住空忍一種平懷泯然自盡止動歸止止更彌動唯滯兩邊寧知一種一種不通兩處失功遣有沒有從空背空多言多慮轉不相應絕言絕慮無處不通歸根得旨隨照失宗須臾返照勝卻前空

The Great Way is not difficult — it only avoids picking and choosing. Simply do not hate or love, and it will be thoroughly clear. A hair's breadth of difference, and heaven and earth are set apart. If you wish it to appear before you, do not hold to for or against. Conflict between for and against — this is the mind's disease. Without recognizing the profound principle, you toil in vain to quiet your thoughts. Perfect like great emptiness, with nothing lacking, nothing in excess. Only because of grasping and rejecting do we fail to see its suchness. Do not chase after entanglements, do not dwell in the patience of emptiness. In oneness and equanimity of heart, all things dissolve of themselves. When you stop movement to return to stillness, that stopping only creates more agitation. If you linger in either extreme, how will you ever know oneness? Without penetrating oneness, both paths lose their function. Banish existence and you drown in it; follow emptiness and you turn your back on it. Excessive words, excessive thinking — only take you further from accordance. Cut off words, cut off thinking, and there is nowhere you cannot penetrate. Return to the root and grasp the meaning; chase after appearances and lose the source. One moment of inward reflection surpasses the emptiness that came before.
