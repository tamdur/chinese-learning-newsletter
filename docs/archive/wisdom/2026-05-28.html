般若波羅蜜多心經 — Heart Sutra — Thursday

無眼耳鼻舌身意，無色聲香味觸法，無眼界，乃至無意識界。無無明，亦無無明盡，乃至無老死，亦無老死盡。

There are no eyes, ears, nose, tongue, body, or mind; no sights, sounds, smells, tastes, touches, or objects of mind; no realm of sight, and so on up to no realm of consciousness. There is no ignorance and no end of ignorance, and so on up to no old age and death and no end of old age and death.

孟子 — 公孫丑上

孟子曰：「仁則榮，不仁則辱。今惡辱而居不仁，是猶惡溼而居下也。如惡之，莫如貴德而尊士，賢者在位，能者在職。國家閒暇，及是時明其政刑。雖大國，必畏之矣。《詩》云：『迨天之未陰雨，徹彼桑土，綢繆牖戶。今此下民，或敢侮予？』孔子曰：『為此詩者，其知道乎！能治其國家，誰敢侮之？』今國家閒暇，及是時般樂怠敖，是自求禍也。禍褔無不自己求之者。《詩》云：『永言配命，自求多褔。』《太甲》曰：『天作孽，猶可違；自作孽，不可活。』此之謂也。」

Mencius said: "Benevolence brings glory; the lack of benevolence brings disgrace. To hate disgrace while dwelling in unbenevolence is like hating dampness while living in a low-lying place. If one truly hates it, the best course is to honor virtue and respect the worthy: place the virtuous in positions of authority and the capable in office. When the state is at peace, use that time to clarify its governance and laws. Then even great states will stand in awe. The Odes say: 'Before the sky clouds over with rain, I strip the bark from mulberry roots and bind tight my windows and doors. Now you people below—who would dare insult me?' Confucius said: 'Whoever composed this poem truly understood the Way! If one can govern his state well, who would dare insult him?' But now, when states are at peace, rulers use the time for idle pleasure and lazy indulgence—this is to invite disaster upon themselves. Misfortune and fortune are never anything but what one brings upon oneself. The Odes say: 'Ever strive to accord with Heaven's mandate, and seek for yourself abundant blessings.' The Taijia says: 'When Heaven sends down calamities, one may still escape them; when one brings calamity upon oneself, there is no surviving it.' This is what it means."
