般若波羅蜜多心經

無罣礙故，無有恐怖，遠離顛倒夢想，究竟涅槃。三世諸佛，依般若波羅蜜多故，得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。

Without hindrance, there is no fear. Far from all inverted views, one attains nirvana. All buddhas of past, present, and future rely on Prajna Paramita and attain supreme, perfect enlightenment.

孟子 · 滕文公下

陳代曰：「不見諸侯，宜若小然；今一見之，大則以王，小則以霸。且志曰：『枉尺而直尋』，宜若可為也。」

Chen Dai said: "Not going to see the feudal lords seems like a small thing to insist on. Yet if you were to see them just once, at best you could help them become a true king, and at least help them become a hegemon. Moreover, the records say: 'Bend one foot to straighten eight feet' — that seems worth doing."

六祖大師法寶壇經 · 坐禪品第五

師示眾云：「此門坐禪，元不著心，亦不著淨，亦不是不動。若言著心，心元是妄，知心如幻，故無所著也。若言著淨，人性本淨，由妄念故，蓋覆真如。但無妄想，性自清淨；起心著淨，却生淨妄。妄無處所，著者是妄。淨無形相，却立淨相，言是工夫。作此見者，障自本性，却被淨縛。善知識！若修不動者，但見一切人時，不見人之是非善惡過患，即是自性不動。善知識！迷人身雖不動，開口便說他人是非長短好惡，與道違背。

The Master addressed the assembly: 'In this school, sitting meditation fundamentally does not focus on the mind, nor does it focus on purity, nor is it about being motionless. If one speaks of focusing on the mind—the mind is fundamentally illusory. Knowing the mind is like a phantom, there is nothing to focus on. If one speaks of focusing on purity—people's nature is originally pure. It is due to deluded thoughts that true suchness is obscured. Simply be free of deluded thoughts and the nature is pure of itself. If you arouse the mind to focus on purity, you instead produce the delusion of purity. Delusion has no fixed location; the one who fixates is deluded. Purity has no form, yet you set up the form of purity and call it spiritual practice. Those who hold this view obstruct their own original nature and become bound by purity. Good friends! Those who cultivate stillness should simply, when seeing all people, not perceive their right and wrong, good and evil, faults and afflictions—that is the stillness of one's own nature. Good friends! A deluded person may sit with the body unmoving, yet the moment they open their mouth they speak of others' right and wrong, strengths and weaknesses, good and bad—this goes against the Way.'
