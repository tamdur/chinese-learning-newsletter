般若波羅蜜多心經

觀自在菩薩，行深般若波羅蜜多時，照見五蘊皆空，度一切苦厄。

Avalokitesvara Bodhisattva, when practicing the profound Prajna Paramita, illuminated the five aggregates and saw that they are all empty, thus overcoming all suffering and distress.

孟子・滕文公下

公孫丑問曰：「不見諸侯何義？」孟子曰：「古者不為臣不見。段干木踰垣而辟之，泄柳閉門而不內，是皆已甚。迫，斯可以見矣。陽貨欲見孔子而惡無禮，大夫有賜於士，不得受於其家，則往拜其門。陽貨矙孔子之亡也，而饋孔子蒸豚；孔子亦矙其亡也，而往拜之。當是時，陽貨先，豈得不見？曾子曰：『脅肩諂笑，病于夏畦。』子路曰：『未同而言，觀其色赧赧然，非由之所知也。』由是觀之，則君子之所養可知已矣。」

Gongsun Chou asked: "What is the principle behind not going to see feudal lords?" Mencius said: "In antiquity, if one was not a minister, one did not seek an audience. Duan Ganmu leapt over a wall to avoid a lord's visit; Xie Liu shut his gate and refused entry. These were excessive. If one is pressed, then one may agree to meet. Yang Huo wanted to see Confucius but disliked appearing discourteous. The rule was that if a great officer sent a gift to a scholar who was not home to receive it, the scholar must go to the officer's gate to pay respects. Yang Huo watched for a time when Confucius was out and sent him a steamed piglet. Confucius likewise watched for a time when Yang Huo was out and went to pay his respects. At that point, Yang Huo had made the first move — how could Confucius have refused to see him? Zengzi said: 'Hunching your shoulders and forcing a flattering smile is more exhausting than working in a summer garden.' Zilu said: 'To speak with someone you are not in accord with, and watch his face flush with shame — that is beyond my understanding.' From this we can see what the gentleman values in self-cultivation."

六祖大師法寶壇經・懺悔品第六

從今日去，稱覺為師，更不歸依邪魔外道，以自性三寶常自證明，勸善知識歸依自性三寶。佛者，覺也。法者，正也。僧者，淨也。自心歸依覺，邪迷不生，少欲知足，能離財色，名兩足尊。自心歸依正，念念無邪見，以無邪見故，即無人我貢高，貪愛執著，名離欲尊。自心歸依淨，一切塵勞愛欲境界，自性皆不染著，名眾中尊。若修此行，是自歸依。凡夫不會，從日至夜受三歸戒。若言歸依佛，佛在何處？若不見佛，憑何所歸，言却成妄。

From this day forward, take awakening as your teacher and never again take refuge in deviant demons or non-Buddhist paths. Constantly verify this through the Three Treasures of your own self-nature. I urge you, good friends, to take refuge in the Three Treasures of your own nature. 'Buddha' means awakening. 'Dharma' means what is right and true. 'Sangha' means purity. When your own mind takes refuge in awakening, deviance and delusion do not arise. With few desires and contentment, able to be free from wealth and sensuality — this is called 'the most honored, replete in both blessings and wisdom.' When your own mind takes refuge in what is right, thought after thought free from wrong views, and because there are no wrong views there is no self-importance, no arrogance, no greed, no attachment — this is called 'the most honored, free from desire.' When your own mind takes refuge in purity, all dusty toil and realms of desire cannot stain your own nature — this is called 'the most honored among communities.' To practice in this way is to take refuge in oneself. Ordinary people do not understand this. Day after day they take the threefold refuge precepts. If they say they take refuge in the Buddha, where is the Buddha? If they do not see the Buddha, on what basis do they take refuge? Their words become falsehood.
