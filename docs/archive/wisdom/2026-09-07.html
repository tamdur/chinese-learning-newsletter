般若波羅蜜多心經

觀自在菩薩，行深般若波羅蜜多時，照見五蘊皆空，度一切苦厄。

Avalokitesvara Bodhisattva, when practicing the profound Prajna Paramita, illuminated the five aggregates and saw that they are all empty, thus overcoming all suffering and distress.

孟子 · 離婁下

禹、稷當平世，三過其門而不入，孔子賢之。顏子當亂世，居於陋巷。一簞食，一瓢飲。人不堪其憂，顏子不改其樂，孔子賢之。孟子曰：「禹、稷、顏回同道。禹思天下有溺者，由己溺之也；稷思天下有飢者，由己飢之也，是以如是其急也。禹、稷、顏子易地則皆然。今有同室之人鬬者，救之，雖被髮纓冠而救之，可也。鄉鄰有鬬者，被髮纓冠而往救之，則惑也，雖閉戶可也。」

Yu and Ji, in a time of peace, passed by their own doors three times without entering, and Confucius praised them for it. Yan Hui, in a time of disorder, lived in a narrow alley. A single bamboo bowl of rice, a single gourd ladle of water — others could not have borne such hardship, yet Yan Hui never changed his joy. Confucius praised him for it. Mencius said: "Yu, Ji, and Yan Hui followed the same Way. Yu felt that if anyone in the world was drowning, it was as if he himself had drowned them. Ji felt that if anyone in the world was starving, it was as if he himself had starved them — that is why they acted with such urgency. If Yu, Ji, and Yan Hui had exchanged positions, each would have done the same as the other. Now suppose someone in your own household is fighting — it is right to rush to the rescue, even with your hair undone and cap untied. But if a neighbor in the village is fighting, to rush out with hair undone and cap untied would be misguided — it would be fine just to bolt your door."

六祖大師法寶壇經 · 頓漸品第八

聽吾偈曰：心地無非自性戒，心地無癡自性慧，心地無亂自性定，不增不減自金剛，身去身來本三昧。」誠聞偈，悔謝，乃呈一偈曰：「五蘊幻身，幻何究竟？迴趣真如，法還不淨。」師然之，復語誠曰：「汝師戒定慧，勸小根智人；吾戒定慧，勸大根智人。若悟自性，亦不立菩提涅槃，亦不立解脫知見。無一法可得，方能建立萬法。若解此意，亦名佛身，亦名菩提涅槃，亦名解脫知見。

'Listen to my verse: When the ground of mind is free from wrong, that is the precept of self-nature. When the ground of mind is free from delusion, that is the wisdom of self-nature. When the ground of mind is free from disturbance, that is the concentration of self-nature. Neither increasing nor decreasing — that itself is diamond. The body comes and goes, yet fundamentally it is samadhi.' Hearing this verse, Zhicheng repented and expressed gratitude, then offered a verse of his own: 'The five aggregates are an illusory body — how could illusion be ultimate? Even turning toward True Suchness, the Dharma is still not pure.' The Master affirmed this and further told Zhicheng: 'Your teacher's precepts, concentration, and wisdom encourage those of lesser capacity and lesser wisdom. My precepts, concentration, and wisdom encourage those of greater capacity and greater wisdom. If one awakens to self-nature, one does not even establish bodhi and nirvana, nor does one establish liberation or knowledge and insight. When not a single dharma can be grasped, only then can one establish the myriad dharmas. If you understand this meaning, it is also called the Buddha-body, also called bodhi and nirvana, also called liberation and knowledge and insight.'
