般若波羅蜜多心經 — Heart Sutra — Thursday

無眼耳鼻舌身意，無色聲香味觸法，無眼界，乃至無意識界。無無明，亦無無明盡，乃至無老死，亦無老死盡。

There are no eyes, ears, nose, tongue, body, or mind; no sights, sounds, smells, tastes, touches, or objects of mind; no realm of sight, and so on up to no realm of consciousness. There is no ignorance and no end of ignorance, and so on up to no old age and death and no end of old age and death.

孟子・離婁上 — Mencius, Li Lou I

孟子曰：「存乎人者，莫良於眸子。眸子不能掩其惡。胸中正，則眸子瞭焉；胸中不正，則眸子眊焉。聽其言也，觀其眸子，人焉廋哉？」孟子曰：「恭者不侮人，儉者不奪人。侮奪人之君，惟恐不順焉，惡得為恭儉？恭儉豈可以聲音笑貌為哉？」淳于髡曰：「男女授受不親，禮與？」孟子曰：「禮也。」曰：「嫂溺則援之以手乎？」曰：「嫂溺不援，是豺狼也。男女授受不親，禮也；嫂溺援之以手者，權也。」

Mencius said: "Of all the features that a person possesses, none is more telling than the pupils of the eyes. The pupils cannot hide a person's wickedness. When one's heart is upright, the pupils are bright and clear; when one's heart is not upright, the pupils are dim and clouded. Listen to a person's words and observe his pupils—how can anyone hide their true self?" Mencius said: "The respectful do not insult others; the frugal do not rob others. A ruler who insults and robs others, constantly fearing only that they will not be obedient—how can he be called respectful or frugal? Can respectfulness and frugality be achieved through mere tone of voice and facial expression?" Chunyu Kun asked: "Is it a rule of propriety that men and women should not touch hands when giving or receiving things?" Mencius said: "It is a rule of propriety." Chunyu Kun said: "If your sister-in-law were drowning, would you pull her out with your hand?" Mencius said: "Not to pull out a drowning sister-in-law would make one a wolf. The rule that men and women should not touch hands when giving or receiving is propriety; pulling out a drowning sister-in-law with your hand is a matter of discretion in extraordinary circumstances."

六祖大師法寶壇經・機緣品第七 — Platform Sutra

見聞轉誦是小乘；悟法解義是中乘；依法修行是大乘；萬法盡通，萬法俱備，一切不染，離諸法相，一無所得，名最上乘。乘是行義，不在口爭。汝須自修，莫問吾也。一切時中，自性自如。」常禮謝執侍，終師之世。

僧志道，廣州南海人也。請益曰：「學人自出家，覽《涅槃經》十載有餘，未明大意，願和尚垂誨。」師曰：「汝何處未明？」曰：「諸行無常，是生滅法；生滅滅已，寂滅為樂。於此疑惑。

Hearing and reciting the sutras by rote is the Lesser Vehicle; understanding the Dharma and grasping its meaning is the Middle Vehicle; practicing in accord with the Dharma is the Greater Vehicle. When all dharmas are thoroughly penetrated, all dharmas fully embodied, with nothing defiled, free from attachment to the marks of all dharmas, and nothing whatsoever attained—this is called the Supreme Vehicle. ‘Vehicle’ means practice, not verbal debate. You must cultivate this yourself; do not ask me about it. At all times, your own nature is naturally so.” Zhichang bowed in gratitude and served as attendant for the rest of the Master’s life.
