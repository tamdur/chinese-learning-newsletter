般若波羅蜜多心經

無苦集滅道，無智亦無得。以無所得故，菩提薩埵，依般若波羅蜜多故，心無罣礙。

There is no suffering, no cause of suffering, no cessation, no path; no wisdom and no attainment. With nothing to attain, a bodhisattva relies on Prajna Paramita, and the mind is without hindrance.

孟子 · 離婁下

曰：「諫行言聽，膏澤下於民；有故而去，則君使人導之出疆，又先於其所往；去三年不反，然後收其田里。此之謂三有禮焉。如此，則為之服矣。今也為臣。諫則不行，言則不聽；膏澤不下於民；有故而去，則君搏執之，又極之於其所往；去之日，遂收其田里。此之謂寇讎。寇讎何服之有？」孟子曰：「無罪而殺士，則大夫可以去；無罪而戮民，則士可以徙。」孟子曰：「君仁莫不仁，君義莫不義。」孟子曰：「非禮之禮，非義之義，大人弗為。」

Mencius said: "When his advice was followed and his words heeded, so that his beneficence flowed down to the people; and when he had reason to leave, the ruler sent an escort to conduct him beyond the border and sent word ahead to his destination; and when after three years he did not return, only then did the ruler reclaim his fields and residence — this is called the three courtesies. Under such conditions, one would wear mourning. But nowadays when serving as a minister, one's advice is not followed and one's words are not heeded; beneficence does not flow down to the people; when one has reason to leave, the ruler seizes and detains him and works to obstruct him at his destination; and on the very day of departure, he immediately reclaims one's fields and residence. This is what is called treating someone as an enemy. What mourning does one owe an enemy?" Mencius said: "When the innocent are killed among the officers, the high ministers may leave. When the innocent are slaughtered among the common people, the officers may relocate." Mencius said: "When the ruler is benevolent, none will be without benevolence. When the ruler is righteous, none will be without righteousness." Mencius said: "Propriety that is not truly proper, righteousness that is not truly right — a great person will have no part in them."

"Venerable one, from what quarter have you come, that you should give rise to such great self-conceit?"

Xuanjue said: "The matter of birth and death is grave; impermanence comes swiftly."

The Master said: "Why not embody what is unborn, and understand what has no swiftness?"

Xuanjue said: "The very embodying is unborn; the understanding is fundamentally without swiftness."

The Master said: "Just so, just so!"

Only then did Xuanjue assume the full formal bearing and make his bow — and a moment later he announced his departure.

The Master said: "Isn't your going back rather swift?"

Xuanjue said: "Fundamentally there is no motion at all — how could there be swiftness?"

The Master said: "Who is it that knows there is no motion?"

Xuanjue said: "It is you, sir, who are producing distinctions."

The Master said: "You have thoroughly grasped the meaning of the unborn."

Xuanjue said: "How could the unborn have any meaning?"

The Master said: "If there is no meaning, who is it that makes distinctions?"

Xuanjue said: "Making distinctions, too, is not meaning."

The Master said: "Excellent! Stay at least for one night."

And so people of that time called him "One-Night Jue."
