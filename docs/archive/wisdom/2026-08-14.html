般若波羅蜜多心經 Heart Sutra — Friday

第 5 段（共七段）

無苦集滅道，無智亦無得。以無所得故，菩提薩埵，依般若波羅蜜多故，心無罣礙。

There is no suffering, no cause of suffering, no cessation, no path; no wisdom and no attainment. With nothing to attain, a bodhisattva relies on Prajna Paramita, and the mind is without hindrance.

孟子・離婁上 Mencius — li-lou-i

第 127 章（共 269 章）

孟子曰：「不仁者可與言哉？安其危而利其菑，樂其所以亡者。不仁而可與言，則何亡國敗家之有？有孺子歌曰：『滄浪之水清兮，可以濯我纓；滄浪之水濁兮，可以濯我足。』孔子曰：『小子聽之！清斯濯纓，濁斯濯足矣，自取之也。』夫人必自侮，然後人侮之；家必自毀，而後人毀之；國必自伐，而後人伐之。《太甲》曰：『天作孽，猶可違；自作孽，不可活。』此之謂也。」

Mencius said: "Can one even reason with the unbenevolent? They feel secure in their peril, profit from their disasters, and take pleasure in the very things that lead to their ruin. If the unbenevolent could be reasoned with, how would there ever be ruined states and destroyed families? A child's song goes: 'When the waters of the Canglang are clear, I can wash my cap-strings; when the waters of the Canglang are muddy, I can wash my feet.' Confucius said: 'Listen, my young students! When the water is clear, it is used to wash cap-strings; when it is muddy, it is used to wash feet—it brought this on itself.' A person must first demean himself before others demean him; a family must first destroy itself before others destroy it; a state must first attack itself before others attack it. The Tai Jia says: 'When Heaven sends down calamities, one may yet escape them; but when one brings calamity upon oneself, there is no surviving.' This is what it means."

六祖大師法寶壇經・機緣品第七 Platform Sutra

第 127 段（共 178 段）

只教汝去假歸實，歸實之後，實亦無名。應知所有珍財，盡屬於汝，由汝受用，更不作父想，亦不作子想，亦無用想。是名持法華經，從劫至劫，手不釋卷，從晝至夜，無不念時也。」達蒙啟發，踊躍歡喜，以偈讚曰：「經誦三千部，曹溪一句亡，未明出世旨，寧歇累生狂。羊鹿牛權設，初中後善揚，誰知火宅內，元是法中王。」師曰：「汝今後方可名念經僧也。」達從此領玄旨，亦不輟誦經。

僧智通，壽州安豐人。

'The teaching is simply to have you abandon the provisional and return to the real. After returning to the real, even "real" has no name. You should know that all the precious treasures belong entirely to you, for you to use freely — without thinking of them as the father's, nor as the son's, nor needing any thought of using them at all. This is called truly upholding the Lotus Sutra — from kalpa to kalpa, the hand never lets go of the scroll; from dawn to night, there is not a moment without mindfulness of it.' Fada, having received this revelation, leapt with joy and offered this verse of praise: 'Three thousand times the sutra was recited, but one phrase at Caoxi swept it all away. Before the meaning of the Buddha's appearance was clear, how could the madness accumulated over lifetimes be put to rest? The goat, deer, and ox carts were provisionally established; the good of the beginning, middle, and end was thus proclaimed. Who would have known that within the burning house, one was all along the Dharma King?' The Master said: 'From now on, you may truly be called a sutra-reciting monk.' Fada, having grasped the profound meaning from that point on, also never ceased reciting the sutra. The monk Zhitong was a native of Anfeng in Shouzhou.
