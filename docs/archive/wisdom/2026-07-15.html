般若波羅蜜多心經

舍利子，是諸法空相，不生不滅，不垢不淨，不增不減。是故空中無色，無受想行識。

Shariputra, all dharmas are marked with emptiness — they are neither born nor destroyed, neither defiled nor pure, neither increasing nor decreasing. Therefore, in emptiness there is no form, no feelings, perceptions, mental formations, or consciousness.

孟子・滕文公上

「從許子之道，則市賈不貳，國中無偽。雖使五尺之童適市，莫之或欺。布帛長短同，則賈相若；麻縷絲絮輕重同，則賈相若；五穀多寡同，則賈相若；屨大小同，則賈相若。」曰：「夫物之不齊，物之情也；或相倍蓰，或相什伯，或相千萬。子比而同之，是亂天下也。巨屨小屨同賈，人豈為之哉？從許子之道，相率而為偽者也，惡能治國家？」墨者夷之，因徐辟而求見孟子。孟子曰：「吾固願見，今吾尚病，病愈，我且往見，夷子不來！」

'If we follow Master Xu's way, then market prices will not vary and there will be no deception in the state. Even if you send a small child to the market, no one will cheat him. Cloth of the same length will sell at the same price; hemp, silk, and cotton of the same weight will sell at the same price; grain of the same quantity will sell at the same price; shoes of the same size will sell at the same price.' Mencius said, 'That things are unequal is the nature of things. Some differ by twice or five times, some by ten or a hundred times, some by a thousand or ten thousand times. If you try to level them all, you will throw the world into chaos. If fine shoes and coarse shoes are priced the same, who would make fine shoes? To follow Master Xu's way would be to lead everyone into deception — how could that govern a state?' A Mohist named Yi Zhi sought through Xu Bi to see Mencius. Mencius said, 'I would certainly like to see him, but I am still ill. When I have recovered, I will go to see him. Yi Zhi need not come.'

六祖大師法寶壇經・定慧品第四

善知識！我此法門，從上以來，先立無念為宗，無相為體，無住為本。無相者，於相而離相。無念者，於念而無念。無住者，人之本性。於世間善惡好醜，乃至冤之與親，言語觸刺欺爭之時，並將為空，不思酬害，念念之中不思前境。若前念今念後念，念念相續不斷，名為繫縛。於諸法上念念不住，即無縛也。此是以無住為本。善知識！外離一切相，名為無相。能離於相，即法體清淨。此是以無相為體。善知識！於諸境上，心不染，曰無念。

'Good friends! This Dharma teaching of mine, from the patriarchs onward, has first established no-thought as its doctrine, formlessness as its substance, and non-abiding as its foundation. Formlessness means to be amid forms yet free from forms. No-thought means to be amid thoughts yet free from thoughts. Non-abiding is the fundamental nature of human beings. In the midst of all worldly things — good and evil, attractive and ugly, even when facing hostility or friendship, provocative words, deception, and conflict — regard it all as empty. Do not contemplate retaliation or harm. In thought after thought, do not dwell on past objects. If previous thoughts, present thoughts, and future thoughts follow one upon another without ceasing, this is called bondage. But in regard to all dharmas, if thought after thought does not abide, then there is no bondage. This is what is meant by taking non-abiding as the foundation. Good friends! Being free from all outward forms is called formlessness. When you can be free from forms, then the Dharma-substance is pure. This is what is meant by taking formlessness as the substance. Good friends! When the mind is not stained by any object encountered, this is called no-thought.'
