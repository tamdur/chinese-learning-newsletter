般若波羅蜜多心經

無苦集滅道，無智亦無得。以無所得故，菩提薩埵，依般若波羅蜜多故，心無罣礙。

There is no suffering, no cause of suffering, no cessation, no path; no wisdom and no attainment. With nothing to attain, a bodhisattva relies on Prajna Paramita, and the mind is without hindrance.

孟子 · 公孫丑下

曰：「古者棺槨無度，中古棺七寸，槨稱之。自天子達於庶人。非直為觀美也，然後盡於人心。不得，不可以為悅；無財，不可以為悅。得之為有財，古之人皆用之，吾何為獨不然？且比化者，無使土親膚，於人心獨無恔乎？吾聞之君子：不以天下儉其親。」沈同以其私問曰：「燕可伐與？」

Mencius said: "In ancient times there were no fixed standards for inner and outer coffins. In the middle period, the inner coffin was seven inches thick, and the outer coffin proportionate. This applied from the Son of Heaven down to commoners. It was not merely for the sake of appearance — only then could people fully express their feelings. If prevented by regulations, they could not be satisfied; if lacking the means, they could not be satisfied. When both permission and means were available, the ancients all did it — why should I alone do otherwise? Moreover, is there not some comfort to the bereaved in ensuring that bare earth does not touch the skin of the deceased? I have heard that a gentleman does not stint on his parents for the sake of the world." Shen Tong asked him privately: "May Yan be attacked?"

六祖大師法寶壇經 · 般若品第二

第一莫著空，若空心靜坐，即著無記空。善知識！世界虛空，能含萬物色像，日月星宿，山河大地，泉源谿澗，草木叢林，惡人善人，惡法善法，天堂地獄，一切大海，須彌諸山，總在空中。世人性空，亦復如是。善知識！自性能含萬法是大，萬法在諸人性中。若見一切人、惡之與善，盡皆不取不捨亦不染著，心如虛空，名之為大，故曰摩訶。善知識！迷人口說，智者心行。又有迷人，空心靜坐，百無所思，自稱為大。

'Above all, do not cling to emptiness. If you sit in meditation with a blank mind, you fall into the emptiness of blankness. Good friends, the emptiness of the universe can contain all things and forms — the sun, moon, and stars; mountains, rivers, and the great earth; springs, streams, and valleys; grass, trees, and forests; bad people and good people; bad dharmas and good dharmas; heavens and hells; all the great oceans; and all the Sumeru mountains — all are contained within space. The emptiness of people's nature is also like this. Good friends, our self-nature can contain the ten thousand dharmas — this is what "great" means. The ten thousand dharmas are within the nature of all people. If you see all people — whether evil or good — and neither grasp nor reject any of them, and are not stained by attachment, then the mind is like empty space, and this is called "great" — hence the term "maha." Good friends, the deluded merely talk about it; the wise practice it in their minds. There are also deluded people who sit in meditation with blank minds, thinking of nothing whatsoever, and call themselves great.'
