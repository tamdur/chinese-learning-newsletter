般若波羅蜜多心經

舍利子，是諸法空相，不生不滅，不垢不淨，不增不減。是故空中無色，無受想行識。

Shariputra, all dharmas are marked with emptiness — they are neither born nor destroyed, neither defiled nor pure, neither increasing nor decreasing. Therefore, in emptiness there is no form, no feelings, perceptions, mental formations, or consciousness.

孟子 · 滕文公下

曰：「士之仕也，猶農夫之耕也，農夫豈為出疆舍其耒耜哉？」曰：「晉國亦仕國也，未嘗聞仕如此其急。仕如此其急也，君子之難仕，何也？」曰：「丈夫生而願為之有室，女子生而願為之有家。父母之心，人皆有之。不待父母之命、媒妁之言，鑽穴隙相窺，踰牆相從，則父母國人皆賤之。古之人未嘗不欲仕也，又惡不由其道。不由其道而往者，與鑽穴隙之類也。」彭更問曰：「後車數十乘，從者數百人，以傳食於諸侯，不以泰乎？」

Mencius said: "A scholar's service in government is like a farmer's plowing — would a farmer abandon his plow and hoe just because he crosses a border?" Zhou Xiao said: "The state of Jin also employs officials, yet I have never heard of such urgency about office. If seeking office is this urgent, why is it that a gentleman finds it difficult to take office?" Mencius said: "When a boy is born, his parents wish for him to have a wife; when a girl is born, they wish for her to have a husband. All parents share this feeling. But if the young, without waiting for their parents' permission or a matchmaker's introduction, bore holes through walls to peek at each other and climb over fences to be together, then parents and countrymen alike would despise them. The ancients certainly desired to serve in government, but they also hated to do so by improper means. To seek office through improper means is of the same kind as boring through walls." Peng Geng asked: "Is it not excessive to travel with dozens of carriages and hundreds of followers, eating your way from one feudal lord to another?"

六祖大師法寶壇經 · 懺悔品第六

自心無礙，常以智慧觀照自性，不造諸惡；雖修眾善，心不執著，敬上念下，矜恤孤貧，名慧香。四、解脫香。即自心無所攀緣，不思善、不思惡，自在無礙，名解脫香。五、解脫知見香。自心既無所攀緣善惡，不可沈空守寂，即須廣學多聞，識自本心，達諸佛理，和光接物，無我無人，直至菩提，真性不易，名解脫知見香。善知識！此香各自內熏，莫向外覓。

「今與汝等授無相懺悔，滅三世罪，令得三業清淨。

your own mind is unobstructed, and you constantly use wisdom to contemplate your own nature. You do not commit any evil; though you practice many good deeds, your mind does not cling to them. You respect those above and care for those below, and show compassion for the orphaned and the poor. This is called the incense of wisdom. Fourth, the incense of liberation — that is, your own mind does not grasp at conditions, does not think of good or evil, and is free and unobstructed. This is called the incense of liberation. Fifth, the incense of the knowledge and insight of liberation — when your own mind neither grasps at good or evil nor sinks into emptiness or clings to stillness, you must study broadly and learn widely, recognize your original mind, comprehend the principles of all Buddhas, harmonize with others in a gentle light, be free of self and other, and go straight to bodhi. Your true nature never changes. This is called the incense of the knowledge and insight of liberation. Good friends! This incense each person must burn inwardly — do not seek it outside. Now I will confer upon you the formless repentance, to eliminate the offenses of the three times and purify the three karmas of body, speech, and mind.'
