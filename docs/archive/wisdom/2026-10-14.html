般若波羅蜜多心經

舍利子，是諸法空相，不生不滅，不垢不淨，不增不減。是故空中無色，無受想行識。

Shariputra, all dharmas are marked with emptiness — they are neither born nor destroyed, neither defiled nor pure, neither increasing nor decreasing. Therefore, in emptiness there is no form, no feelings, perceptions, mental formations, or consciousness.

孟子 · 萬章下

孟子曰：「仕非為貧也，而有時乎為貧；娶妻非為養也，而有時乎為養。為貧者，辭尊居卑，辭富居貧。辭尊居卑，辭富居貧，惡乎宜乎？抱關擊柝。孔子嘗為委吏矣，曰『會計當而已矣』。嘗為乘田矣，曰『牛羊茁壯，長而已矣』。位卑而言高，罪也；立乎人之本朝，而道不行，恥也」萬章曰：「士之不託諸侯，何也？」孟子曰：「不敢也。諸侯失國，而後託於諸侯，禮也；士之託於諸侯，非禮也。」萬章曰：「君餽之粟，則受之乎？」

Mencius said: "Taking office is not for the sake of poverty, yet sometimes it is because of poverty. Marrying a wife is not for the sake of having someone to care for one's parents, yet sometimes it is for that reason. One who takes office because of poverty should decline high rank and remain in a low position, decline wealth and remain in poverty. What position would be appropriate for such a person? A gatekeeper or a night watchman. Confucius once served as a minor official in charge of stores, and he said: 'My accounts are accurate — that is all that matters.' He once served as an overseer of livestock, and he said: 'The cattle and sheep are thriving and well-grown — that is all that matters.' To hold a low position and speak of lofty matters is a transgression. To stand in a ruler's court while one's Way is not practiced is a disgrace." Wan Zhang said: "Why does a scholar not depend on feudal lords for his livelihood?" Mencius said: "He does not dare to. When a feudal lord has lost his state, it is proper for him to depend on another feudal lord. But for a scholar to depend on a feudal lord is not proper." Wan Zhang said: "If the ruler sends him grain, should he accept it?"

正信希有分第六

須菩提白佛言：「世尊！頗有眾生，得聞如是言說章句，生實信不？」佛告須菩提：「莫作是說。如來滅後，後五百歲，有持戒修福者，於此章句能生信心，以此為實，當知是人不於一佛、二佛、三四五佛而種善根，已於無量千萬佛所種諸善根，聞是章句，乃至一念生淨信者，須菩提！如來悉知悉見，是諸眾生得如是無量福德。何以故？是諸眾生無復我相、人相、眾生相、壽者相。」「無法相，亦無非法相。何以故？是諸眾生若心取相，則為著我、人、眾生、壽者。」「若取法相，即著我、人、眾生、壽者。何以故？若取非法相，即著我、人、眾生、壽者。是故不應取法，不應取非法。以是義故，如來常說：汝等比丘，知我說法，如筏喻者，法尚應捨，何況非法。」

Subhuti addressed the Buddha: "World-Honored One, will there be beings who, upon hearing these words and passages, can give rise to true faith?" The Buddha told Subhuti: "Do not speak thus. In the last five hundred years after the Tathagata's passing, there will be those who uphold the precepts and cultivate merit who can give rise to faith in these passages and accept them as truth. Know that such people have planted good roots not with just one, two, three, four, or five Buddhas, but have already planted good roots with immeasurable millions of Buddhas. Upon hearing these passages, those who give rise to even a single thought of pure faith — Subhuti, the Tathagata fully knows and fully sees them. These beings attain such immeasurable merit. Why? Because these beings no longer hold to the notion of a self, a person, a sentient being, or a life span." "They hold neither to the notion of dharmas nor to the notion of no-dharmas. Why? If these beings seize upon characteristics in their minds, they cling to self, person, sentient being, and life span." "If they seize upon the notion of dharmas, they cling to self, person, sentient being, and life span. Why? If they seize upon the notion of no-dharmas, they likewise cling to self, person, sentient being, and life span. Therefore, one should not grasp at dharmas, nor should one grasp at no-dharmas. For this reason, the Tathagata always says: You monks should know that my teaching of the Dharma is like a raft. Even the Dharma should be let go of, how much more so what is not the Dharma."
