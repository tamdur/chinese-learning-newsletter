般若波羅蜜多心經

無苦集滅道，無智亦無得。以無所得故，菩提薩埵，依般若波羅蜜多故，心無罣礙。

There is no suffering, no cause of suffering, no cessation, no path; no wisdom and no attainment. With nothing to attain, a bodhisattva relies on Prajna Paramita, and the mind is without hindrance.

孟子 · 公孫丑下

有欲為王留行者，坐而言。不應，隱几而臥。客不悅曰：「弟子齊宿而後敢言，夫子臥而不聽，請勿復敢見矣。」曰：「坐！我明語子。昔者魯繆公無人乎子思之側，則不能安子思；泄柳、申詳，無人乎繆公之側，則不能安其身。子為長者慮，而不及子思，子絕長者乎？長者絕子乎？」

Someone who wanted to persuade the king to detain Mencius sat down and began to speak. Mencius did not respond, but leaned on his armrest and dozed. The visitor was displeased and said: "I purified myself with a night of fasting before daring to speak, yet you doze and do not listen. I shall never presume to see you again." Mencius said: "Sit down! Let me speak plainly. In the past, if Duke Mu of Lu did not have a trusted person at Zi Si's side, he could not have kept Zi Si at ease. And if Xie Liu and Shen Xiang had not had trusted people at Duke Mu's side, they could not have kept themselves secure. You presume to plan on behalf of an elder, yet your concern does not match what Zi Si received. Is it you who are cutting off the elder, or is it the elder who is cutting you off?"

六祖大師法寶壇經 · 般若品第二

因智慧性，方能建立。若無世人，一切萬法本自不有，故知萬法本自人興。一切經書，因人說有。緣其人中有愚有智，愚為小人，智為大人。愚者問於智人，智者與愚人說法。愚人忽然悟解心開，即與智人無別。善知識！不悟即佛是眾生，一念悟時眾生是佛，故知萬法盡在自心。何不從自心中，頓見真如本性？《菩薩戒經》云：『我本元自性清淨，若識自心見性，皆成佛道。』《淨名經》云：『即時豁然，還得本心。

'It is because of the nature of wisdom that they could be established. If there were no people in the world, the ten thousand dharmas would not exist of themselves. Therefore we know that the ten thousand dharmas originally arise because of people. All scriptures exist because people expound them. Among people, some are foolish and some are wise. The foolish are called small-minded; the wise are called great-minded. The foolish ask the wise, and the wise teach the Dharma to the foolish. When the foolish suddenly awaken and their minds open, they become no different from the wise. Good friends, without awakening, even a buddha is a sentient being; with a single thought of awakening, a sentient being is a buddha. Therefore we know that the ten thousand dharmas are all within our own mind. Why not, from within your own mind, directly see the true suchness of your original nature? The Bodhisattva Precepts Sutra says: "My fundamental original nature is inherently pure. If one recognizes one's own mind and sees one's nature, one will attain the Buddha-way." The Vimalakirti Sutra says: "In an instant, there is sudden clarity, and one recovers one's original mind."'
