般若波羅蜜多心經

Heart Sutra · Monday segment (1/7)

觀自在菩薩，行深般若波羅蜜多時，照見五蘊皆空，度一切苦厄。

Avalokitesvara Bodhisattva, when practicing the profound Prajna Paramita, illuminated the five aggregates and saw that they are all empty, thus overcoming all suffering and distress.

孟子・離婁上

Mencius · 離婁上 · passage 124 of 269

孟子曰：「三代之得天下也以仁，其失天下也以不仁。國之所以廢興存亡者亦然。天子不仁，不保四海；諸侯不仁，不保社稷；卿大夫不仁，不保宗廟；士庶人不仁，不保四體。今惡死亡而樂不仁，是猶惡醉而強酒。」孟子曰：「愛人不親反其仁，治人不治反其智，禮人不答反其敬。行有不得者，皆反求諸己，其身正而天下歸之。《詩》云：『永言配命，自求多福。』」

Mencius said: "The Three Dynasties won the realm through benevolence, and lost the realm through the lack of benevolence. The same principle applies to why states decline or flourish, survive or perish. If the Son of Heaven is not benevolent, he cannot protect the four seas; if a feudal lord is not benevolent, he cannot protect his altars of soil and grain; if a minister or high official is not benevolent, he cannot protect his ancestral temple; if a gentleman or commoner is not benevolent, he cannot protect his own four limbs. Now, to hate death and ruin yet delight in cruelty is like hating drunkenness yet forcing yourself to drink." Mencius said: "If you love others but they do not respond with affection, examine your own benevolence. If you try to govern others but they remain ungoverned, examine your own wisdom. If you treat others with propriety but they do not reciprocate, examine your own respectfulness. Whenever your actions fail to achieve their aim, always turn inward and examine yourself. When you are upright in yourself, the whole realm will turn to you. The Odes say: 'Always be in accord with Heaven's mandate, and seek your own blessings.'"

六祖大師法寶壇經・機緣品第七

Platform Sutra · 機緣品第七 · passage 124 of 178

吾亦勸一切人，於自心中，常開佛之知見。世人心邪，愚迷造罪，口善心惡，貪瞋嫉妬，諂佞我慢，侵人害物，自開眾生知見。若能正心，常生智慧，觀照自心，止惡行善，是自開佛之知見。汝須念念開佛知見，勿開眾生知見。開佛知見，即是出世；開眾生知見，即是世間。汝若但勞勞執念，以為功課者，何異犛牛愛尾。」達曰：「若然者，但得解義，不勞誦經耶？」師曰：「經有何過，豈障汝念？只為迷悟在人，損益由己。

'I too urge all people to constantly open the Buddha's knowledge and insight within their own minds. The minds of worldly people are crooked — foolish, deluded, and creating offenses; their mouths speak of goodness while their hearts harbor evil; they are full of greed, anger, jealousy, flattery, deceit, and arrogance; they harm others and injure living things. This is to open the knowledge and insight of sentient beings. If one can rectify the mind, constantly give rise to wisdom, contemplate and illuminate one's own mind, and cease evil while practicing good — this is to open the Buddha's knowledge and insight for oneself. In every thought, you must open the Buddha's knowledge and insight; do not open the knowledge and insight of sentient beings. To open the Buddha's knowledge and insight is to be beyond the world; to open the knowledge and insight of sentient beings is to remain in the world. If you merely toil away clinging to recitation and consider it your spiritual practice, how are you different from a yak infatuated with its own tail?' Fada asked: 'If that is so, then one need only understand the meaning — there is no need to recite the sutra?' The Master said: 'What fault does the sutra have? How could it obstruct your recitation? Delusion and awakening lie in the person; loss and gain depend on oneself.'
