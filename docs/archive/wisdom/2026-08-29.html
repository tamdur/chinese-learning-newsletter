Afterwards he composed the Song of Enlightenment, which spread widely throughout the world.

There was a meditator named Zhihuang, who in his early years had studied under the Fifth Patriarch and believed that he had already attained correct absorption. He lived in a hermitage and sat in meditation for long stretches, keeping this up for twenty years. The Master's disciple Xuance, wandering on pilgrimage, came to the region north of the Yellow River. Hearing of Zhihuang's reputation, he went to the hermitage and asked, "What are you doing here?"

Zhihuang said, "Entering samadhi."

Xuance said, "You speak of entering samadhi. Do you enter it with a mind, or without a mind? If you enter without a mind, then all insentient things — grasses, trees, tiles, and stones — ought to attain samadhi as well. If you enter with a mind, then every sentient being possessed of consciousness ought to attain it too."

Zhihuang said, "At the very moment I enter samadhi, I do not perceive any mind that either exists or does not exist."

Xuance said, "If you do not perceive any mind that exists or does not exist, then that is constant samadhi. How could there be any entering or leaving it? If there is entering and leaving, it is not the great samadhi."
