般若波羅蜜多心經

無苦集滅道，無智亦無得。以無所得故，菩提薩埵，依般若波羅蜜多故，心無罣礙。

There is no suffering, no cause of suffering, no cessation, no path; no wisdom and no attainment. With nothing to attain, a bodhisattva relies on Prajna Paramita, and the mind is without hindrance.

孟子・離婁上

曰：「今天下溺矣，夫子之不援，何也？」曰：「天下溺，援之以道；嫂溺，援之以手。子欲手援天下乎？」公孫丑曰：「君子之不教子，何也？」孟子曰：「勢不行也。教者必以正；以正不行，繼之以怒；繼之以怒，則反夷矣。『夫子教我以正，夫子未出於正也。』則是父子相夷也。父子相夷，則惡矣。古者易子而教之。父子之間不責善。責善則離，離則不祥莫大焉。」

Chunyu Kun said: "Now the whole realm is drowning. Why does the Master not rescue it?" Mencius said: "When the realm is drowning, one rescues it with the Way; when a sister-in-law is drowning, one rescues her with one's hand. Do you want to rescue the entire realm with your bare hand?" Gongsun Chou asked: "Why does a gentleman not teach his own sons?" Mencius said: "The circumstances do not allow it. A teacher must teach by what is correct; when correctness is not followed, anger ensues; when anger ensues, feelings are hurt. The son would think: 'My father teaches me what is correct, yet he himself does not always act correctly.' This leads father and son to hurt each other's feelings, and that is a terrible thing. In ancient times, people exchanged sons and taught each other's children. Between father and son, there should be no demanding of goodness from each other. To demand goodness leads to estrangement, and nothing is more inauspicious than estrangement between father and son."

六祖大師法寶壇經・機緣品第七

」師曰：「汝作麼生疑？」曰：「一切眾生皆有二身，謂色身、法身也。色身無常，有生有滅；法身有常，無知無覺。經云：『生滅滅已，寂滅為樂』者，不審何身寂滅？何身受樂？若色身者，色身滅時，四大分散，全然是苦，苦不可言樂。若法身寂滅，即同草木瓦石，誰當受樂？又法性是生滅之體，五蘊是生滅之用，一體五用，生滅是常。生則從體起用，滅則攝用歸體。若聽更生，即有情之類，不斷不滅；若不聽更生，則永歸寂滅，同於無情之物。

The Master said, “What exactly is it that you doubt?”

Zhidao said, “All sentient beings possess two bodies — namely the physical body and the Dharma body. The physical body is impermanent: it arises and it perishes. The Dharma body is permanent: it neither knows nor perceives. The sutra says, ‘When arising and ceasing have themselves been extinguished, that quiescent extinction is bliss.’ But I cannot make out which body it is that becomes quiescent and extinguished, and which body it is that experiences the bliss.

“If it is the physical body, then when the physical body perishes the four elements scatter apart, and that is nothing but suffering — suffering cannot be called bliss. And if it is the Dharma body that becomes quiescent and extinguished, then it is no different from grass and trees, tiles and stones — who would there be to experience the bliss?

“Furthermore, dharma-nature is the substance of arising and ceasing, while the five aggregates are the function of arising and ceasing. One substance with five functions — so arising and ceasing go on forever. In arising, function issues forth from the substance; in ceasing, function is drawn back and returns to the substance. If further arising is allowed, then sentient beings are never cut off and never extinguished. But if further arising is not allowed, then they return forever into quiescent extinction, no different from insentient things.”
