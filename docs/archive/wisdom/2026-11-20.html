般若波羅蜜多心經

無苦集滅道，無智亦無得。以無所得故，菩提薩埵，依般若波羅蜜多故，心無罣礙。

There is no suffering, no cause of suffering, no cessation, no path; no wisdom and no attainment. With nothing to attain, a bodhisattva relies on Prajna Paramita, and the mind is without hindrance.

孟子・告子下

「五霸，桓公為盛。葵丘之會諸侯，束牲、載書而不歃血。初命曰：『誅不孝，無易樹子，無以妾為妻。』再命曰：『尊賢育才，以彰有德。』三命曰：『敬老慈幼，無忘賓旅。』四命曰：『士無世官，官事無攝，取士必得，無專殺大夫。』五命曰：『無曲防，無遏糴，無有封而不告。』曰：『凡我同盟之人，既盟之後，言歸于好。』今之諸侯，皆犯此五禁，故曰：今之諸侯，五霸之罪人也。

'Of the Five Hegemons, Duke Huan of Qi was the greatest. At the covenant at Kuiqiu, the feudal lords bound the sacrificial animals and placed the written covenant on them, but did not smear their lips with blood. The first injunction said: "Punish the unfilial; do not set aside the designated heir; do not elevate a concubine to the status of wife." The second said: "Honor the worthy and nurture the talented, so as to distinguish the virtuous." The third said: "Respect the elderly and care for the young; do not neglect travelers and guests." The fourth said: "Officials shall not hold hereditary office; one office shall not be given multiple duties; selection of officials must be based on merit; do not execute ministers arbitrarily." The fifth said: "Do not build crooked dikes; do not block grain trade; do not grant fiefs without reporting them." It was declared: "All of us who have joined this covenant shall return to amicable relations after pledging." The feudal lords of today all violate these five prohibitions. That is why I say the feudal lords of today are sinners against the Five Hegemons.'

金剛般若波羅蜜經・應化非真分第三十二

「須菩提！若有人以滿無量阿僧祇世界七寶，持用布施；若有善男子、善女人，發菩提心者，持於此經，乃至四句偈等，受持讀誦，為人演說，其福勝彼。云何為人演說，不取於相，如如不動。何以故？」「一切有為法，如夢幻泡影，如露亦如電，應作如是觀。」佛說是經已，長老須菩提及諸比丘、比丘尼、優婆塞、優婆夷、一切世間天、人、阿修羅，聞佛所說，皆大歡喜，信受奉行。

"Subhuti, if someone used the seven treasures filling immeasurable asamkhya worlds for giving; and if a good man or good woman who has aspired to the bodhi mind upheld even as few as four lines of verse from this sutra, received, upheld, read, and recited it, and explained it to others — the merit of the latter would surpass the former. How should one explain it to others? By not grasping at appearances, remaining in thusness, unmoving. Why?" "All conditioned phenomena are like dreams, illusions, bubbles, and shadows; like dew and like lightning — one should contemplate them thus." When the Buddha finished speaking this sutra, the elder Subhuti, along with all the monks, nuns, laymen, laywomen, and all the beings of the world — devas, humans, and asuras — having heard what the Buddha taught, all rejoiced greatly, and faithfully received and practiced it.
