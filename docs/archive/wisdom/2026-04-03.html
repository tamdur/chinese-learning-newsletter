般若波羅蜜多心經 — 週五

無苦集滅道，無智亦無得。以無所得故，菩提薩埵，依般若波羅蜜多故，心無罣礙。

There is no suffering, no cause of suffering, no cessation, no path; no wisdom and no attainment. With nothing to attain, a bodhisattva relies on Prajna Paramita, and the mind is without hindrance.

孟子 · 梁惠王上

孟子對曰：「地方百里而可以王。王如施仁政於民，省刑罰，薄稅斂，深耕易耨。壯者以暇日修其孝悌忠信，入以事其父兄，出以事其長上，可使制梃以撻秦楚之堅甲利兵矣。彼奪其民時，使不得耕耨以養其父母，父母凍餓，兄弟妻子離散。彼陷溺其民，王往而征之，夫誰與王敵？故曰：『仁者無敵。』王請勿疑！」孟子見梁襄王。出，語人曰：「望之不似人君，就之而不見所畏焉。卒然問曰：『天下惡乎定？』吾對曰：『定于一。』

Mencius replied, 'A territory of even a hundred li square is sufficient to become a true king. If Your Majesty practices benevolent government toward the people — reducing punishments, lightening taxes and levies, encouraging deep plowing and thorough weeding — then the able-bodied can use their spare days to cultivate filial piety, brotherly respect, loyalty, and faithfulness, serving their fathers and elder brothers at home and their elders and superiors abroad. They could then be made to take up clubs and defeat the strong armor and sharp weapons of Qin and Chu. Those other states steal their people's time, preventing them from plowing and weeding to feed their parents. Parents freeze and starve; brothers, wives, and children are scattered and separated. Those rulers drown and trap their own people — if Your Majesty were to march against them, who would stand against you? Therefore it is said: "The benevolent have no enemies." I beg Your Majesty not to doubt this!' Mencius met King Xiang of Liang. After leaving, he said to someone, 'When I looked at him from afar, he did not look like a ruler of men; when I drew close, I saw nothing commanding about him. Abruptly he asked, "How can the world be settled?" I replied, "It will be settled through unity."'

金剛般若波羅蜜經 — Diamond Sutra

「復次，須菩提！菩薩於法，應無所住，行於布施。所謂不住色布施，不住聲、香、味、觸、法布施。須菩提！菩薩應如是布施，不住於相。何以故？若菩薩不住相布施。其福德不可思量。」「須菩提！於意云何？東方虛空，可思量不？」「不也，世尊！」「須菩提！南、西、北方，四維上下虛空，可思量不？」「不也，世尊！」「須菩提！菩薩無住相布施，福德亦復如是不可思量。須菩提！菩薩但應如所教住。」

"Furthermore, Subhuti, a bodhisattva should practice giving without abiding in anything. That is to say, one should give without abiding in form, and give without abiding in sound, smell, taste, touch, or mental objects. Subhuti, a bodhisattva should give in this way — without abiding in any characteristic. Why? If a bodhisattva gives without abiding in characteristics, the merit is beyond reckoning." "Subhuti, what do you think? Can the space to the east be measured?" "No, World-Honored One." "Subhuti, can the space to the south, west, north, or in the four intermediate directions, above and below, be measured?" "No, World-Honored One." "Subhuti, the merit of a bodhisattva who gives without abiding in characteristics is likewise beyond reckoning. Subhuti, a bodhisattva should abide only as instructed."
