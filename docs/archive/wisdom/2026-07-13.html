般若波羅蜜多心經

觀自在菩薩，行深般若波羅蜜多時，照見五蘊皆空，度一切苦厄。

Avalokitesvara Bodhisattva, when practicing the profound Prajna Paramita, illuminated the five aggregates and saw that they are all empty, thus overcoming all suffering and distress.

孟子 · 滕文公上

「堯以不得舜為己憂，舜以不得禹、皋陶為己憂。夫以百畝之不易為己憂者，農夫也。分人以財謂之惠，教人以善謂之忠，為天下得人者謂之仁。是故以天下與人易，為天下得人難。孔子曰：『大哉堯之為君！惟天為大，惟堯則之，蕩蕩乎民無能名焉！君哉舜也！巍巍乎有天下而不與焉！』堯舜之治天下，豈無所用其心哉？亦不用於耕耳。

'Yao regarded not finding Shun as his own greatest worry; Shun regarded not finding Yu and Gao Yao as his own greatest worry. One who worries about his hundred mu not being properly tilled is a farmer. To share one's wealth with others is called generosity; to teach others to be good is called loyalty; to find the right person for the world is called benevolence. Thus it is easy to give the world to someone, but hard to find the right person for the world. Confucius said: "How great was Yao as a ruler! Only Heaven is great, and only Yao measured up to it. So vast was his virtue that the people could find no name for it! What a ruler Shun was! How lofty — he possessed the world and yet took no personal part in it!" Did Yao and Shun govern the world without using their minds? They simply did not use them on farming.'

六祖大師法寶壇經 · 定慧品第四

有燈即光，無燈即闇。燈是光之體，光是燈之用；名雖有二，體本同一。此定慧法，亦復如是。」

師示眾云：「善知識！一行三昧者，於一切處行住坐臥，常行一直心是也。《淨名》云：『直心是道場，直心是淨土。』莫心行諂曲，口但說直；口說一行三昧，不行直心。但行直心，於一切法勿有執著。迷人著法相、執一行三昧，直言：『常坐不動，妄不起心，即是一行三昧。』作此解者，即同無情，却是障道因緣。

'When there is a lamp, there is light; without a lamp, there is darkness. The lamp is the substance of light; the light is the function of the lamp. Their names are two, but their substance is fundamentally one. The Dharma of samadhi and prajna is exactly like this.' The Master addressed the assembly: 'Good friends! The single-practice samadhi means that in all places — whether walking, standing, sitting, or lying down — one always acts with a straightforward mind. The Vimalakirti Sutra says: "The straightforward mind is the place of awakening; the straightforward mind is the Pure Land." Do not let your mind engage in crookedness and flattery while your mouth merely speaks of straightforwardness. Do not speak of the single-practice samadhi while failing to act with a straightforward mind. Simply practice the straightforward mind, and do not cling to any dharma whatsoever. The deluded cling to the characteristics of dharmas, fixate on the single-practice samadhi, and declare: "Just sitting still and not moving, not giving rise to false thoughts in the mind — that is the single-practice samadhi." Those who interpret it this way are no different from insentient things, and they actually create an obstruction to the Way.'
