般若波羅蜜多心經

舍利子，是諸法空相，不生不滅，不垢不淨，不增不減。是故空中無色，無受想行識。

Shariputra, all dharmas are marked with emptiness — they are neither born nor destroyed, neither defiled nor pure, neither increasing nor decreasing. Therefore, in emptiness there is no form, no feelings, perceptions, mental formations, or consciousness.

孟子 · 離婁下

孟子曰：「世俗所謂不孝者五：惰其四支，不顧父母之養，一不孝也；博弈好飲酒，不顧父母之養，二不孝也；好貨財，私妻子，不顧父母之養，三不孝也；從耳目之欲，以為父母戮，四不孝也；好勇鬥很，以危父母，五不孝也。章子有一於是乎？夫章子，子父責善而不相遇也。責善，朋友之道也；父子責善，賊恩之大者。夫章子，豈不欲有夫妻子母之屬哉？為得罪於父，不得近。出妻屏子，終身不養焉。其設心以為不若是，是則罪之大者，是則章子已矣。

Mencius said: "There are five things the world conventionally calls unfilial: Laziness of limb, neglecting the support of one's parents — this is the first. Indulgence in chess and drinking, neglecting the support of one's parents — this is the second. Love of wealth and favoritism toward wife and children, neglecting the support of one's parents — this is the third. Indulging the desires of the eyes and ears, bringing disgrace upon one's parents — this is the fourth. Being fond of bravery and fighting, thereby endangering one's parents — this is the fifth. Does Zhang have any of these? Zhang and his father fell out over the insistence on goodness between them. Insisting on goodness is the way of friends; between father and son, insisting on goodness is the greatest injury to affection. Did Zhang not wish to have the bonds of husband and wife, parent and child? But having offended his father, he could not draw near, so he sent away his wife, put aside his son, and to the end of his life received no care from them. In his heart he felt that to do anything less would be an even greater wrong. Such is the case of Zhang."

六祖大師法寶壇經 · 頓漸品第八

自南北分化，二宗主雖亡彼我，而徒侶競起愛憎。時北宗門人，自立秀師為第六祖，而忌祖師傳衣為天下聞，乃囑行昌來刺師。師心通，預知其事，即置金十兩於座間。時夜暮，行昌入祖室，將欲加害。師舒頸就之，行昌揮刃者三，悉無所損。師曰：「正劍不邪，邪劍不正。只負汝金，不負汝命。」行昌驚仆，久而方蘇，求哀悔過，即願出家。師遂與金，言：「汝且去，恐徒眾翻害於汝。汝可他日易形而來，吾當攝受。」行昌稟旨宵遁。

The monk Zhiche, a native of Jiangxi, was originally surnamed Zhang with the given name Xingchang. In his youth he was a man of martial daring. Since the division into Southern and Northern schools, although the two founders themselves were free of rivalry, their followers increasingly gave rise to attachment and aversion. At that time the followers of the Northern school, having on their own authority established Master Shenxiu as the Sixth Patriarch, resented that the Patriarch's transmission of the robe was known throughout the land. So they dispatched Xingchang to assassinate the Master. The Master, whose mind was penetrating, foreknew this matter, and placed ten taels of gold beside his seat. That night, Xingchang entered the Patriarch's chamber intending to do harm. The Master extended his neck to receive the blade. Xingchang swung the sword three times, but caused no injury whatsoever. The Master said: 'A righteous sword does not cut crookedly; a crooked sword cannot cut rightly. I owe you gold, not my life.' Xingchang fell down in shock and lay unconscious for a long time before reviving. He begged for mercy and repented, vowing to leave home and become a monk. The Master gave him the gold and said: 'You should go now — I fear the assembly may turn and harm you. Come another day in a different guise, and I will accept you.' Xingchang followed these instructions and fled into the night.
