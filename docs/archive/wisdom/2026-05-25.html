Mencius said: "Their ways were different. Bo Yi would serve no lord who was not his ideal, and would employ no people who were not his ideal; he advanced when there was good order and withdrew when there was chaos. Yi Yin said: 'Whom may I not serve as lord? Whom may I not employ as subjects?' He advanced in times of order and also advanced in times of chaos. Confucius would serve when it was appropriate to serve, would stop when it was appropriate to stop, would stay long when it was appropriate to stay, and would leave quickly when it was appropriate to leave. All three were ancient sages. I have not yet been able to practice what any of them did, but what I aspire to is to learn from Confucius." Gongsun Chou asked: "Are Bo Yi and Yi Yin in the same class as Confucius?" Mencius said: "No. Since the beginning of humankind, there has never been another Confucius." Gongsun Chou asked: "Then did they share anything in common?" Mencius said: "Yes. If given a territory of a hundred li to rule, they could all have brought the feudal lords to court and possessed the empire. And if it required committing a single unjust act or killing a single innocent person to gain the empire, none of them would have done it. In this they were the same."

After Shenxiu finished composing his verse, he tried to present it several times. Each time he walked to the hall, his mind became dazed, his whole body broke out in sweat, and he could not bring himself to submit it. Over four days, he attempted thirteen times without success. Then Shenxiu thought, 'It would be better to write it on the corridor wall and let the Master see it for himself. If he says it is good, I will come forward, bow, and say that I, Shenxiu, wrote it. If he says it is no good, then I have wasted years in these mountains receiving people's reverence for nothing — and what further cultivation should I pursue?' That night, at the third watch, telling no one, he took a lamp and wrote his verse on the wall of the south corridor, expressing what his mind had seen. The verse said: The body is the bodhi tree, / The mind is like a bright mirror's stand. / At all times diligently polish it — / Do not let it gather dust. After writing the verse, Shenxiu returned to his room, and no one knew.
