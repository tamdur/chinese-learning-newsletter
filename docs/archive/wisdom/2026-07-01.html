般若波羅蜜多心經

舍利子，是諸法空相，不生不滅，不垢不淨，不增不減。是故空中無色，無受想行識。

Shariputra, all dharmas are marked with emptiness — they are neither born nor destroyed, neither defiled nor pure, neither increasing nor decreasing. Therefore, in emptiness there is no form, no feelings, perceptions, mental formations, or consciousness.

孟子・滕文公上

滕文公為世子，將之楚，過宋而見孟子。孟子道性善，言必稱堯舜。世子自楚反，復見孟子。孟子曰：「世子疑吾言乎？夫道一而已矣。成覸謂齊景公曰：『彼丈夫也，我丈夫也，吾何畏彼哉？』顏淵曰：『舜何人也？予何人也？有為者亦若是。』公明儀曰：『文王我師也，周公豈欺我哉？』今滕，絕長補短，將五十里也，猶可以為善國。《書》曰：『若藥不瞑眩，厥疾不瘳。』」

When Duke Wen of Teng was still heir apparent, he was traveling to Chu and passed through Song, where he met Mencius. Mencius spoke to him about the goodness of human nature, and in every instance cited Yao and Shun as examples. On his way back from Chu, the heir apparent visited Mencius again. Mencius said, 'Do you doubt my words? The Way is one and only one. Cheng Jian said to Duke Jing of Qi: "He is a man and I am a man — why should I be in awe of him?" Yan Yuan said: "What sort of man was Shun? What sort of man am I? Anyone who makes the effort can become like him." Gongming Yi said: "King Wen is my teacher — how could the Duke of Zhou deceive me?" Now Teng, if you trim the long and patch the short, has a territory of about fifty li square. It can still become a good state. The Book of Documents says: "If the medicine does not make you dizzy and nauseous, the illness will not be cured."'

六祖大師法寶壇經・般若品第二

色類自有道，各不相妨惱，離道別覓道，終身不見道。波波度一生，到頭還自懊，欲得見真道，行正即是道。自若無道心，暗行不見道，若真修道人，不見世間過。若見他人非，自非却是左，他非我不非，我非自有過。但自却非心，打除煩惱破，憎愛不關心，長伸兩腳臥。欲擬化他人，自須有方便，勿令彼有疑，即是自性現。佛法在世間，不離世間覺，離世覓菩提，恰如求兔角。正見名出世，邪見名世間，邪正盡打却，菩提性宛然。

'Each form and kind has its own path, none obstructing or troubling the others. If you leave the Way to seek the Way elsewhere, you will never see the Way in your entire life. Rushing busily through this one life, in the end you will only regret it. If you wish to see the true Way, right action is itself the Way. If you yourself lack the mind of the Way, you walk in darkness and cannot see it. If you are a person who truly cultivates the Way, you do not see the faults of the world. If you see the wrongs of others, your own finding of fault is itself wrong. Others' wrongs are not my wrong; my own faults are my own transgressions. Simply rid yourself of the fault-finding mind, and shatter afflictions completely. When neither hate nor love concerns the mind, stretch out both legs and lie down at ease. If you wish to guide and transform others, you must have skillful means. Do not cause them to have doubts — that is when self-nature manifests. The Buddha-dharma is in the world, and is never apart from worldly awakening. To leave the world seeking bodhi is like looking for rabbit horns. Right views are called transcending the world; wrong views are called worldly. When both wrong and right are completely cast away, the bodhi-nature is perfectly revealed.'
