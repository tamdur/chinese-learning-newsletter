般若波羅蜜多心經

無罣礙故，無有恐怖，遠離顛倒夢想，究竟涅槃。三世諸佛，依般若波羅蜜多故，得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。

Without hindrance, there is no fear. Far from all inverted views, one attains nirvana. All buddhas of past, present, and future rely on Prajna Paramita and attain supreme, perfect enlightenment.

孟子・離婁上

孟子曰：「事孰為大？事親為大；守孰為大？守身為大。不失其身而能事其親者，吾聞之矣；失其身而能事其親者，吾未之聞也。孰不為事？事親，事之本也；孰不為守？守身，守之本也。曾子養曾皙，必有酒肉。將徹，必請所與。問有餘，必曰『有』。曾皙死，曾元養曾子，必有酒肉。將徹，不請所與。問有餘，曰『亡矣』。將以復進也。此所謂養口體者也。若曾子，則可謂養志也。事親若曾子者，可也。」

Mencius said: "Of all forms of service, which is the greatest? Serving one's parents is the greatest. Of all things to guard, which is the greatest? Guarding one's own integrity is the greatest. I have heard of those who, without losing their integrity, were able to serve their parents well; but I have never heard of anyone who, having lost his integrity, was able to serve his parents well. What duty does one not perform? But serving one's parents is the root of all duties. What does one not guard? But guarding one's integrity is the root of all that must be guarded. When Zengzi cared for his father Zeng Xi, there was always wine and meat. When the dishes were about to be cleared, he always asked to whom the leftovers should be given. When asked if there was any left over, he always answered 'Yes.' After Zeng Xi died, when Zeng Yuan cared for Zengzi, there was always wine and meat. But when the dishes were about to be cleared, he did not ask to whom the leftovers should be given. When asked if there was any left over, he answered 'No'—intending to serve the same food again. This is what is called nourishing the mouth and body. Zengzi's way may be called nourishing the will. To serve one's parents as Zengzi did—that is acceptable."

‘If this is so, then all dharmas are suppressed and constrained by nirvana—they cannot even arise, so what bliss could there be?’ The Master said: ‘You are a disciple of the Buddha—why have you adopted the heterodox views of eternalism and annihilationism from non-Buddhist teachings, and presume to discuss the Supreme Vehicle Dharma? According to what you have said, there is a Dharma body separate from the physical body, and one must leave arising-and-ceasing to seek quiescent cessation. Furthermore, you infer that nirvana is permanent bliss and say there is a body that experiences it. This is clinging to birth-and-death and indulging in worldly pleasures. You should now understand: the Buddha taught this because all deluded people take the union of the five aggregates as their own self, distinguish all dharmas as external sense-objects, crave life and loathe death, drift from thought to thought, not knowing that all is like a dream or illusion, and vainly undergo the cycle of rebirth. They turn the permanent bliss of nirvana into a mark of suffering, and all day long they chase after things. Out of compassion for this, the Buddha revealed the true bliss of nirvana.’
