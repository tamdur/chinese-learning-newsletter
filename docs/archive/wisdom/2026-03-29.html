般若波羅蜜多心經 — 週日

故知般若波羅蜜多，是大神咒，是大明咒，是無上咒，是無等等咒，能除一切苦，真實不虛。故說般若波羅蜜多咒，即說咒曰：揭諦揭諦，波羅揭諦，波羅僧揭諦，菩提薩婆訶。

Therefore, know that Prajna Paramita is the great transcendent mantra, the great bright mantra, the supreme mantra, the unequalled mantra, which can remove all suffering and is true, not false. Therefore he proclaimed the Prajna Paramita mantra, saying: Gate gate paragate parasamgate bodhi svaha.

孟子 — 梁惠王上

孟子對曰：「賢者而後樂此，不賢者雖有此，不樂也。《詩》云：『經始靈臺，經之營之，庶民攻之，不日成之。經始勿亟，庶民子來。王在靈囿，麀鹿攸伏，麀鹿濯濯，白鳥鶴鶴。王在靈沼，於牣魚躍。』文王以民力為臺為沼。而民歡樂之，謂其臺曰靈臺，謂其沼曰靈沼，樂其有麋鹿魚鼈。古之人與民偕樂，故能樂也。《湯誓》曰：『時日害喪？予及女偕亡。』民欲與之偕亡，雖有臺池鳥獸，豈能獨樂哉？」

Mencius replied, 'Only the worthy can truly take pleasure in such things. Those who are not worthy, even if they possess them, cannot find true pleasure. The Book of Odes says: "He began the Spirit Tower, planning and measuring it; the common people worked on it and completed it in no time. He began it without haste, yet the people came as if they were his children. The king was in the Spirit Park, where the does lay at rest; the does were sleek and glossy, the white birds glistened. The king was at the Spirit Pond, where the fish leapt in abundance." King Wen used the labor of the people to build his tower and pond, yet the people delighted in it, calling his tower the Spirit Tower and his pond the Spirit Pond, rejoicing that he had deer, fish, and turtles. The ancients shared their pleasures with the people, and therefore could truly enjoy them. The Declaration of Tang says: "O sun, when will you perish? I am willing to perish together with you!" When the people wished to perish together with their ruler, even though he had towers, ponds, birds, and beasts, how could he enjoy them alone?'

信心銘 — Faith in Mind

不好勞神何用疏親欲取一乘勿惡六塵六塵不惡還同正覺智者無為愚人自縛法無異法妄自愛著將心用心豈非大錯迷生寂亂悟無好惡一切二邊良由斟酌夢幻空華何勞把捉得失是非一時放卻眼若不眠諸夢自除心若不異萬法一如一如體玄兀爾忘緣萬法齊觀歸復自然泯其所以不可方比止動無動動止無止兩既不成一何有爾究竟窮極不存軌則

It is no good to weary the spirit —
what use is there in estrangement or intimacy?
If you wish to travel the One Vehicle,
do not despise the six sense-objects.

The six sense-objects, not despised,
become identical with true awakening.
The wise act without contrivance;
the foolish bind themselves.

Dharma has no separate dharmas;
delusion creates its own attachments.
Using the mind to grasp the mind —
is this not the great mistake?

From confusion arise stillness and turmoil;
in awakening there is no good or bad.
All dualities
come from deliberate thinking.

Dreams, illusions, flowers in the sky —
why trouble yourself to grasp them?
Gain and loss, right and wrong —
let them all go at once.

If the eyes never sleep,
all dreams naturally cease.
If the mind makes no distinctions,
the myriad things are one suchness.

One suchness is profound in its essence,
unmoving, free from conditions.
Contemplate the myriad things as equal
and you return to your natural state.

When their reasons are dissolved,
no comparisons can be made.
Stop movement and there is no movement;
set movement going and there is no stillness.

When both cease to exist,
how can the one stand alone?
Pursue this to the ultimate end:
no rules or measures remain.
