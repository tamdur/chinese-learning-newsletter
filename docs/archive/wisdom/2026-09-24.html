般若波羅蜜多心經

無眼耳鼻舌身意，無色聲香味觸法，無眼界，乃至無意識界。無無明，亦無無明盡，乃至無老死，亦無老死盡。

There are no eyes, ears, nose, tongue, body, or mind; no sights, sounds, smells, tastes, touches, or objects of mind; no realm of sight, and so on up to no realm of consciousness. There is no ignorance and no end of ignorance, and so on up to no old age and death and no end of old age and death.

孟子・萬章上

曰：「使之主祭而百神享之，是天受之；使之主事而事治，百姓安之，是民受之也。天與之，人與之，故曰：天子不能以天下與人。舜相堯二十有八載，非人之所能為也，天也。堯崩，三年之喪畢，舜避堯之子於南河之南。天下諸侯朝覲者，不之堯之子而之舜；訟獄者，不之堯之子而之舜；謳歌者，不謳歌堯之子而謳歌舜，故曰天也。夫然後之中國，踐天子位焉。而居堯之宮，逼堯之子，是篡也，非天與也。《太誓》曰：『天視自我民視，天聽自我民聽』，此之謂也。」

Mencius said: "Yao had Shun preside over the sacrifices, and all the spirits received them with satisfaction — this was Heaven accepting him. He had Shun preside over affairs, and affairs were well governed and the people were at peace — this was the people accepting him. Heaven gave it to him, and the people gave it to him. That is why I say the Son of Heaven cannot give the realm to another person. Shun served as Yao's minister for twenty-eight years — this was not something any human could have brought about; it was Heaven. When Yao died and the three years of mourning were complete, Shun withdrew from Yao's son to the south of the Southern River. Yet the feudal lords of the realm who came to pay court went not to Yao's son but to Shun. Those with legal disputes went not to Yao's son but to Shun. Those who sang songs of praise sang not of Yao's son but of Shun. Therefore I say it was Heaven. Only after this did Shun go to the central kingdom and assume the position of Son of Heaven. Had he simply occupied Yao's palace and pushed aside Yao's son, that would have been usurpation, not the gift of Heaven. The 'Great Declaration' says: 'Heaven sees through the eyes of my people; Heaven hears through the ears of my people.' This is what it means."

六祖大師法寶壇經・付囑品第十

法相語言十二對：語與法對，有與無對，有色與無色對，有相與無相對，有漏與無漏對，色與空對，動與靜對，清與濁對，凡與聖對，僧與俗對，老與少對，大與小對；此是十二對也。自性起用十九對：長與短對，邪與正對，癡與慧對，愚與智對，亂與定對，慈與毒對，戒與非對，直與曲對，實與虛對，險與平對，煩惱與菩提對，常與無常對，悲與害對，喜與瞋對，捨與慳對，進與退對，生與滅對，法身與色身對，化身與報身對；此是十九對也。

'For dharma-characteristics and language there are twelve pairs: speech and dharma, existence and non-existence, form and formlessness, having characteristics and having no characteristics, conditioned and unconditioned, form and emptiness, movement and stillness, purity and turbidity, ordinary and sagely, monastic and lay, old and young, great and small — these are the twelve pairs. For self-nature's own functioning there are nineteen pairs: long and short, deviant and correct, delusion and wisdom, foolishness and intelligence, disorder and concentration, compassion and cruelty, morality and immorality, straightness and crookedness, truth and falsehood, danger and safety, affliction and bodhi, permanence and impermanence, pity and harm, joy and anger, renunciation and miserliness, advance and retreat, arising and ceasing, the Dharmakaya and the form body, the transformation body and the reward body — these are the nineteen pairs.'
