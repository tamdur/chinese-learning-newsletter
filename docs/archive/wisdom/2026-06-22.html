般若波羅蜜多心經

觀自在菩薩，行深般若波羅蜜多時，照見五蘊皆空，度一切苦厄。

Avalokitesvara Bodhisattva, when practicing the profound Prajna Paramita, illuminated the five aggregates and saw that they are all empty, thus overcoming all suffering and distress.

孟子 · 公孫丑下

曰：「周公使管叔監殷，管叔以殷畔。知而使之，是不仁也；不知而使之，是不智也。仁智，周公未之盡也，而況於王乎？賈請見而解之。」見孟子，問曰：「周公何人也？」曰：「古聖人也。」曰：「使管叔監殷，管叔以殷畔也，有諸？」曰：「然。」曰：「周公知其將畔而使之與？」曰：「不知也。」「然則聖人且有過與？」

Chen Jia said: "The Duke of Zhou appointed his brother Guan Shu to oversee the Yin remnants, and Guan Shu used the Yin people to rebel. If the Duke of Zhou knew he would rebel and still appointed him, that was a lack of benevolence. If he did not know, that was a lack of wisdom. Even the Duke of Zhou did not fully attain benevolence and wisdom — how much less can be expected of Your Majesty? Allow me to go see Mencius and explain this." He went to see Mencius and asked: "What sort of man was the Duke of Zhou?" Mencius said: "A sage of antiquity." "He appointed Guan Shu to oversee the Yin remnants, and Guan Shu used them to rebel — is this true?" "It is." "Did the Duke of Zhou know he would rebel when he appointed him?" "He did not know." "Then even a sage makes mistakes?"

六祖大師法寶壇經 · 般若品第二

不修即凡；一念修行，自身等佛。善知識！凡夫即佛，煩惱即菩提。前念迷即凡夫，後念悟即佛。前念著境即煩惱，後念離境即菩提。

「善知識！摩訶般若波羅蜜，最尊最上最第一，無住無往亦無來，三世諸佛從中出。當用大智慧，打破五蘊煩惱塵勞。如此修行，定成佛道，變三毒為戒定慧。善知識！我此法門，從一般若生八萬四千智慧。何以故？為世人有八萬四千塵勞。若無塵勞，智慧常現，不離自性。

'Those who do not cultivate remain ordinary beings; with a single thought of cultivation, one is equal to the Buddha. Good friends, ordinary beings are themselves Buddha; afflictions are themselves bodhi. The previous thought, if deluded, is that of an ordinary being; the next thought, if awakened, is that of a Buddha. The previous thought, if attached to sense-objects, is affliction; the next thought, if free from sense-objects, is bodhi.' 'Good friends, Mahaprajnaparamita is the most honored, the most supreme, the foremost. It neither abides, nor goes, nor comes. All buddhas of the three times emerge from it. One should use great wisdom to shatter the afflictions and defilements of the five aggregates. Practicing in this way, one will certainly attain buddhahood, transforming the three poisons into morality, concentration, and wisdom. Good friends, in my Dharma-gate, from one prajna, eighty-four thousand wisdoms are born. Why? Because worldly people have eighty-four thousand defilements. If there were no defilements, wisdom would always be manifest, never separate from one's own nature.'
