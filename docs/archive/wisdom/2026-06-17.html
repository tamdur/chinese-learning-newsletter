般若波羅蜜多心經

舍利子，是諸法空相，不生不滅，不垢不淨，不增不減。是故空中無色，無受想行識。

Shariputra, all dharmas are marked with emptiness — they are neither born nor destroyed, neither defiled nor pure, neither increasing nor decreasing. Therefore, in emptiness there is no form, no feelings, perceptions, mental formations, or consciousness.

孟子 · 公孫丑下

他日，見於王曰：「王之為都者，臣知五人焉。知其罪者，惟孔距心。為王誦之。」王曰：「此則寡人之罪也。」孟子謂蚔鼃曰：「子之辭靈丘而請士師，似也，為其可以言也。今既數月矣，未可以言與？」蚔鼃諫於王而不用，致為臣而去。齊人曰：「所以為蚔鼃，則善矣；所以自為，則吾不知也。」公都子以告。曰：「吾聞之也：有官守者，不得其職則去；有言責者，不得其言則去。我無官守，我無言責也，則吾進退，豈不綽綽然有餘裕哉？」

Another day, Mencius had an audience with the king and said: "Among Your Majesty's city governors, I know five. The only one who recognizes his own failings is Kong Juxin." He recounted the conversation for the king. The king said: "In that case, the fault is mine." Mencius then said to Chi Wa: "You resigned from the governorship of Lingqiu and requested the post of Chief Justice — that seemed reasonable, since the position would give you the standing to speak up. Now several months have passed — have you still not been able to speak?" Chi Wa remonstrated with the king but was ignored, so he resigned his office and left. The people of Qi said: "What he did for Chi Wa was well done, but what he did for himself — that we do not understand." When Gongduzi reported this to Mencius, he said: "I have heard it said: He who holds an office leaves when he cannot fulfill its duties; he who bears the responsibility of counsel leaves when his words go unheeded. I hold no office and bear no responsibility for counsel — so in my comings and goings, am I not perfectly free and at ease?"

六祖大師法寶壇經 · 般若品第二

次日，韋使君請益。師陞座，告大眾曰：「總淨心念摩訶般若波羅蜜多。」復云：「善知識！菩提般若之智，世人本自有之；只緣心迷，不能自悟，須假大善知識，示導見性。當知愚人智人，佛性本無差別，只緣迷悟不同，所以有愚有智。吾今為說摩訶般若波羅蜜法，使汝等各得智慧。志心諦聽！吾為汝說。善知識！世人終日口念般若，不識自性般若，猶如說食不飽。口但說空，萬劫不得見性，終無有益。

The next day, Magistrate Wei requested further teaching. The Master ascended the platform and addressed the assembly: 'All of you, purify your minds and recite Mahaprajnaparamita.' He continued: 'Good friends, the wisdom of bodhi-prajna is something all people inherently possess. It is only because the mind is deluded that one cannot awaken to it oneself, and must rely on a great good teacher to show the way and reveal one's true nature. You should understand that the buddha-nature of the foolish and the wise is fundamentally no different. It is only because delusion and awakening differ that some are foolish and some are wise. I will now teach you the Mahaprajnaparamita Dharma, so that each of you may attain wisdom. Listen carefully with sincere minds, and I will explain it for you. Good friends, people in the world recite "prajna" all day long, yet do not recognize the prajna of their own nature. This is like talking about food but never being full. If your mouth merely speaks of emptiness, you will not see your true nature even in ten thousand kalpas — in the end it brings no benefit.'
