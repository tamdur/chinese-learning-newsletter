般若波羅蜜多心經

無苦集滅道，無智亦無得。以無所得故，菩提薩埵，依般若波羅蜜多故，心無罣礙。

There is no suffering, no cause of suffering, no cessation, no path; no wisdom and no attainment. With nothing to attain, a bodhisattva relies on Prajna Paramita, and the mind is without hindrance.

孟子·告子下

曰：「《凱風》，親之過小者也；《小弁》，親之過大者也。親之過大而不怨，是愈疏也；親之過小而怨，是不可磯也。愈疏，不孝也；不可磯，亦不孝也。孔子曰：『舜其至孝矣，五十而慕。』」宋牼將之楚，孟子遇於石丘。曰：「先生將何之？」曰：「吾聞秦楚構兵，我將見楚王說而罷之。楚王不悅，我將見秦王說而罷之，二王我將有所遇焉。」曰：「軻也請無問其詳，願聞其指。說之將何如？」曰：「我將言其不利也。」

Mencius said: '"Kai Feng" deals with a minor fault of a parent; "Xiao Bian" deals with a major fault. Not to resent a major fault in a parent is to grow more distant from them. To resent a minor fault is to be overly sensitive. Growing more distant is unfilial; being overly sensitive is also unfilial. Confucius said: "Shun was the most filial of all—at fifty he still yearned for his parents."' Song Keng was on his way to Chu. Mencius met him at Shiqiu and asked: 'Where are you going, sir?' Song Keng replied: 'I have heard that Qin and Chu are waging war. I intend to see the King of Chu and persuade him to stop. If the King of Chu is not receptive, I shall see the King of Qin and persuade him. Of the two kings, I shall find one I can work with.' Mencius said: 'I will not ask for details, but I would like to hear the gist of your approach. How will you persuade them?' Song Keng said: 'I shall explain to them that war is unprofitable.'

金剛般若波羅蜜經·化無所化分第二十五

「須菩提！於意云何？汝等勿謂如來作是念：『我當度眾生。』須菩提！莫作是念。何以故？實無有眾生如來度者。若有眾生如來度者，如來則有我、人、眾生、壽者。須菩提！如來說：『有我者，則非有我，而凡夫之人以為有我。』須菩提！凡夫者，如來說則非凡夫，是名凡夫。」

"Subhuti, what do you think? You should not say that the Tathagata has this thought: 'I shall deliver sentient beings.' Subhuti, do not think this way. Why? There are in truth no sentient beings for the Tathagata to deliver. If there were sentient beings for the Tathagata to deliver, then the Tathagata would have a self, a person, a being, and one who lives a lifespan. Subhuti, when the Tathagata speaks of 'having a self,' it is not truly having a self, yet ordinary people take it as having a self. Subhuti, as for ordinary people — the Tathagata says they are not ordinary people; they are merely called ordinary people."
