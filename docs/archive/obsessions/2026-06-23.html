被遺忘的電子四季：陳揚的超前之作

來源：FOND/SOUND

一九八七年，台北作曲家陳揚推出了專輯《現代四季》。他用當時最新的電子樂器（合成器和取樣機），把台灣的旋律疊在迷幻的音色上。

這張專輯原本是為雲門舞集寫的配樂。像〈稻〉這樣的曲子，聽起來像早期的電子舞曲。它比台灣公認的電子音樂早了將近十年。

In 1987, Taipei composer Chen Yang released the album "Modern Four Seasons." Using the latest electronic instruments of the time—synthesizers and samplers—he layered Taiwanese melodies over psychedelic timbres. The album was originally composed as a soundtrack for the Cloud Gate Dance Theatre. Pieces like "Rice" sound like early electronic dance music. It predated Taiwan's recognized electronic music scene by nearly a decade.

大地是我們的孩子：極北的薩米博物館

來源：thisisFINLAND

在芬蘭最北邊的伊納里，離首都有一千公里遠。這裡有一座薩米博物館（薩米人是當地的原住民），二零二二年重新開放。

新展覽叫作「這片大地是我們的孩子」，由三百多位族人一起完成。館內收藏超過一萬件物品，是三月到拉普蘭的旅人最值得一看的地方。

In Inari, Finland's northernmost town, a thousand kilometers from the capital, sits the Sámi Museum (the Sámi are the region's indigenous people), which reopened in 2022. The new exhibition, titled "These Lands Are Our Children," was created collaboratively by over 300 community members. Housing more than 10,000 artifacts, it is a must-see destination for travelers visiting Lapland in March.

親手澆鑄金屬：芝加哥的翻砂課

來源：Chicago Industrial Arts & Design Center

芝加哥工業藝術設計中心是一間不收營利的工作坊。六月二十五日起，每週六早上九點，他們開了一門為期五週的翻砂鑄造課。

學生會把砂壓進模子裡，再倒入熔化的金屬，做出自己的鋼鐵作品。這種手藝已經有幾千年歷史，十六歲以上的人都能報名。

The Chicago Industrial Arts & Design Center is a nonprofit workshop. Starting June 25th, they are offering a five-week sand-casting course every Saturday morning at 9 a.m. Students will pack sand into molds, then pour molten metal to create their own steel works. This ancient craft, with roots stretching back thousands of years, is open to anyone sixteen and older.

兩千年的調音難題：一頁音樂歷史

來源：kylegann.com

作曲家凱爾．甘恩做了一個網頁，記下人類兩千年來怎麼把樂器調準。問題在於：用純數學算出的音，沒辦法剛好湊滿一個八度。

這會留下一個特別走音的音程，叫做「狼音」。這一頁講解了每一種解決辦法，也說明了為什麼巴哈的曲子在不同調上聽起來不一樣。網頁上還能聽到聲音範例。

Composer Kyle Gann created a webpage documenting how humanity has tuned musical instruments over the past two thousand years. The problem is this: intervals calculated through pure mathematics don't divide an octave evenly. This leaves behind one particularly out-of-tune interval, called the "wolf tone." The page explains each solution attempted throughout history and reveals why Bach's compositions sound different depending on what key they're played in. The webpage also includes audio examples you can listen to.
