電線之間，長出一座島的聲音

來源：ato.archives (Bandcamp)

二○二五年七月十八日，日本廠牌 ato.archives 發行了合輯《Pulses on the Horizon》。九首曲子，第一次把台灣的模組合成器（用電線把聲音機器一台一台接起來的樂器）玩家，聚在同一張唱片裡。

策劃的人是住在台灣的 Ryan J Raffa。裡面有 weiii，他從畫畫走進聲音，把實地錄下的風聲水聲疊進機器；也有廖海廷，本來是打擊樂手，後來開了台北的 PrintedNoise Lab。他們不按琴鍵，而是拉線讓電壓自己長出節奏。

On July 18, 2025, the Japanese label ato.archives released a compilation called "Pulses on the Horizon." Nine tracks bring together Taiwanese modular synthesizer players—musicians who connect sound machines together with wires—on a single record for the first time. Ryan J Raffa, who lives in Taiwan, curated the project. The album features weiii, who transitioned from painting to sound and layers field recordings of wind and water into his machines. It also includes Liao Haiting, originally a percussionist, who later founded the PrintedNoise Lab in Taipei. Rather than pressing keys, they pull wires and let the voltage grow its own rhythm.

美國最老的木屋，出自芬蘭人之手

來源：Atlas Obscura

在美國新澤西州的吉布斯敦，有一間名叫 Nothnagle 的小木屋，大約建於一六三八年，是全美國最老的木屋。蓋它的不是英國人，而是「森林芬蘭人」。

他們是新瑞典殖民地的居民，把家鄉東部森林的疊木工法帶到美洲：木頭兩端刻出凹口，一根一根疊起來，不用釘子。後來德國和蘇格蘭裔移民帶著這種蓋法往西走，木屋就成了美國拓荒的象徵。而它的老家，正是芬蘭湖邊小屋的同一個傳統。

In Gibbstown, New Jersey, there stands a small log cabin called Nothnagle, built around 1638 and the oldest log cabin in all of the United States. It was not built by English settlers, but by "forest Finns"—residents of the New Sweden colony who brought with them the log-stacking construction technique from the forests of their homeland. Their method was elegant: carve notches on both ends of each log, stack them one upon another, and use no nails. Later, German and Scottish-descended immigrants carried this building practice westward, and the log cabin became the defining symbol of American frontier expansion. Yet its roots trace back to the same tradition of Finnish lakeside cottages.

一個字一個字撿起來：芝加哥的活字印刷課

來源：Spudnik Press Cooperative

九月二十三日起，烏克蘭村的 Spudnik Press 開了一門八週的晚間課。學生要親手把金屬和木頭做的字，一個一個撿出來排好，鎖進鋼框，再搖動老式的平壓印刷機。

紙上會壓出淺淺的凹痕，是電腦印不出來的觸感。課程不需要任何設計或版畫基礎，八週後，你會排版、調油墨，也能印出好幾層顏色對得整齊的作品。這些機器，比電腦早了一百年。

Starting September 23rd, Spudnik Press in Ukrainian Village is launching an eight-week evening course. Students will hand-pick metal and wooden type, one letter at a time, arrange them neatly, lock them into a steel frame, and then operate an old-fashioned flatbed press. The paper comes away with shallow indentations—a tactile quality that no computer can replicate. No design or printmaking experience is required; by week eight, you'll have mastered typesetting, ink mixing, and printing multi-color work with perfect registration. These machines predate the computer by a hundred years.

三萬五千公里，搶救牆上的馬賽克

來源：Yevgen Nikiforov (personal site)

基輔攝影師 Yevgen Nikiforov 花了三年，開車三萬五千公里，走過烏克蘭一百零九個城鎮，拍下一千多幅蘇聯時期的大型馬賽克（用小玻璃和陶片拼成的牆面畫）。

畫上是工人、太空人和運動員，完成於一九五○到八○年代。二○一五年烏克蘭通過「去共產化」法律後，他拍過的一些作品，幾個月內就被拆掉。於是這個個人網站，成了它們最後留下的樣子。

Kyiv photographer Yevgen Nikiforov spent three years driving 35,000 kilometers across 109 Ukrainian towns, documenting over 1,000 Soviet-era mosaics—wall paintings crafted from tiny glass and ceramic tiles. The works depicted workers, cosmonauts, and athletes, created between the 1950s and 1980s. After Ukraine passed a "decommunization" law in 2015, many of the pieces he photographed were demolished within months. His personal website has become the final record of what they once looked like.
