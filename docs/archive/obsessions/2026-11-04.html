夢東《道火》：走過奈何橋的迷幻之聲

來源：Bandcamp Daily

夢東（Mong Tong）是來自台北的兄弟二人組。2023年，他們在日本唱片公司 Guruguru Brain 發行了專輯《道火》。

他們把東南亞的鑼聲、鼓聲，還有在婚禮和喪禮（送別死去的人的典禮）上錄下的聲音放進音樂裡，再配上迷幻的電吉他。其中一首歌叫〈奈何橋〉，是台灣民間傳說裡，人死後要走過的一座橋。

整張專輯聽起來像夜裡廟口的一場神祕儀式，又古老，又新鮮。

Mong Tong is a sibling duo from Taipei. In 2023, they released the album Dao Huo on Japanese record label Guruguru Brain. They mix the gongs and drums of Southeast Asia and sounds recorded at weddings and funerals (the ceremonies that send off the dead) into their music, then add hazy, psychedelic electric guitar. One song, "Naihe Bridge," is named after a bridge from Taiwanese folk legend that the dead must cross after they die. The whole album sounds like a mysterious night ritual at a temple gate, both ancient and fresh.

塞馬湖海豹：在人堆的雪洞裡出生

來源：This is Finland

芬蘭東部的塞馬湖，住著全世界最少見的淡水（不鹹的水）海豹之一。大約一萬年前，這座湖和大海分開了，牠們的祖先留在湖裡，慢慢學會在淡水中生活。

現在只剩下大約四百隻。每年二月，海豹媽媽會在雪堆裡挖洞生寶寶。可是冬天越來越暖，雪常常不夠。

所以每年冬天，志工會用手堆出幾百個雪堆，讓小海豹有地方躲。三月來到湖區，正是牠們在雪下長大的時候。

In Lake Saimaa in eastern Finland lives one of the rarest freshwater seals in the world, a seal that makes its home in water that is not salty. About ten thousand years ago, the lake was cut off from the sea. The seals' ancestors stayed behind and slowly learned to live in fresh water. Today only about 400 remain. Every February, mother seals dig dens in the snowdrifts to give birth. But winters are growing warmer, and there is often not enough snow. So each winter, volunteers pile up several hundred snow mounds by hand, giving the pups places to hide. By March, when the lake region is in full view, the pups are growing up beneath the snow.

一枝花的線條：湖景區的日本插花課

來源：Japanese Culture Center

日本插花也叫做「花道」，已經有幾百年的歷史。它不是把花插得越多越好，而是看重線條、空白和平衡。

芝加哥湖景區（Lakeview）的日本文化中心，將在11月8日星期日下午2點半，開始一堂六週的插花入門課。

不需要任何經驗，完全的新手也可以參加。在冬天來臨前，學會讓一枝花自己說話。

Japanese flower arranging, also known as kadō or "the way of flowers," has a history of several hundred years. Rather than cramming in as many blooms as possible, it prizes line, negative space, and balance. The Japanese cultural center in Chicago's Lakeview neighborhood will start a six-week introductory ikebana course on Sunday, November 8, at 2:30 p.m. No experience is needed, and complete beginners are welcome. Before winter arrives, learn to let a single flower speak for itself.

世界紅蘿蔔博物館：一個人為一種蔬菜寫的網站

來源：World Carrot Museum

從1996年開始，英國人約翰（John Stolarczyk）一個人做出了「世界紅蘿蔔博物館」。它沒有房子，只是一個網站，卻有超過兩百頁。

在這裡，你會讀到紅蘿蔔原本是白色和紫色的，後來才慢慢變成橘色。第二次世界大戰時，英國政府還用紅蘿蔔做海報，鼓勵大家多吃。

2011年，他還和一位學者一起寫論文，指出「荷蘭人為了紀念王室，才把紅蘿蔔變成橘色」這個說法並不正確。

Starting in 1996, Briton John Stolarczyk built the "World Carrot Museum" largely by himself. It has no building, only a website, but it runs to more than two hundred pages. There you can read that carrots were originally white and purple and only gradually turned orange. During the Second World War, the British government even printed carrot posters to encourage people to eat more of them. In 2011, he co-wrote a paper with an academic arguing that the story about the Dutch turning carrots orange to honor their royal family is not correct.
