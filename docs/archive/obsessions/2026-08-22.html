台灣的聲音：林強把日常錄成音樂

來源：Pure Person Press (Bandcamp)

林強是台灣第一位用台語唱紅的流行歌手，後來成為島上電子音樂的重要人物。二○二○到二○二一年，他帶著錄音機走遍台灣：老糖廠裡搖椅的吱吱聲、西螺大橋上幾千位媽祖信徒的腳步聲、風吹過甘蔗田，還有公共廁所裡的蟋蟀（一種會叫的小蟲）。

二○二五年十一月，這些聲音交給多位電子音樂人重新製作，做成專輯《Sounds of Taiwan》。前後花了四年，畫出一張島嶼的聲音地圖。

Lim Giong was the first pop star in Taiwan to make his name singing in Taiwanese, and later became a central figure in the island's electronic music scene. From 2020 to 2021 he carried a recorder across Taiwan, capturing the creak of a rocking chair in an old sugar factory, the footsteps of thousands of Mazu pilgrims crossing the Xiluo Bridge, wind moving through sugarcane fields, and crickets chirping in a public restroom. In November 2025 these sounds were handed to a group of electronic musicians to be rebuilt into the album Sounds of Taiwan. Four years in the making, it draws a sonic map of the island.

不穿襪子的靴子：北極圈裡的乾草保暖術

來源：Future Footwear Foundation

在芬蘭的拉普蘭，薩米人（北歐的原住民）牧養馴鹿時不穿襪子。他們穿柔軟的鹿皮靴，裡面塞滿曬乾的莎草（一種長在濕地的草），先用手扭成鳥巢的形狀，再包住整隻腳。

草會把腳上的汗吸走，又能留住空氣保暖。就算在零下四十度，牧人還是靠它，據說比現代的化學纖維襪更管用。每天早上把用過的草打鬆、在火邊烤乾，隔天就能再用一次。

In Finnish Lapland, the Sámi — the indigenous people of northern Europe — wear no socks when herding reindeer. They wear soft reindeer-hide boots packed with dried sedge, a grass that grows in wetlands, first twisted by hand into the shape of a bird's nest and then wrapped around the whole foot. The grass draws sweat away from the foot while trapping air for warmth. Even at forty below, herders still rely on it, and it is said to work better than modern synthetic socks. Each morning the used grass is beaten loose and dried by the fire, ready to use again the next day.

大人的分享會：五分鐘說服你，這件事很酷

來源：Eventbrite / Tell Me Why It's Cool Show

二○二六年九月二十日星期日，芝加哥的「大人分享會」回到威克公園的酒吧 Clara。任何人都能上網報名，拿到五分鐘，帶著實物上台，講一件冷門的事為什麼迷人。

以前有人講木工怎麼讓兩塊木頭互相卡住，也有人講九○年代一款指甲油的化學結構。每場講完，觀眾還能發問幾分鐘。這個活動原本只是小酒吧裡的夜間節目，現在已經開到好幾個場地。

On Sunday, September 20th, 2026, Chicago's “Show & Tell for Grown-Ups” returns to Clara, a bar in Wicker Park. Anyone can sign up online for a five-minute slot, bring an object on stage, and explain why some obscure thing is fascinating. Past presenters have covered how woodworkers make two pieces of wood lock into each other, and the chemical structure of a 1990s nail polish. After each talk the audience gets a few minutes to ask questions. The event started as a small night at a neighborhood bar and now runs at several venues.

人造語言總目：收齊世上每一種發明出來的話

來源：The Conlang Database

二○二○年，語言創作者 Matthew McVeagh 開始建一個資料庫，想收下世界上每一種「人造語言」。不只是有名的克林貢語和世界語，還有幾千種業餘愛好者純粹因為喜歡而造出來的語言。

它原本只是一份大家一起填的表格，就已經收了約八百種。後來搬進正式網站，可以查發音、文字、語法和創造者，把散在論壇和部落格裡的心血，整理成一座能翻閱的檔案館。

In 2020, conlanger Matthew McVeagh began building a database meant to hold every constructed language in the world. Not only the famous ones like Klingon and Esperanto, but also thousands built by amateurs purely for the love of it. It started as a shared spreadsheet that anyone could fill in, and had already gathered around eight hundred languages. It later moved to a proper website where you can look up phonology, script, grammar, and creator — turning work scattered across forums and blogs into an archive you can actually browse.
