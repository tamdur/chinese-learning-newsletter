和檢查員玩捉迷藏的歌聲

來源：StoryStudio 故事

一九八三年，台灣還在戒嚴（政府管得很嚴）。羅大佑花了九個月，做出專輯《未來的主人翁》。他把對社會的批評，偷偷藏進歌裡，騙過了檢查的人。

最有名的是〈亞細亞的孤兒〉。那時美國剛和台灣斷交，這首歌像在說台灣的孤單。一把吉他配上尖尖的嗩吶（一種傳統吹奏樂器），聽起來又冷又痛。

In 1983, Taiwan was under martial law, with the government keeping tight control over everything. Luo Dayou spent nine months creating the album "Future Masters." He hid his social criticism inside the songs, slipping past the censors undetected.

The most famous track is "Orphan of Asia." At that time, the United States had just cut diplomatic ties with Taiwan, and the song felt like it was capturing Taiwan's loneliness. A guitar paired with the sharp, piercing sound of a suona—a traditional Chinese wind instrument—created something that felt both cold and painful.

全世界最悲觀的小鎮

來源：NZZ (Neue Zürcher Zeitung)

在芬蘭北邊，有個小鎮叫波蘭卡（Puolanka），只住了大約兩千六百人。很多年來，它的人口一直變少，工作也不好找。於是他們乾脆說：我們是世界上最悲觀（總是往壞處想）的地方。

鎮上開了一間「悲觀之家」，賣著好笑的衣服和徽章。路邊的牌子寫著：「快到了，你還來得及掉頭回去。」這種笑自己的幽默，反而吸引了很多遊客來看。

In northern Finland, there's a small town called Puolanka with about twenty-six hundred residents. For years, its population has steadily declined and jobs are hard to find. So they decided to lean into it: we're the world's most pessimistic place.

The town opened a "House of Pessimism" selling humorous merchandise and badges. Signs along the roadside read: "You're almost here—there's still time to turn back." This self-deprecating humor has paradoxically attracted plenty of curious tourists.

騎單車，走過一段傷心的歷史

來源：Chicago Race Riot of 1919 Commemoration Project

七月二十五日星期六，芝加哥有一場免費的單車導覽，帶大家騎過南邊的社區。一九一九年，一位黑人少年在湖裡游泳，因為越過一條看不見的分界線，被白人丟石頭而淹死。

這件事引發五天的暴動，三十八人因此喪命。兩個半小時的路程會停在幾個重要的地點，也說說黑人如何在這裡重新建立自己的家園。任何人都能參加。

On Saturday, July 25th, Chicago is hosting a free bike tour through neighborhoods on the South Side. In 1919, a Black teenager was swimming in the lake when he crossed an invisible boundary line. White people threw rocks at him, and he drowned.

The incident sparked five days of rioting that claimed thirty-eight lives. The two-and-a-half-hour ride will stop at several significant locations and tell the story of how Black residents rebuilt their lives and community here. Anyone can participate.

一個人，把機械錶拆給你看

來源：ciechanow.ski (Bartosz Ciechanowski)

波蘭工程師齊哈諾夫斯基（Bartosz Ciechanowski）有個自己的網站。在那裡，他寫機械錶、齒輪、單車和相機是怎麼動的。和別人不同的是，他的圖都會動，你還能用滑鼠親手去轉動它們。

在〈機械錶〉這篇裡，你可以轉動錶的旋鈕，看著裡面的零件一起跳動，明白三百年前的錶到底怎麼走。這個網站沒有廣告，也沒有團隊，只是一個人多年的熱情。

Polish engineer Bartosz Ciechanowski has his own website. There, he writes about how mechanical watches, gears, bicycles, and cameras work. What sets him apart is that his diagrams are interactive — you can use your mouse to manipulate them directly.

In his article on mechanical watches, you can turn the watch's crown and watch the internal components move in sync, understanding exactly how timepieces from three hundred years ago functioned. The website carries no advertisements and has no team behind it — just one man's years of passion.
