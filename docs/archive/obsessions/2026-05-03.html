林強的台語搖滾革命

來源：Bite-size Taiwanese

一九九○年十二月，台灣的流行音樂幾乎全是國語慢歌。一個叫林強的年輕人決定用台語唱歌，加上嘻哈鼓機和電吉他，發行了專輯《向前走》。

同名歌曲講的是一個年輕人到台北追夢的故事，賣了超過四十萬張，還得了金曲獎年度歌曲。在台語還被壓抑（不被鼓勵使用）的年代，這張專輯打開了「新台語歌運動」的大門。

In December 1990, Taiwan's pop music scene was dominated almost entirely by Mandarin ballads. A young man named Lin Qiang decided to sing in Taiwanese, pairing it with hip-hop drum machines and electric guitar, and released the album "Moving Forward."

The title track tells the story of a young person chasing dreams in Taipei and sold over 400,000 copies, earning Song of the Year at the Golden Melody Awards. At a time when Taiwanese was still suppressed and discouraged, this album opened the door to the "New Taiwanese Language Song Movement."

通往靈界的冰河湖

來源：National Parks Finland

在芬蘭西北方的拉普蘭，有一個叫 Äkässaivo 的冰河湖。湖的四面是很陡的石壁，薩米族（北歐原住民）相信湖底有一個小洞，可以通到靈界。

湖邊有一塊三十公尺高的聖石，幾百年來人們在這裡放肉、魚和錢幣給神靈。三月時可以穿雪鞋走一條短步道到達，一個下午就能來回。

In Lapland, northwestern Finland, there is a glacial lake called Äkässaivo. The lake is surrounded by steep cliff walls on all sides, and the Sámi people—the indigenous inhabitants of Northern Europe—believe that a small opening at the lake's bottom leads to the spirit world.

On the shore stands a sacred stone thirty meters high. For centuries, people have left meat, fish, and coins here as offerings to the spirits. In March, you can reach it via snowshoes on a short trail—the round trip takes just an afternoon.

城市裡的野菜散步

來源：Eventbrite / Odd Produce

五月十日星期天，芝加哥唯一的專業採集人 Dave Odd 會帶大家在林肯公園動物園附近散步兩小時，認識超過五十種可以吃的野生植物和蘑菇。

他平時用公司 Odd Produce 供應野菜給全市的餐廳。每人四十美元，母親節當天媽媽免費。想看看城市裡藏了多少食物嗎？

On Sunday, May 10th, Dave Odd, Chicago's only professional forager, will lead a two-hour walk near the Lincoln Park Zoo, introducing over fifty species of edible wild plants and mushrooms.

Through his company Odd Produce, he regularly supplies foraged vegetables to restaurants across the city. The walk costs forty dollars per person, with free admission for mothers on Mother's Day. Curious to discover how much food is hiding in the city?

一個人蓋的中世紀動物百科

來源：bestiary.ca

加拿大學者 David Badke 從二○○二年開始，一個人建了一個網站，收集中世紀歐洲「動物寓言書」裡的奇怪記錄。比如古人相信鵜鶘（一種大鳥）會用自己的血救活死掉的小鳥。

這個網站現在有三千多頁，連結了幾百本古書和手繪插圖。他甚至自己寫程式來管理資料。二十多年的熱情，全在 bestiary.ca 上。

Canadian scholar David Badke began building a website in 2002, single-handedly collecting strange medieval European records from "bestiaries" — illustrated books about animals. For example, ancient texts claimed that pelicans (large birds) would revive their dead chicks using their own blood.

The website now contains over three thousand pages, linked to hundreds of ancient texts and hand-drawn illustrations. Badke even wrote his own code to manage the database. Two decades of passion are preserved at bestiary.ca.
