被遺忘的台灣迪斯可

來源：Spectrum Culture

一九七〇年代末，台灣有一群女歌手，在放克和迪斯可（一種跳舞音樂）的節奏裡大聲唱歌。這些歌後來被一首首慢情歌蓋過，幾乎沒有人記得。

台北一家小唱片行，從一箱箱舊黑膠唱片裡把它們找了出來。二〇二〇年，小廠牌 Aberrant Records 出了十二首歌的合輯《Taiwan Disco》。

鼓聲很重，貝斯很跳，歌聲又亮又自由。這張唱片已經補貨好幾次，讓世界聽見台灣曾經這麼會跳舞。

In the late 1970s, a group of Taiwanese women singers belted out songs over the rhythms of funk and disco, a style of dance music. Those songs were later buried under a wave of slow love ballads, and almost no one remembered them. A small record shop in Taipei dug them back up from box after box of old vinyl. In 2020, the small label Aberrant Records released Taiwan Disco, a compilation of twelve tracks. The drums hit hard, the bass grooves, and the voices are bright and free. The album has been reprinted several times, letting the world hear that Taiwan once knew how to dance.

芬蘭的「全民看薪水日」

來源：MetaFilter

每年十一月一日，芬蘭政府會公開每個人去年賺多少錢、繳多少稅。外國記者叫這天「嫉妒日」（嫉妒：看到別人比自己好，心裡不舒服）。

早上八點資料一出來，排隊的記者就搶著找出最有錢的人。二〇一五年，大家發現有些老闆搬到葡萄牙，退休金就不用繳稅，引起很多人生氣。

芬蘭人相信，政府的文件本來就該讓人民看。很多人說，這樣公開，能讓有錢人和窮人的差距不會太大。

Every year on November 1, the Finnish government publishes how much each person earned the previous year and how much tax they paid. Foreign reporters call the day "Envy Day." As soon as the data goes public at 8 a.m., reporters waiting in line rush to find the wealthiest people. In 2015, people discovered that some business owners had moved to Portugal, where their pensions were not taxed, and many people were angry. Finns believe that government documents should be open to the public in the first place. Many people say that this kind of openness keeps the gap between rich and poor from growing too wide.

秋天，跟老樹散步

來源：Openlands

十月二十四日星期六早上十點，Openlands 的志工會帶大家免費走一趟洛根廣場的 Palmer Square 公園，大約一個半小時。

這裡有二十八種老樹，是芝加哥第一個拿到「樹木園」認證的公園。志工會教你看葉子的形狀和樹皮的花紋，慢慢認出每一棵樹。

不用報名，也不用懂樹，直接到公園集合就好。記得帶手機或筆記本，把秋天的樹記下來。

On Saturday, October 24, at 10 a.m., volunteers from Openlands will lead a free walk of about an hour and a half through Palmer Square Park in Logan Square. The park is home to 28 kinds of mature trees, and it was the first park in Chicago to earn arboretum certification. The volunteers will show you how to read leaf shapes and bark patterns, helping you slowly learn to identify each tree. No registration is needed, and you don't need to know anything about trees. Just meet at the park. Bring your phone or a notebook to jot down the autumn trees you see.

一位物理學家的雪花博物館

來源：SnowCrystals.com

從一九九九年起，加州理工學院的物理教授 Kenneth Libbrecht 幾乎一個人經營 SnowCrystals.com，已經有超過兩千六百萬人來看過。

他的方法很簡單：在零下十五度左右的雪天，放一張黑色紙板接住雪花，趁它還沒化掉就拍下來。在實驗室裡，他還能讓冰晶（很小的冰粒）在玻璃上慢慢長成雪花。

網站也用好懂的物理告訴你：為什麼世界上找不到兩片一樣的雪花。

Since 1999, Kenneth Libbrecht, a physics professor at Caltech, has run SnowCrystals.com almost single-handedly, and more than 26 million people have visited it. His method is simple: on snowy days when the temperature is around -15°C, he sets out a sheet of black cardboard to catch snowflakes and photographs them before they melt. In his lab, he can also let ice crystals (tiny ice grains) grow slowly into snowflakes on glass. The site also uses accessible physics to explain why no two snowflakes in the world are alike.
