三個上班族的台語搖滾

來源：The News Lens

2005年，台南有三個上班族去看「春天吶喊」音樂祭。吶喊就是大聲叫。台上的樂團又吵又真，讓他們很感動，當場決定自己也要寫歌。他們叫「拍謝少年」，「拍謝」是台語的「不好意思」。

他們只用台語唱歌。2012年的第一張專輯《海口味》，吉他聲粗粗的，像在車庫裡練團。歌詞不寫愛情，寫的是工人和平凡人的日子。

他們已經三次拿下金音獎最佳搖滾專輯，最近一次是2024年。

In 2005, three office workers from Tainan went to the "Spring Scream" (春天吶喊) music festival. "Scream" simply means to shout out loud. The bands on stage were loud and raw, and the three were so moved that they decided on the spot to write their own songs. They named their band 拍謝少年 ("Sorry Youth"), and "拍謝" is the Taiwanese way of saying "sorry" or "excuse me." They sing only in Taiwanese Hokkien. Their 2012 debut album, 《海口味》, has rough, gritty guitar sounds, like a band rehearsing in a garage. The lyrics don't tell love stories. They tell the everyday lives of factory workers and ordinary people. The band has already won Best Rock Album at the Golden Indie Music Awards three times, most recently in 2024.

一朵不准畫的紅花

來源：Vogue Scandinavia

1964年，芬蘭布料公司Marimekko的老闆阿爾米·拉蒂亞定下規定：不准用花的圖案。設計師麥婭·伊索拉偷偷不聽，畫了一朵很大的紅黑色大花，叫作Unikko，在芬蘭話裡是一種紅色野花的名字。

這朵花要用四張網板（印布用的板子）才印得出來。六十年後，她的女兒和孫女還在畫新版本，Unikko也成了Marimekko最有名的圖案。

到了赫爾辛基，可以坐地鐵約20分鐘到Herttoniemi區。那裡有Marimekko的工廠商店，可以買到比較便宜的布、杯子和衣服。

In 1964, Armi Ratia, the owner of Finnish textile company Marimekko, set a firm rule: no flower patterns. Designer Maija Isola quietly ignored it and drew a very large red-and-black flower called Unikko, which is the Finnish name for a kind of red wildflower. Printing it took four screens (the frames used to print patterns onto fabric). Sixty years later, her daughter and granddaughter are still drawing new versions, and Unikko has become Marimekko's most famous pattern. In Helsinki, you can take the metro about 20 minutes to the Herttoniemi district, where Marimekko's factory outlet sells cheaper fabric, mugs and clothing.

酒吧裡的七分鐘文章大戰

來源：Choose Chicago

11月17日星期二晚上7點半，芝加哥的Write Club會在湖景區Clark街上的GMan Tavern辦一場比賽，主題叫「Why, I Autumn...!」。

比賽有三個回合。每回合，作家上台讀一篇自己寫的文章，最多只能七分鐘，題目都很抽象（看不見、摸不到）。讀完後，觀眾用拍手來投票。

贏家可以把錢捐給自己選的慈善（幫助別人）團體。這是芝加哥酒吧特有的「文學運動」，誰都可以走進去看。

On Tuesday, November 17, at 7:30 p.m., Chicago's Write Club will hold a contest at GMan Tavern on Clark Street in the Lakeview neighborhood. The theme is "Why, I Autumn...!" The contest has three rounds. In each round, writers take the stage and read an essay they wrote themselves, for no more than seven minutes. The topics are abstract, things you cannot see or touch. After each reading, the audience votes by applause. The winner may donate the prize money to a charity of their choice. It is a literary movement unique to Chicago's bars, and anyone can walk in and watch.

一個人記下全世界的教堂鐘

來源：Dove's Guide

1950年，英國敲鐘人羅納德·達夫自己出了一本小書，列出英國每一座可以用英式方法「轉圈敲鐘」的鐘塔。

他一直親手修改，出了八版。1994年，他把這份一輩子的心血交給一群志工，後來變成網站。

現在網站裡有超過14,000座塔、60,000個鐘。你可以用鐘的重量、做鐘的工廠，甚至每個鐘敲出的音來找。這些資料都是敲鐘人自己免費寫上去的。

In 1950, Ronald Dove, an English bell-ringer, published a small book of his own listing every tower in England where bells could be rung using the English method of "change ringing." He kept revising it by hand and brought out eight editions. In 1994, he handed this lifelong labor of love over to a group of volunteers, and it later grew into a website. The site now holds more than 14,000 towers and 60,000 bells. You can search by a bell's weight, by the foundry that cast it, or even by the note each bell sounds. All of this information was contributed for free by the bell-ringers themselves.
