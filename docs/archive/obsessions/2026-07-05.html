雷光夏的第一張專輯：把聲音當成一部電影

來源：FOND/SOUND

雷光夏是台灣有名的「音樂詩人」。這張唱片出來時，她還是一位電台的廣播主持人，爸爸是作家雷驤（她爸爸的名字）。

一九九五年，她把年輕時寫的歌重新錄音，只花三天就做完。

專輯裡有一組叫「時間」的作品，用像夢一樣的聲音和唸詩，把回憶當成電影，一次又一次重新拍。這在當年的流行音樂裡很少見。

Lei Guangxia is Taiwan's celebrated "music poet." When this album was released, she was still working as a radio broadcaster; her father was the writer Lei Xiang. In 1995, she re-recorded songs she had written in her youth, completing the work in just three days. The album features a suite titled "Time," which uses dreamlike vocals and recited poetry to transform memories into a film, replaying them over and over. This approach was rare in the pop music world of that era.

打鐵村變成芬蘭的設計之都

來源：Discovering Finland

芬蘭有一個小村子叫菲斯卡（Fiskars）。三百多年前，這裡是一座打鐵的工廠，做鐵鍋和工具。

到了一九八○年代，工廠關門了。村子沒有因此變空，反而請來很多設計師和做陶器的人搬進空房子。

現在大約有六百人在這裡住和工作。世界有名的橘色把手剪刀就是這裡發明的。從首都赫爾辛基開車來玩一天剛剛好。

Finland has a small village called Fiskars. Over three hundred years ago, it was an ironworks factory that produced cast-iron cookware and tools. By the 1980s, the factory had closed. Rather than becoming abandoned, the village instead invited designers and ceramicists to move into the vacant buildings. Today, around 600 people live and work here. The world-famous orange-handled scissors were invented right here. It's a perfect day trip from the capital, Helsinki.

失蹤的默片，配上現場音樂重新登場

來源：Chicago Film Society

一部一九二六年的老電影《你永遠不懂女人》要在芝加哥放映。這部片曾經失蹤很多年，直到二○○一年才在圖書館的倉庫裡被找到。

七月十二日星期日晚上七點，芝加哥電影協會會在Music Box戲院播這部片，用的正是那份找回來的老底片。

放映的時候，還有兩位音樂家在現場配樂，讓這部老電影更有味道。

A 1926 silent film titled "You'll Never Understand Women" is heading to Chicago for a special screening. The film had been lost for decades until a copy was discovered in a library storage room in 2001. On Sunday, July 12th at 7 PM, the Chicago Film Society will present it at the Music Box Theater, using the recovered original print. Two live musicians will accompany the screening, bringing new life to this classic film.

用簡單規則畫出大自然的孤獨藝術家

來源：inconvergent.net

有一位挪威（北歐國家）的數學家安德斯（Anders Hoff），十多年來一直在網站上分享他用電腦畫的圖。

他寫程式，用簡單的規則畫出像珊瑚、青苔和沙丘一樣的圖案，而且把程式公開，讓大家都能看。

他說他要找的，是介於整齊和混亂中間的那個位置——夠整齊才看得懂，夠混亂才有新意思。

There's a Norwegian mathematician named Anders Hoff who, for more than a decade, has been sharing on his website images generated by computer code he's written. Using simple rules, he creates patterns that resemble coral, moss, and sand dunes, and he shares the code publicly so anyone can access it. What he's looking for, he explains, is the space between perfect order and complete chaos—structured enough to make sense, yet chaotic enough to feel genuinely new.
