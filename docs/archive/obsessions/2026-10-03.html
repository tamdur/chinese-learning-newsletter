抓狂歌：一張用台語大聲說話的唱片

來源：Wikipedia / The News Lens / CommonWealth Magazine

1989年，台灣解嚴（不再實行軍事管制）才兩年，一群叫「黑名單工作室」的音樂人，推出了專輯《抓狂歌》。

這張唱片幾乎全用台語唱，把老民謠、客家和原住民的聲音，混進搖滾和電子合成器，還有台灣流行歌裡最早的一些饒舌（用說的方式唱歌）。

歌詞直接談政治打壓，也談經濟快速成長留下的問題。他們不肯改唱國語，也不肯說得小聲。這張專輯後來被看作「新台語歌運動」的起點。

In 1989, just two years after Taiwan lifted martial law—ending military control—a group of musicians called Blacklist Studio released the album Crazy Songs.

The record was sung almost entirely in Taiwanese, blending old folk melodies, Hakka and indigenous voices with rock and electronic synthesizers, and featuring some of Taiwan's earliest rap (singing in a spoken style).

The lyrics directly addressed political repression and the problems spawned by rapid economic growth. The band refused to switch to Mandarin and refused to keep their voices down. The album would later be recognized as the starting point of the "New Taiwanese Song Movement."

赫爾辛基地下十五公尺的游泳池

來源：MyHelsinki / Finland.fi

芬蘭首都赫爾辛基的岩石底下，藏著五千五百多個空間。平時是大家生活、運動的地方；遇到戰爭，就變成防空洞。

最有名的是東邊的 Itäkeskus 游泳館。它在1993年從硬石頭裡炸出來，有五十公尺長的泳池、滑水道、攝氏十四度的冷水池，還有五間三溫暖。

如果需要，池水一天內就能放乾，讓三千八百人在這裡躲避。三月外面還很冷，地底下卻是一座溫暖的城市。

Beneath the bedrock of Helsinki, Finland's capital, lies over 5,500 underground spaces. In peacetime, they serve as places where people live and exercise; in war, they transform into bomb shelters.

The most famous is the Itäkeskus swimming hall on the eastern side. It was blasted out of solid rock in 1993 and features a 50-meter swimming pool, water slides, a cold-water pool kept at 14 degrees Celsius, and five saunas.

If needed, the pool water can be drained within a day, allowing 3,800 people to take shelter here. In March, while it's still cold outside, underground there's a warm city.

走出畫框的人：三年一次的免費講座

來源：University of Chicago Center for the Art of East Asia / Art Institute of Chicago

10月18日星期日晚上六點，紐約大學的藝術史學者 Christopher Wood 將在芝加哥藝術博物館的老講堂 Fullerton Hall 開講，題目叫「越界」。

他要談1800年以前的歐洲畫家怎麼打破繪畫的規則：不照遠近的畫法，讓畫裡的人把手伸出畫框，或偷偷放進不屬於畫面的東西。

這個講座每三年才辦一次，之後在10月22日和29日還有兩場。不用買票，任何人都可以走進去聽。

On Sunday, October 18 at 6 p.m., Christopher Wood, an art history scholar from New York University, will give a lecture at Fullerton Hall in the Chicago Art Institute. The topic is "Crossing Boundaries."

He will discuss how European painters before 1800 broke the rules of painting—disregarding perspective conventions, allowing figures to reach their hands beyond the frame, or inserting elements that don't belong in the picture.

This lecture series occurs only once every three years. Two more sessions will follow on October 22 and 29. There is no admission fee, and anyone is welcome to attend.

往後數七個字：把文章變成怪詩的小網頁

來源：spoonbill.org

1960年代，法國作家 Jean Lescure 替文學團體 Oulipo 發明了一個文字遊戲，叫 N+7。

玩法很簡單：把文章裡每個名詞，換成字典裡往後數第七個名詞。平常的句子，一下子就變成奇怪又好笑的詩。

以前作家要翻紙本字典一個一個找。現在 spoonbill.org 有一個小網頁，貼上文字、選一個數字，馬上換好。沒有廣告，也不用註冊，一直安靜地運作著。

In the 1960s, French writer Jean Lescure invented a word game called N+7 for the literary group Oulipo.

The rules are simple: replace every noun in an article with the seventh noun that follows it in a dictionary. Ordinary sentences instantly become strange and funny poetry.

Writers used to flip through physical dictionaries and find each replacement manually. Now spoonbill.org hosts a small webpage where you paste text, select a number, and it transforms instantly. No ads, no registration required—it just quietly does its work.
