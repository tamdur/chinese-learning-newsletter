把悲傷關進玻璃球：I Mean Us 的科幻夢

來源：Bandwagon Asia

台北的樂團 I Mean Us 在二〇二一年做了一張很特別的專輯，叫《Into Innerverse》。他們的好朋友過世後，樂團想像了一個未來：人的感情變得很少見，只好放進博物館，封在一顆顆玻璃水晶球裡。

這張專輯把吉他模糊的聲音，和慢慢變強的樂句疊在一起，聽起來像在做夢。它讓樂團入圍金曲獎「最佳樂團」，單曲〈24 Years Old of You〉也拿下金音獎。

Taiwan's band I Mean Us created a distinctive album in 2021 called "Into Innerverse." After losing a close friend, the band imagined a future where human emotion had become so rare it had to be preserved in a museum—locked away in individual glass crystal spheres.

The album layers hazy guitar textures with gradually intensifying musical phrases, creating a dreamlike soundscape. The work earned the band a nomination for Best Band at the Golden Melody Awards, while the single "24 Years Old of You" won the Golden Indie Music Awards.

十五分鐘的藍：芬蘭冬天的秘密時刻

來源：Reissu Ruoti

芬蘭人給日落後那短短一段時間，取了一個名字，叫 sininen hetki，意思是「藍色時刻」。太陽剛落到地平線下面時，天空會變成很濃、很深的藍色。

這段魔法只有十五到三十分鐘，一天會出現兩次。在拉普蘭的冰湖、赫爾辛基的海邊市集廣場，都能看到。比起追極光，這是三月旅行時，更安靜的享受。

The Finns have named that brief moment after sunset sininen hetki—the "Blue Hour." When the sun slips below the horizon, the sky becomes intensely, richly blue.

This magical window lasts only fifteen to thirty minutes and appears twice daily. You can experience it on the frozen lakes of Lapland and in the waterfront squares of Helsinki. Compared to chasing the Northern Lights, it offers a quieter, more peaceful way to embrace winter travel in March.

不用錢的購物：巴克鎮的換衣派對

來源：Funk-it Swap

七月二十五日星期六，中午到下午四點，芝加哥的環保時尚團體 Funk-it，在巴克鎮的 Ray's 辦了一場「換衣社交會」。

大家帶來自己不再穿的衣服，直接和別的芝加哥人交換，換到「對你來說是新的」衣服。全程不用花錢，只用衣服換衣服。這是城市裡愈來愈流行的以物易物小市集。

On Saturday, July 25th, from noon to 4 p.m., Chicago's eco-conscious fashion group Funk-it hosts a clothing swap social at Ray's in Bucktown.

Attendees bring clothes they no longer wear and exchange them directly with other Chicagoans for pieces that are new to them. The whole thing costs nothing—it's pure clothing-for-clothing trade. It's the kind of barter market that's becoming increasingly popular in the city.

麵包袋夾子的博物學：一個人的分類王國

來源：Holotypic Occlupanid Research Group

從一九九四年開始，有一位很著迷的收藏家，替「麵包袋上的塑膠夾子」做了一套像真的科學分類。

他把這些小夾子分成十七個科、兩百零八種，還幫它們取了拉丁文的學名。他看的是每個夾子開口的「牙齒」形狀，用真正的生物學方法，去研究一個幾乎沒有人仔細看過的東西。

Since 1994, one devoted collector has created a rigorous scientific classification system for plastic bread bag clips.

He organized these small clips into seventeen families and two hundred and eight species, giving each a Latin scientific name. His system focuses on the "tooth" shape of each clip's opening, applying genuine biological taxonomy to study an everyday object that almost no one has ever examined carefully.
