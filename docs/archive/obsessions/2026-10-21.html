黑手那卡西：工人自己唱的歌

來源：Taipei Times

1996年，台灣很多工廠關門。聯福製衣和福昌紡織（做布）的工人丟了工作，走上台北街頭抗議。他們和音樂人一起，組成了「黑手那卡西」（一種走唱的小樂團）。

他們一起寫了〈福氣個屁〉，拿當時很紅的廣告詞開玩笑：工作都沒了，哪來的福氣？1997年，這首歌收進台灣第一張工人抗議專輯。錄音的錢很少，只能用最簡單的方法錄。

後來，他們教從沒學過音樂的工人，把自己的故事寫成歌。對他們來說，一起寫歌，就是團結的方法。

In 1996, many factories in Taiwan shut their doors. Workers at Lianfu Garment and Fuchang Textile, which made cloth, lost their jobs and took to the streets of Taipei to protest. Together with musicians, they formed the "Black Hand Nakasi" (a kind of street-singing folk band). Among their songs was "Fortune, My Foot" (福氣個屁), which poked fun at a popular advertising slogan of the time: with no work left, where was the good fortune? In 1997, the song was included on Taiwan's first workers' protest album. Recording money was scarce, so they had to make the record in the simplest way possible. Later, they taught workers who had never studied music to turn their own stories into songs. For them, writing songs together was a way of standing in solidarity.

沼澤之歌：在芬蘭泥地裡敷臉

來源：Arctic Lakeland

芬蘭東部的庫莫（Kuhmo），靠近俄羅斯邊界。這裡有一個導覽，帶人走進一片沼澤（有水的泥地）。客人穿上古代芬蘭人的衣服，沿著木板路走大約600公尺，導遊一路用魚骨頭做的樂器演奏。

到了沼澤，大家把黑色的泥炭（很老的植物泥土）抹在臉、手和腳上。芬蘭人說，他們很早就用泥炭來保養身體。最後，大家在一間百年木屋裡喝花草茶。

這個導覽只在五月到十月舉行，每人大約52歐元起。三月的沼澤還在雪下，可以先記下來，夏天再來。

In Kuhmo, in eastern Finland near the Russian border, there is a tour that leads visitors into a bog, a stretch of waterlogged mud. Guests put on clothing modeled on that of the ancient Finns and walk about 600 meters along a wooden boardwalk, while the guide plays instruments made from fish bones the whole way. At the bog, everyone smears black peat, a very old plant-based mud, on their faces, hands and feet. Finns say they have used peat to care for their bodies for a very long time. At the end, everyone drinks herbal tea in a century-old wooden cabin. The tour runs only from May to October and starts at about 52 euros per person. In March the bog is still under snow, so make a note of it and come back in the summer.

把西方的花放進淺盤：小原流插花課

來源：Japanese Culture Center (Chicago)

林肯公園的日本文化中心（2940 N. Lincoln Ave.）要開插花課。上課時間是11月1日和8日兩個星期日，早上10點到11點半，費用124美元，不需要經驗。

老師 Yuko Inoue-Darcy 從2005年起帶領小原流的芝加哥分會。小原流在1912年成立。當時很多西方的花傳進日本，舊的插法不好用，他們就想出「盛花」：不用高高的花瓶，而是把花堆在又寬又淺的盤子裡。

The Japan Cultural Center in Lincoln Park (2940 N. Lincoln Ave.) is offering a flower arranging class. The class meets on two Sundays, November 1 and November 8, from 10:00 to 11:30 a.m. The fee is $124, and no experience is needed. The teacher, Yuko Inoue-Darcy, has led the Chicago chapter of the Ohara School since 2005. The Ohara School was founded in 1912. At that time, many Western flowers were being brought into Japan, and the old arranging methods did not suit them. So the school came up with moribana, or "piled flowers," which uses not tall vases but wide, shallow dishes into which the flowers are heaped.

快要消失的文字地圖

來源：Endangered Alphabets Project

美國佛蒙特州的作家 Tim Brookes，本來只是把快要沒人用的文字刻在碎木頭上，當作興趣。後來，這變成了「瀕危（快要消失）文字計畫」。

計畫的網站上有一張世界地圖，標出將近90種快要消失的文字。點一下，就能看到它的照片和故事。像非洲喀麥隆的巴蒙文，曾經被法國殖民（佔領別國來管）政府禁止。現在，發明者的後代正在讓它重新活過來。

在這裡，每一種文字都像一種需要保護的動物。

Tim Brookes, a writer from Vermont, started out simply carving nearly extinct writing systems into scraps of wood as a hobby. Over time, that turned into the Endangered Alphabets Project. The project's website has a world map marking close to 90 endangered scripts. Click on any one and you can see a photo of it and its story. Take Bamum, a script from Cameroon in Africa, which was banned by the French colonial government when it ruled the country. Today, the inventor's descendants are bringing it back to life. Here, every writing system is treated like an endangered animal that needs protecting.
