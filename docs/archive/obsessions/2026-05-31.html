解嚴之後的狂歌：黑名單工作室《抓狂歌》

來源：Takes on 200 (200albums.wordpress.com)

一九八九年，台灣解嚴（取消軍事管制）才兩年，黑名單工作室發行了《抓狂歌》。這張專輯把搖滾、饒舌和台灣民謠混在一起，歌詞用了閩南語、中文、英文和日文四種語言。

核心人物陳明章曾為侯孝賢電影《戀戀風塵》寫配樂。這張唱片被選入台灣百佳專輯第十名，也開啟了「新台語歌」運動的大門。

In 1989, just two years after the lifting of martial law in Taiwan, Blacklist Studio released Songs of Madness. The album blended rock, rap, and Taiwanese folk music, with lyrics in four languages: Hokkien, Mandarin, English, and Japanese. Key member Chen Ming-chang had previously composed the soundtrack for Hou Hsiao-hsien's film Dust in the Wind. The album was ranked 10th on Taiwan's 100 Best Albums list and opened the door for the New Taiwanese Song movement.

冰湖上的木造音樂殿堂：拉赫蒂西貝流士音樂廳

來源：Sibeliustalo (official site)

從赫爾辛基坐一小時火車，就到了拉赫蒂市。這裡有一座西貝流士音樂廳，在兩千年開幕時，它是芬蘭一百多年來最大的公共木造建築。九根巨大松木柱撐起木頂，讓這個一千兩百席的廳擁有北歐最好的音響效果。

三月時，透過玻璃大廳可以直接看到結冰的湖面。一個下午，就能同時享受建築、音樂和冰湖風景。

A one-hour train ride from Helsinki brings you to Lahti. Here stands the Sibelius Hall, which at its opening in 2000 was Finland's largest public wooden structure in over a century. Nine massive pine columns support the wooden roof, giving this 1,200-seat hall the finest acoustics in Northern Europe. In March, through the glass lobby you can look directly out onto the frozen lake. In a single afternoon, you can experience architecture, music, and an icy landscape all at once.

用身體說故事：芝加哥肢體劇場節

來源：Physical Theater Festival Chicago

芝加哥第十四屆肢體劇場節將在六月一日到七日舉行。六月四日起有四場早晨工作坊，不需要任何經驗，任何人都可以參加。課程包括雙人特技和面具表演。

晚間的演出也很特別：來自北韓的白素拉將用肢體動作講述她逃離北韓的故事，完全不用語言。

Chicago's 14th annual Physical Theatre Festival runs June 1-7. Starting June 4, four morning workshops are open to everyone — no experience needed. Classes cover partner acrobatics and mask performance. Evening shows are equally compelling: Sora Baek from North Korea will tell her story of escape using only movement and gesture, without a single word spoken.

收聽冷戰的幽靈電波：數字電台研究站

來源：numbers-stations.com

冷戰時期，各國情報機關透過短波廣播，把一串串數字念給海外的間諜（秘密情報人員）聽。歷史學家 Māris Goldmanis 建立了一個網站，記錄了幾十個這樣的「數字電台」。

最神秘的是俄羅斯的 UVB-76，人稱「蜂鳴器」，從一九七零年代至今一直在發出嗡嗡聲，偶爾夾雜人聲。沒有人確定它的用途。

During the Cold War, intelligence agencies around the world used shortwave radio broadcasts to transmit coded number sequences to spies stationed overseas. Historian Māris Goldmanis has created a website documenting dozens of these "number stations." The most mysterious is Russia's UVB-76, nicknamed "the Buzzer," which has been emitting a continuous buzzing sound since the 1970s, occasionally interspersed with human voices. No one is certain of its purpose.
