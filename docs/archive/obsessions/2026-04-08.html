《讓我想一想》——陳綺貞的首張專輯，一把吉他寫下台灣民謠新聲音

來源：魔岩唱片 (1998)

一九九八年，剛從政治大學畢業的陳綺貞，在一家小唱片公司「魔岩唱片」發行了她的第一張專輯《讓我想一想》。她自己寫歌、自己彈吉他，聲音乾淨、有點害羞，一點都不像當時流行的偶像歌手。

這張專輯後來被很多人當作台灣獨立民謠的起點之一。陳綺貞日後被稱作「文青女神」，但《讓我想一想》其實更直接：只有一把吉他、一本筆記本、一顆想好好把一件事講清楚的心。二十多年過去，許多新一代的台灣創作者仍然說，他們就是在這張專輯裡第一次聽見「原來歌可以這樣寫」。

Let Me Think About It — Cheer Chen's debut album, one guitar and a new voice in Taiwanese folk

In 1998, Cheer Chen (陳綺貞), fresh out of National Chengchi University, released her first album, Let Me Think About It (讓我想一想), on the small indie label Magic Stone. She wrote her own songs, played her own guitar, and sang with a clean, slightly shy voice — nothing like the polished pop idols dominating the charts at the time.

The album has since come to be seen as one of the starting points of Taiwanese indie folk. Cheer Chen would later be dubbed the 'indie-cool goddess,' but Let Me Think About It is simpler than that label suggests: just a guitar, a notebook, and a voice trying to say one thing properly. More than twenty years on, a new generation of Taiwanese songwriters still say this was the album where they first heard 'oh — you can write songs like that.'

芬蘭的「麵包之根」：兩千年的黑麥酵母，代代相傳

來源：thisisFINLAND / Yle

芬蘭的國民食物不是鮭魚，也不是馴鹿肉，而是黑麥麵包 (ruisleipä)。二〇一七年，為了慶祝芬蘭獨立一百年，芬蘭人投票選出這個又黑又酸的圓麵包，當作國家的代表食物。

最特別的是它的酵母。每一戶人家都有一塊「麵包的根」(leivän juuri)，一代一代傳下來，有些可以追溯到曾祖母的時代。黑麥在芬蘭已經種了兩千多年，連國家史詩《卡勒瓦拉》裡都有它的身影。旅行到芬蘭，若早餐桌上有一塊又硬又香的黑麵包，咬下去的其實是一整段家族的歷史。

Finland's 'Root of the Bread': Two Thousand Years of Rye Sourdough, Passed Down the Generations

Finland's national food is not salmon, nor reindeer — it is dark rye bread (ruisleipä). In 2017, to mark the centenary of Finnish independence, Finns voted this dark, sour, disc-shaped loaf as the food that best represents the country.

The most remarkable thing about it is the sourdough starter. Every household keeps a 'root of the bread' (leivän juuri), handed down through the generations — some starters can be traced back to a great-grandmother's kitchen. Rye has been cultivated in Finland for more than two thousand years and even appears in the national epic, the Kalevala. Travel to Finland, and the hard, fragrant dark slice on your breakfast plate is, in effect, a bite of somebody's family history.

四月十二日：在芝加哥聽一場「故事幸運秀」

來源：Story Luck Show / Chicago Reader

這個星期日晚上，芝加哥有一場很特別的聚會叫做 Story Luck Show (故事幸運秀)。參加的人要帶兩樣東西：一道自己做的菜，還有一個親身經歷的真實故事，兩樣都要和大家分享。沒有劇本，也沒有專業演員，只有普通人輪流坐下來講自己的事。

這種「故事聚餐」在芝加哥其實有不少，大多在小酒吧或公寓客廳裡低調舉行，外地人很難發現。想參加的人只要先報名就可以，說得不好也沒關係——有時候最讓人記得的故事，反而是那些口氣最平淡的。

April 12: Catch a 'Story Luck Show' in Chicago

This Sunday evening, Chicago hosts a quietly delightful gathering called the Story Luck Show. Guests are asked to bring two things: a dish they cooked themselves and a true story from their own life — both to be shared with the group. There is no script and no professional performers, just ordinary people taking turns sitting down and telling their own stories.

These 'story potlucks' are more common in Chicago than you'd think, but most are held quietly in small bars or apartment living rooms, so outsiders rarely stumble across them. Attending is easy: sign up in advance, and don't worry if you're not a gifted speaker — often the most unforgettable stories are the ones told in the flattest voice.

The Public Domain Review：一座只有一位客人的線上博物館

來源：publicdomainreview.org

The Public Domain Review (公共領域評論) 是一個很安靜的網站。它只做一件事：從世界各大圖書館的「公共領域」(版權已經過期的舊資料) 裡，挑出奇怪又美麗的東西——十七世紀的怪物圖鑑、十九世紀的催眠術手冊、早期電影的片段——再寫一篇短短的導讀，告訴你該怎麼看、為什麼重要。

主編 Adam Green 和一個小團隊從二〇一一年開始做這件事，一直到現在。他們不放廣告，靠讀者捐款和出版書籍活下來。每一篇文章都像一場迷你策展：圖像、背景、作者故事，全都細心地擺好。花半小時在這個網站，就像逛了一間當天只有你一個客人的博物館。

The Public Domain Review: An Online Museum Where You Are the Only Visitor

The Public Domain Review is a very quiet website. It does only one thing: it digs through the public-domain collections of the world's great libraries (materials whose copyright has expired) and surfaces the strange and beautiful — a seventeenth-century bestiary, a nineteenth-century mesmerism manual, a fragment of early cinema — then writes a short essay telling you how to look at it and why it matters.

Editor Adam Green and a small team have been at it since 2011. They run no advertising, living instead on reader donations and the books they publish. Each piece is a miniature curation: images, background, the author's story — everything carefully laid out. Spend half an hour there, and it feels like visiting a museum where, for today at least, you are the only guest in the building.
