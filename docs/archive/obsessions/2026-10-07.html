夢裡的東邊：電子琴唱出的神怪台灣

來源：Beats Per Minute

「夢東」是台北的一對兄弟樂團。他們在國外讀書，2017年回到台灣後，開始把小時候看過的神怪電視節目和廟口的奇怪圖片，做成音樂。

2020年，他們在家裡錄好專輯《秘神》，由日本唱片公司發行。八首歌用便宜的電子琴、老電玩的聲音和重金屬的低沉節奏，拼出一場又怪又好笑的夢。

這裡沒有廟裡的樂器，而是電子合成器在說台灣民間的鬼神故事。

"Dream East" is a two-brother band from Taipei. They studied abroad and, after returning to Taiwan in 2017, began turning childhood memories of supernatural TV shows and strange images from temple entrances into music. In 2020, they recorded their album "Secret Gods" at home, released by a Japanese record label. Eight songs built from cheap electronic keyboards, retro video game sounds, and heavy metal's deep bass lines piece together a strange and hilarious dream. Here, there are no temple instruments—instead, electronic synthesizers tell Taiwan's folk ghost stories.

赫爾辛基的「難吃」糖果亭

來源：ThisisFINLAND

在芬蘭首都赫爾辛基，有一間小小的亭子，只賣一種糖：鹹甘草糖。老闆科霍寧準備了91種口味。

這種糖又鹹又苦，因為裡面加了氯化銨（一種以前用在咳嗽藥水裡的鹽）。很多外國人第一次吃，都覺得很難吃。

它來自1930年代北歐的藥局：為了讓小孩願意吃咳嗽藥，藥師把藥和甘草混在一起。後來，這種藥變成了芬蘭人最愛的零食。

In Helsinki, Finland's capital, there's a tiny kiosk that sells just one type of candy: salty licorice. The owner, Kohonen, has created 91 different flavors. The candy tastes both salty and bitter because it contains ammonium chloride—a salt that once appeared in cough syrups. Many foreigners find it disgusting on their first try. It originated in Nordic pharmacies during the 1930s, when pharmacists mixed medicine with licorice to make cough medicine more palatable for children. Eventually, what started as medicine became Finland's favorite snack.

阿蓋爾街上的燈籠與傳說

來源：TheCraftMap

10月25日星期天，芝加哥上城區的阿蓋爾街（Argyle Street）會有一天的市集，名字叫「燈籠與傳說」。

這條街被叫做「亞洲在阿蓋爾」，多年來都是越南和其他亞洲小吃店、超市聚在一起的地方。這次市集的主題是亞洲的民間故事和神話，攤販（擺攤賣東西的人）賣的作品都和這些古老傳說有關。

地址是西阿蓋爾街1132號，免費入場，走過去就能逛。

On Sunday, October 25th, Argyle Street in Chicago's Uptown will host an all-day market called 'Lanterns and Legends.' Known as 'Asia on Argyle,' the street has long been a hub for Vietnamese and other Asian restaurants and shops. This year's market theme celebrates Asian folklore and mythology, with vendors offering works inspired by these ancient legends. Located at 1132 West Argyle Street, the event is free to attend—just come by and browse.

一個人的紅蘿蔔博物館

來源：World Carrot Museum

1995年左右，英國退休的政府法律顧問約翰・斯托拉齊克，在網路上開了「世界紅蘿蔔博物館」。他說，這是世界上第一間只研究紅蘿蔔的虛擬（只在網路上）博物館。

網站有好幾百頁，從古代波斯的紫色紅蘿蔔，到紅蘿蔔藝術和基因，什麼都有。

很多人相信，荷蘭農夫為了紀念奧蘭治王室，才把紅蘿蔔種成橘色。他卻找不到任何證據，認為農夫其實是想讓它更甜。

Around 1995, retired British government legal adviser John Stolarczyk launched the World Carrot Museum online. He claimed it was the world's first virtual museum dedicated entirely to researching carrots. The website contains several hundred pages covering everything from ancient Persian purple carrots to carrot art and genetics. Many people believe Dutch farmers bred carrots to be orange in honor of the House of Orange, but Stolarczyk found no evidence to support this. He suspects farmers actually just wanted to make them sweeter.
