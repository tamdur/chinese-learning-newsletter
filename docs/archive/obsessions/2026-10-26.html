被遺忘的第一聲：閃靈《祖靈之流》

來源：Wikipedia

一九九八年，台灣樂團閃靈在台北錄下第一張專輯《祖靈之流》，隔年發行。它只在台灣賣，很快就買不到了。

它的聲音很像挪威的黑金屬（一種又快又吵、很黑暗的搖滾），鼓聲像下大雨。但主唱林昶佐在裡面拉起二胡，古老的弦聲和重金屬第一次混在一起。

唱片裡有三首連在一起的歌，講人們冒險越過台灣海峽。這個故事，後來成了閃靈一輩子的主題。

In 1998, the Taiwanese band Chthonic (閃靈) recorded its first album, Ancestral Spirits Flow (祖靈之流) in Taipei, and it was released the following year. It was sold only in Taiwan and quickly became impossible to find. Its sound is close to Norwegian black metal, a fast, loud and very dark style of rock, and its drumming hits like a heavy downpour. But vocalist Freddy Lim (林昶佐) plays the erhu, a traditional two-stringed Chinese instrument, and for the first time its ancient bowed melodies collide with heavy metal. The record contains three songs that run together, telling of people who risked crossing the Taiwan Strait. That story became a lifelong theme for Chthonic.

用魚骨做的琴：芬蘭的康特勒琴

來源：Wikipedia / Visit Finland

在芬蘭史詩《卡勒瓦拉》裡，老英雄萬奈莫寧用一條大魚的下巴骨，做出第一把康特勒琴（kantele），再用少女的頭髮當琴弦。

真正的康特勒琴比故事更老，可能有一千到三千年。最早只有五、六條馬毛做的弦，平放在腿上用手指撥。今天北卡累利阿的工匠做的大琴，最多有三十九條弦。

想聽現場演奏，可以去伊洛曼齊的木屋村。只是那裡大多在六月到九月才有表演，三月去的話，就當作下次再來的理由吧。

In Finland's epic poem Kalevala, the old hero Väinämöinen makes the first kantele from the jawbone of a large fish, then uses a maiden's hair for the strings. The real kantele is older than the story, perhaps one to three thousand years old. The earliest ones had only five or six strings made of horsehair, laid flat across the lap and plucked with the fingers. Today, craftsmen in North Karelia build large kanteles with as many as thirty-nine strings. To hear one played live, visit the log-cabin village in Ilomantsi. But most performances there happen between June and September, so if you go in March, consider it a reason to come back.

一隻雞，一個晚上：林肯廣場的切雞課

來源：CourseHorse / The Chopping Block

十一月九日星期一晚上六點，林肯廣場的 The Chopping Block 料理教室，要教大家把一整隻雞切成一塊一塊。不會用刀也沒關係。

學生三到五人一組，切好以後，馬上用自己切的雞做兩道菜：摩洛哥塔吉鍋雞，和義大利馬沙拉酒雞。老師也會教你怎麼看雞肉熟了沒。

課程三小時，費用一百五十美元，滿十八歲就能報名。名額不多，想學要早點報。

On Monday evening, November 9, at 6:00 p.m., The Chopping Block cooking school in Lincoln Square will teach a class on breaking down a whole chicken into pieces. You don't need any knife skills. Students work in groups of three to five people. Once the chicken is cut, each group uses its own pieces to make two dishes right away: Moroccan chicken tagine and Marsala wine chicken, the Italian dish. The instructor will also show you how to tell whether the chicken is fully cooked.

The class runs for three hours and costs $150. You must be 18 or older to sign up. Spots are limited, so if you want to take it, register early.

兩萬年前的骨頭，有了自己的編號

來源：OEIS

一九六四年，康乃爾大學的學生斯隆，開始把有趣的數列寫在小卡片上。數列就是照規則排好的一串數字，像一、二、四、八。

這個小興趣越長越大，變成網路上的「整數數列線上大全」（OEIS），今天收了三十九萬多條數列。

二〇〇四年，第十萬號要給誰？斯隆選了「伊尚戈骨」上的刻痕。這根骨頭來自剛果，有兩萬多年歷史，是人類最早的數學文物。人類第一次數數，終於也有了號碼。

In 1964, Neil Sloane, a student at Cornell University, began writing interesting number sequences on small index cards. A number sequence is a string of numbers arranged by a rule, such as 1, 2, 4, 8. The small hobby grew and grew until it became the online "On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences" (OEIS), which today holds more than 390,000 sequences. In 2004, which sequence would receive the 100,000th number? Sloane chose the notches carved into the Ishango bone. The bone comes from the Congo and is more than 20,000 years old, making it one of the earliest mathematical artifacts of humanity. The very first human counting has finally been given a number of its own.
