廟裡的電子音：夢東的《台灣謎景》

來源：WV Sorcerer Productions (Bandcamp)

二零二一年，台北的兩兄弟用「夢東」這個名字，做了一張很特別的專輯《台灣謎景》。他們花六個月走遍台灣奇怪的廟，拍下十八層地獄的塑像，還有保存好幾百年的和尚身體。

專輯就是這些照片的聲音。老電子琴的音，加上錄音帶的沙沙聲，再放進廟裡的念經聲，一圈一圈繞著轉，像在做夢。二零二一年四月二十八日，Bandcamp 選它當「每日專輯」。

In 2021, two brothers from Taipei, recording as Mong Tong, made a remarkable album called Music from Taiwan Mystery. They spent six months traveling to Taiwan's stranger temples, photographing statues of the eighteen levels of hell and the centuries-old preserved bodies of monks.

The album is the sound of those photographs. Vintage synthesizer tones mix with the hiss of cassette tape, layered with temple chanting that loops around and around, like a dream.

On April 28, 2021, Bandcamp named it their Album of the Day.

樺木做的杯子：拉普蘭不能自己買的禮物

來源：Finland Naturally

在芬蘭北方的拉普蘭，薩米人（當地的原住民）會用一整塊樺木瘤，手工挖出一個小木杯，叫做 kuksa。樹瘤是樹受傷後長出來的一團木頭，裡面的紋路互相交錯，所以不容易裂開。

薩米人有個規矩：kuksa 不可以自己買，要等別人送。送杯子的人，是在祝你一路平安。用咖啡渣或奶油保養，不能用滾水燙，一個木杯可以用一輩子，還能傳給下一代。

In Lapland, in northern Finland, the Sámi people (the region's indigenous inhabitants) carve a small wooden cup called a kuksa by hand from a single birch burl. A burl is the lump of wood a tree grows after an injury; its grain runs in all directions, which makes it hard to crack.

The Sámi have a rule: you do not buy a kuksa for yourself — you wait for someone to give you one. The person giving it is wishing you a safe journey. Cared for with coffee grounds or butter, never scalded with boiling water, a single cup can last a lifetime and be passed down to the next generation.

地下九十公尺：芝加哥的第二套下水道

來源：Metropolitan Water Reclamation District of Greater Chicago

芝加哥地底下藏著一條「深隧道」，長六十五公里，最深的地方在地下九十公尺。下大雨的時候，它會先把髒水吞進去，不讓髒水流到密西根湖。

每個星期四早上十點，水務局在 Hodgkins 的抽水站開放免費參觀，兩個小時。只要帶身分證、簽一張同意書就能下去。名額要先預約，想去的人現在就得報名。

Hidden beneath Chicago is a "Deep Tunnel" 65 kilometers long, reaching 90 meters underground at its deepest. When heavy rain falls, it swallows the dirty water first, keeping it from flowing into Lake Michigan.

Every Thursday at 10 a.m., the water reclamation district opens its pumping station in Hodgkins for a free two-hour tour. All you need is a photo ID and a signed waiver to go down. Spots must be booked in advance, so anyone who wants to go should register now.

用年輪畫出來的移民史

來源：pmcruz.com

設計師 Pedro Cruz 和同事做了一個網頁，把美國從一七九零年到二零一六年的移民故事，畫成一圈一圈的年輪。一圈是十年，一個小點代表一百個人。

電腦會把每個點放在他們家鄉的方向，所以歐洲人多的年代，年輪往東邊長胖；拉丁美洲人多的年代就往南邊長。這個作品放在他自己的網站上，二零一八年拿下資料設計大獎的第一名。

Designer Pedro Cruz and a colleague built a webpage that draws the story of immigration to the United States from 1790 to 2016 as a set of concentric tree rings. One ring is a decade; one small dash stands for a hundred people.

The computer places each dash in the direction of the immigrants' homeland, so in decades of heavy European arrival the rings thicken toward the east, and in decades of Latin American arrival they grow toward the south. The piece lives on his own website, and in 2018 it took first prize at the Information is Beautiful Awards.
