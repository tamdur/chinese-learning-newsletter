不准想媽媽的一首歌

來源：Wikipedia / Taipei Times

1959年，台南歌手文夏拿一首日本歌的曲子，配上台語歌詞，寫成「媽媽請你也保重」。那時候，很多台語歌都是這樣「混血」出來的。

歌裡唱的是離家的孩子想念媽媽。可是政府說，當兵的人不可以想媽媽，會影響軍心，就把這首歌禁了。

這一禁就是快30年，直到1987年戒嚴（軍隊管理社會的時期）結束才解開。文夏一生錄了一千多首歌，有99首被禁，人稱「禁歌之王」。

In 1959, Tainan singer Wen Hsia took the melody of a Japanese song, set Taiwanese lyrics to it, and called it "Mama, Please Take Care Too." Back then, many Taiwanese-language songs were born this kind of "mixed-blood" way. The song sings of a child who has left home and misses his mother. But the government said that soldiers were not allowed to think of their mothers, as it would undermine troop morale, so the song was banned. The ban lasted nearly 30 years, until martial law (the period when the military governed society) ended in 1987 and the song was finally cleared. Over his lifetime Wen Hsia recorded more than a thousand songs, and 99 of them were banned, earning him the nickname "King of Banned Songs."

冰洞裡的幾秒鐘

來源：Yle (Finnish Broadcasting Company)

芬蘭人冬天愛在結冰的湖上挖一個洞，跳下去泡一下。很多人說這樣對身體好。

不過，中國有一份研究追蹤了大約900位冬泳的人30年，發現他們因心臟病和中風死去的比例，比一般人高了大約一成。

芬蘭學者林塔馬基說，冷水確實會讓心臟很累。但芬蘭人通常只泡幾秒鐘，時間長短差很多。想在拉普蘭試試的話，記得：快進快出。

Finns love cutting a hole in the ice on frozen lakes in winter and jumping in for a quick dip. Many people say it's good for the body. But a Chinese study that tracked roughly 900 winter swimmers over 30 years found that they died from heart disease and stroke at a rate about 10 percent higher than the general population. Finnish researcher Lintamäki says cold water really does make the heart work hard. But Finns usually stay in for only a few seconds, and the difference in how long you stay in matters a lot. If you want to try it in Lapland, remember: get in fast, get out fast.

警察局裡的無線電老師

來源：ARRL Club Directory / Chicago FM Club

玩業餘無線電的人，英文叫ham，中文叫「火腿族」。帶新手入門的老手，大家叫他們Elmer。

芝加哥FM俱樂部每月第四個星期三開會，地點在Milwaukee大道5151號的第16區警察局。免費，誰都能去，下一次是10月28日。

俱樂部也在這裡辦執照考試，11月14日和12月12日各有一場。一個下午，你就可能帶著自己的呼號（電台的名字）走出來。

People who play amateur radio are called "hams" in English, and "火腿族" (huǒtuǐ zú, the "ham tribe") in Chinese. Experienced operators who mentor newcomers are known as "Elmers." The Chicago FM Club meets on the fourth Wednesday of every month at the 16th District police station at 5151 Milwaukee Avenue. Admission is free and anyone can attend; the next meeting is on October 28. The club also holds licensing exams at the same location, with sessions on November 14 and December 12. In a single afternoon, you could walk out with your own call sign (your station's name).

公車司機的七千種植物

來源：Plants For A Future (PFAF) Database

1990年代初，倫敦公車司機Ken Fern搬到英國西南角的鄉下，在一大片地上種起各種植物。

他一種一種記下來：哪些能吃、能治病、能做繩子、油或肥皂。光是手寫的紙，就有大約4000張。

後來這些紀錄變成網站Plants For A Future，收了將近八千種植物，每種都有1到5分的「有用分數」。網頁上還留著1997年的字樣，像一個時光膠囊。

In the early 1990s, Ken Fern, a London bus driver, moved to the countryside in the southwest corner of England and planted a large plot of land with all kinds of plants. He recorded them one by one: which ones were edible, which could treat illnesses, and which could be made into rope, oil, or soap. His handwritten notes alone filled roughly 4,000 sheets of paper. Those records later became the website Plants For A Future, which catalogs nearly 8,000 plants, each with a "usefulness score" from 1 to 5. The webpage still carries the year 1997, like a time capsule.
