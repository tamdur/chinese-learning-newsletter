一首歌，橫跨兩片大洋

來源：Small Island Big Song

台灣的陳玫君和澳洲丈夫提姆，花了三年走訪十六個南島語族（太平洋島嶼上同源的民族）國家，錄下一百多位音樂家的歌聲。他們不用錄音室，就在海邊和樹下錄音。

台灣被認為是南島語言的老家。專輯《小島大歌》把布農族的合唱，和馬達加斯加的竹琴，放進同一首歌裡。二○一九年，它得到英國世界音樂雜誌的亞太最佳專輯獎。

BaoBao Chen of Taiwan and her Australian husband Tim spent three years travelling through sixteen Austronesian-speaking nations, recording the voices of more than a hundred musicians. They used no studio — they recorded by the sea and under trees. Taiwan is considered the ancestral homeland of the Austronesian languages. Their album Small Island Big Song weaves the choral singing of Taiwan's Bunun people together with the Malagasy valiha, placing them inside a single song. In 2019 it won the Songlines Music Award for Best Asia/Pacific Album.

每年重蓋一次的雪城堡

來源：Wikipedia / The Icebreaking Company

芬蘭北部的凱米市，從一九九六年開始，每年一月都用雪蓋一座新城堡。塔最高超過二十公尺，牆最長超過一千公尺，蓋一次要五個多星期。

城堡裡有一間全部用雪和冰做成的小教堂，有人從日本、香港特地飛來這裡結婚。春天一到，整座城堡就融化，流回海裡。明年三月，新的城堡還會站在那裡。

Since 1996, the northern Finnish city of Kemi has built a new snow castle every January. The tallest towers rise over 20 metres, the longest walls run more than 1,000 metres, and each build takes over five weeks. Inside stands a small chapel made entirely of snow and ice, and people have flown in from Japan and Hong Kong specifically to be married there. When spring comes the whole castle melts and flows back into the sea. Next March, the new castle will still be standing.

星期三晚上，和天文學家聊天

來源：Adler Planetarium

八月十九日星期三，芝加哥的艾德勒天文館開到晚上十點，伊利諾州的居民免費入場。

館裡的「太空視覺實驗室」不是劇場，而是真的研究室。天文學家就站在巨大的太空影像前面，和走進來的人聊最新的天文研究。想問什麼都可以，不用預約，也不用懂科學。

On Wednesday, August 19th, the Adler Planetarium in Chicago stays open until 10 p.m., with free admission for Illinois residents. Inside, the "Space Visualization Laboratory" isn't a theater — it's a real research room. Astronomers stand in front of enormous images of space and talk with whoever walks in about the latest astronomical research. You can ask anything you like; no reservation required, and no science background needed.

一個人蓋起來的日晷博物館

來源：mySundial.ca

加拿大人卡爾・薩班斯基自己讀書、自己畫圖，做出一個專講日晷（用太陽影子看時間的鐘）的網站。

水平的、直立的、傾斜的日晷，他都寫下做法和算法。網站上還有給小朋友的紙日晷，和世界各地的人寄來的作品照片。沒有廣告，也沒人付他錢，只因為他喜歡。

Canadian Carl Sabanski taught himself the subject and drew his own diagrams to build a website devoted entirely to sundials (clocks that tell time by the sun's shadow). For horizontal, vertical and inclined dials alike, he has written down both the construction method and the mathematics. The site also has paper sundials for children and photographs of finished dials sent in by people all over the world. There are no ads and nobody pays him — he does it simply because he loves it.
